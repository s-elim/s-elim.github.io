The JEPA family, from I-JEPA to H-JEPA

Every model on this page makes the same move: encode what is visible, predict the encoding of what is hidden or still to come, and score the prediction in a space the encoder learned. What changes from one model to the next is five design choices: what is hidden (image blocks, video tubes, the next frame, a state several steps ahead), what the prediction is conditioned on (token positions, actions, pooled macro-actions), the distance (L2 or L1), how collapse is prevented (a slow teacher network, or a regulariser on the batch, or an action-regression head), and what the result is used for (frozen features, or a simulator a planner searches). Which "H-JEPA"?

Three different things carry the name, and citing the wrong one is easy. This page means the first item of the third group whenever it writes H-JEPA without qualification.

1. The proposal (2022 to 2023)

LeCun's position paper, A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence (OpenReview, v0.9.2, 2022), proposes stacking JEPAs: a lower JEPA makes short-term predictions from detailed representations, a higher one makes longer-term predictions from more abstract ones, and planning runs across the levels. Dawid and LeCun's lecture notes (arXiv 2306.02572, §6.2) restate it. No training recipe, no experiments. Context

2. Same name, different subject

Girgis, Labriji and Bennis, Hierarchical JEPA Meets Predictive Remote Control in Beyond 5G Networks (arXiv 2602.07000, Jan 2026): an H-JEPA for compressing device states in wireless networked control. Unrelated GitHub repositories also use the name for multi-scale image SSL (jonwiggins/H-JEPA), a language model (Griffith-7/H-JEPA-LM), biosignals (lucapegolotti/h-jepa) and recommendation (csanadlajko/H-JEPA4Rec). None of these is the October 2026 method. Context, checked 12 Oct 2026

3. The method this page covers, and its nearest predecessor

H-JEPA: End-to-End Learning of Hierarchical World Models for Visual Planning. Wancong Zhang and Basile Terver (equal contribution), Michael Rabbat, Yann LeCun and Randall Balestriero (equal advising), arXiv 2610.06805, submitted 5 Oct 2026; code at github.com/kevinghst/H-JEPA (MIT), built on LeWM and stable-worldmodel. It is the first instance of the 2022 proposal trained end to end with planning results, and it differs from HWM (Zhang, Terver et al., Apr 2026) on exactly one axis: HWM's levels predict in one shared latent space, H-JEPA gives each level its own encoder and space.
