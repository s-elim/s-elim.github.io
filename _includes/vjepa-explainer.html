A JEPA puts no density over anything. It learns an encoder <span class="mono">f</span> and a predictor <span class="mono">g</span> so that <span class="mono">g(f(x_ctx), ξ) ≈ f(x_tgt)</span>, and is charged only for the part of the target that survives <span class="mono">f</span>. (2024) prove the matching statement for deep linear networks: a latent objective prioritises features with large regression coefficients, a reconstruction objective prioritises high-variance features. <span class="ev ev--wl">E</span> for the linear case, <span class="ev ev--inf">I</span> for the transfer to ViTs. </p> </div> <div class="panel"> <div class="panel__head">Two families, side by side</div> <div class="panel__body tablewrap"> <table> <thead><tr><th scope="col">Axis</th><th scope="col">Generative (pixel or VAE latent)</th><th scope="col">JEPA</th></tr></thead> <tbody> <tr><td>Target</td><td>x, or a frozen autoencoder's latent</td><td>learned f(x)</td></tr> <tr><td>Uncertainty</td><td>explicit sampling</td><td>point prediction, uncertainty discarded by f</td></tr> <tr><td>Failure mode</td><td>compute spent on texture, sampling cost</td><td>representation collapse, uninterpretable latents</td></tr> <tr><td>Cost per step</td><td>many denoising steps, or O(N) tokens</td><td>one forward pass</td></tr> <tr><td>Evaluation</td><td>FVD, PSNR, human preference</td><td>only through probes or control</td></tr> <tr><td>Counterfactual video</td><td>yes</td><td>no, not without a decoder</td></tr> </tbody> </table> </div> <div class="panel__foot" data-ev="wl"> The efficiency claim has a clean measurement behind it: swapping SD-VAE latents for V-JEPA 2.1 features in an NWM-style CDiT planner cut denoising steps from at least 128 to 8 and planning time from 103.2 s to 10.6 s at equal or lower ATE. <span class="ev ev--wl">E</span> </div> </div> <div class="col"> <div class="note note--warn" data-ev="inf"> <p><b>The caveat the motivation glosses over.</b> A deterministic JEPA predictor trained with ℓ2 converges to E[S<sub>y</sub> | S<sub>x</sub>], and with ℓ1 to the coordinate-wise median. Every JEPA is therefore a prediction loss plus a mechanism that keeps the representation from going flat. Turn the regulariser off below and watch the embeddings fall into a point. </p> </div> <div class="panel"> <div class="panel__head"> <span>Embedding batch under a prediction pull</span> <span class="readout" id="sim-read" style="margin-left:auto"></span> </div> <div class="panel__body"> <canvas class="sim__canvas" id="sim" width="880" height="300" aria-label="A batch of embeddings collapsing to a point, or held isotropic by the regulariser"></canvas> <div class="ctrl" style="margin-top:.8rem"> <button type="button" class="btn" id="sim-run">Run 200 steps</button> <button type="button" class="btn" id="sim-reset">Reset</button> <div class="seg" role="group" aria-label="Collapse control"> <button type="button" data-sim="none" aria-pressed="true">no regulariser</button> <button type="button" data-sim="sigreg" aria-pressed="false">SIGReg</button> </div> </div> </div> <div class="panel__foot"> The pull is the prediction term, which is happiest when every embedding is the same point. SIGReg answers it with a distributional constraint rather than a negative pair: keep the batch isotropic Gaussian, tested through 1-D projections. A constant batch has a degenerate characteristic function, so the statistic blows up and collapse is excluded by construction. <span class="ev ev--wl">E</span> </div> </div> <div class="acc" data-acc> <button type="button" class="acc__head" aria-expanded="true"><span class="n">a</span> EMA target with stop-gradient <span class="chev">›</span></button> <div class="acc__body" data-ev="wl"> <p>BYOL, I-JEPA, V-JEPA. The collapsed solution is a fixed point but not the attractor. Tian, Chen and Ganguli (ICML 2021) show for the linear case that the predictor comes to share eigenvectors with the representation correlation matrix, and eigenmodes above a threshold set by weight decay and EMA rate grow instead of decaying. Three ingredients carry it: the predictor gives the online branch a degree of freedom the target lacks, the stop-gradient removes the term that would pull the target toward the prediction, and the EMA makes the target a lagged, non-degenerate function. <span class="ev ev--wl">E</span></p> <p>There is no guarantee. Partial dimensional collapse, where the representation occupies a low-rank subspace, is common in non-contrastive methods. V-JEPA's robustness here is empirical. <span class="ev ev--wl">E</span></p> </div> </div> <div class="acc" data-acc> <button type="button" class="acc__head" aria-expanded="false"><span class="n">b</span> Masked prediction as a bottleneck on the query <span class="chev">›</span></button> <div class="acc__body" data-ev="inf"> <p>A constant f still scores zero loss, so masking does not prevent collapse by itself. What it does is shape which non-trivial solutions are reachable: the predictor must produce a different output per masked position, so a slightly non-constant encoder immediately becomes useful. <span class="ev ev--inf">I</span></p> </div> </div> <div class="acc" data-acc> <button type="button" class="acc__head" aria-expanded="false"><span class="n">c</span> Variance and covariance terms (VICReg, PLDM) <span class="chev">›</span></button> <div class="acc__body" data-ev="wl"> <p>Hinge the per-dimension standard deviation above γ and drive off-diagonal covariance to zero. Sample-contrastive in effect, with no explicit negatives. <span class="ev ev--wl">E</span></p> </div> </div> <div class="acc" data-acc> <button type="button" class="acc__head" aria-expanded="false"><span class="n">d</span> Distribution matching (SIGReg, LeJEPA, used by LeWM) <span class="chev">›</span></button> <div class="acc__body" data-ev="wl"> <p>Constrain the batch to be isotropic Gaussian. Testing d-dimensional Gaussianity directly is expensive, so SIGReg uses Cramér-Wold: a distribution is fixed by its 1-D projections. Draw M unit directions, project, and apply the Epps-Pulley statistic against the standard normal characteristic function, evaluated by quadrature. Cost is O(BMK) and it is differentiable. LeWM uses M = 1024 and weight 0.1. This is what removes the EMA, the stop-gradient and the pretrained encoder, and it is the enabling change for end-to-end training from pixels. <span class="ev ev--wl">E</span></p> <p><b>The price.</b> SIGReg forces all d dimensions to carry unit variance. When the true state manifold has intrinsic dimension far below d, a point mass in a room has about two, the encoder is pushed to spread information into nuisance directions. LeWM reports exactly this on Two-Room. The EMA route has the opposite bias: it tolerates dimensional collapse, which is harmless at low intrinsic dimension and costly at high. <span class="ev ev--wl">E</span> for the report, <span class="ev ev--inf">I</span> for the symmetry.</p> </div> </div> </section> <!-- 3 ------------------------------------------------------------------ --> <section id="s3"> <div class="sec-head"><span class="num">03</span><h2>The masking cube</h2></div> <div class="col"> <p> This is the part worth turning over in your hands. Move L across 1 and the whole character of the model changes. </p> <div class="formula">‖e<sub>k</sub>‖ ≤ ε · (L<sup>k</sup> − 1)/(L − 1)   →   saturates at ε/(1−L) when L < 1, grows geometrically when L > 1</div> </div> <div class="panel"> <div class="panel__head"><span>Compounding error over a rollout</span><span class="readout" id="drift-read" style="margin-left:auto"></span></div> <div class="panel__body"> <canvas class="drift__canvas" id="drift" width="880" height="300" aria-label="Error growth across rollout steps for three Lipschitz constants"></canvas> <div class="ctrl" style="margin-top:.8rem"> <label>one-step error ε <input type="range" id="drift-e" min="1" max="50" value="10"></label> <label>Lipschitz L <input type="range" id="drift-l" min="70" max="140" value="105"></label> <label>steps <input type="range" id="drift-k" min="5" max="60" step="5" value="30"></label> </div> </div> <div class="panel__foot" data-ev="wl"> Teacher forcing only ever trains g on the data manifold, so L off-manifold is uncontrolled. I have not seen this used. <span class="ev ev--spec">S</span></li> <li style="padding-left:0" data-ev="inf"><b>Short horizon plus MPC.</b> The fix everyone actually ships: plan short, execute one action, re-encode the real observation, replan.
