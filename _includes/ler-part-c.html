More structure, less scalability

The more structure a method has, the less it scales. Structure added for today's compute becomes tomorrow's ceiling. Talk, closing slide Talk

The slide's plot carries no data. It restates two older positions: Hyung Won Chung's argument (Stanford CS25, 2024) that the right amount of inductive bias depends on the compute available, and Rich Sutton's bitter lesson.

The biggest lesson that can be read from 70 years of AI research is that general methods that leverage computation are ultimately the most effective, and by a large margin. Rich Sutton, The Bitter Lesson, 2019

  1. One model carries the case. Nearly every robot result is GPT-6 Astra within five weeks of release, as preprints, leaderboard entries and demos. GPT-5.5 at 0.88% on RoboDojo shows that "language models" is the wrong unit of analysis. Reviewer

Part 14

What this changes for my research

My work sits on three of the talk's fault lines: world models, agentic policies and the representation a manipulation policy needs. Here is where the argument moves my plans and where I think it is wrong.

World models: a fitted simulator is one

The talk's real2sim objective is the training loss of an action-conditioned world model, with a physics engine as the function class. For the latent world models I build for mobile robots and for latent lookahead in manipulation, the useful import is the evaluation: score rollouts over a horizon and score contact events separately, and keep dynamics fidelity, controllability and downstream planning utility as three axes. RoboJEPA's claim that imagination error predicts planning success is a hypothesis I can test on my own models before trusting it. Reviewer

Agentic policies: keep a grounded low level, and report recovery honestly

The hybrid results (48% against 26% on bimanual tasks) support what AFFORD-X and the AGI latent-lookahead work already assume: a reasoning model selects and verifies, and a grounded layer handles metric precision. RoboICL's grammar of observation, action, receipt and observation is a concrete template for an agent's memory of its own attempts. And Lab A is a warning for my own loop numbers: a loop that retries must be reported at pass@1 as well. Reviewer

Representation: the outcome decides what is noise

"Exact kinematics are redundant" is the bet behind learning manipulation-sufficient representations through outcome bottlenecks, put in other words: keep what changes the outcome and drop the rest. The insertion counter-case in Part 07 is the test such a representation has to pass. Reviewer

The idea it raises: agentic loops reported at pass@1, recovery and verifier false-pass

Every agentic manipulation result with retries, mine included, should carry four numbers: first-try success, success within k retries, the recovery rate after a failure, and the share of verifier-passed rollouts a human marks as failures. Cheap to collect from rollouts that already exist, and it pre-empts the first question a reviewer will ask about a 99%.
