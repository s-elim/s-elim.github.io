Open research questions, most decision-relevant first

  1. Does hierarchy survive closed-loop contact? Every H-JEPA success is simulated with a fixed camera, or offline on DROID. The first physical test, on HWM's Franka protocol, decides whether separate latent spaces matter on a real arm at all. Inference
  2. What replaces inverse dynamics when there are no actions? Internet video has none, and the paper shows SIGReg and time repulsion are not enough on diverse scenes. Latent actions learned from video, or a joint-in-time regulariser, are the candidates. Inference App. J.5, L.6
  3. Where should a level's step end? Fixed strides ignore task structure. In manipulation, contact and gripper events mark the natural boundaries; DROID's evaluation already reads its waypoints off the gripper trace. Inference §6, App. J.1
  4. Can a frozen foundation encoder be the bottom level? V-JEPA 2-AC brings a 1B encoder trained on a million hours; H-JEPA brings a training recipe that shapes small encoders per level. Whether the two compose, a frozen V-JEPA 2 or 2.1 at level 1 with learned levels above, is untested. Inference
  5. Which latent distance should a planner trust? Both papers plan on a uniform distance over every feature. Tables 1 and 9 show the choice of space changes success by up to a factor of four (FourRoom, two levels: 9.3% native against 38.7% projected); nobody has learned the metric for planning directly. Inference
  6. How should uncertainty propagate down the hierarchy? Every predictor in the family is deterministic. A top level that is unsure which of two routes the world takes should hand the level below two subgoals, not their average. Speculation
