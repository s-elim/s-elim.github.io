## Core concept

Long-horizon robot planning demands predictive representations at different timescales and levels of abstraction. While high-frequency motor control requires tracking millimeter-level contact geometries and instantaneous joint velocities at 50 Hz, high-level task planning requires reasoning over macro-milestones spanning seconds or minutes.

Traditional pixel-generative world models try to predict raw visual observations across extended futures. This strategy carries severe drawbacks:
1. Computational overhead: Autoregressively synthesizing pixel frames wastes representational capacity on high-frequency visual textures, background shadows, and ambient lighting that exert zero causal effect on the robot's physical transitions.
2. Timescale mismatch: When a single latent state is forced to capture both millisecond contact forces and long-range task navigation, high-frequency physical details drift unpredictably over extended rollouts.
3. Compounding error: Autoregressive rollouts accumulate compounding drift in latent space, destroying downstream model-predictive control accuracy.

The Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), pioneered by LeCun and Balestriero, bypasses pixel generation entirely. Instead of predicting raw observation frames $\hat{o}_{t+1} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$, a JEPA optimizes predictions purely in a learned latent representation space:

$$\hat{z}_{t+1} = \text{Pred}_\theta(z_t, a_t), \qquad z_{t+1} = \text{Enc}_\phi(o_{t+1}) .$$

Hierarchical world models scale this paradigm to multi-timescale predictive abstraction. The architecture trains a multi-level pyramid of latent world models end-to-end. Each level operates over a distinct temporal stride and predicts forward in **its own learned latent space**:
- Level 1 operates at the base control frequency (50 Hz), capturing fine contact dynamics and motor torques.
- Level 2 operates at a macroscopic timescale (e.g., 5 Hz, temporal stride $\sigma = 10$), predicting abstract subgoals while ignoring high-frequency joint flutter and distractor motions.
- Level 3 operates across multi-second task horizons, establishing global task trajectory milestones.

Planning runs top-down: the highest level optimizes macro-actions toward the target goal, its predictions serve as intermediate subgoals for the intermediate level, and the lowest level unrolls concrete motor primitives for closed-loop execution.

## Hierarchical predictive learning

Hierarchical world models learn this multi-timescale latent dynamics directly from observation-action trajectories without generative decoders or hand-engineered state abstractions.

At each level $l \in \{1, \dots, L\}$, the architecture consists of four modular components:
1. **Observation Encoder** $\text{Enc}^{(l)}$: Maps lower-level representations into level-$l$ latent vectors. Level 1 processes raw multimodal observations (RGB camera frames, depth maps, proprioceptive joint states). Level 2 processes a temporal window of Level 1 latent embeddings $z_{t : t + W}^{(1)}$.
2. **Action Encoder** $\text{Enc}_a^{(l)}$: Compresses a sequence of lower-level actions over temporal stride $\sigma_l$ into a macro-action embedding $a^{(l)}$. At Level 1, this represents raw motor torques or Cartesian velocity vectors.
3. **Action-Conditioned Predictor** $\text{Pred}^{(l)}$: An autoregressive Transformer or MLP that advances the latent state conditioned on macro-action $a_t^{(l)}$:
$$\hat{z}_{t+1}^{(l)} = \text{Pred}^{(l)}(z_t^{(l)}, a_t^{(l)}) .$$
4. **Regularization Objective**: Self-supervised non-contrastive regularization via SIGReg (Skew-Independent Gaussian Regularization) to prevent representation collapse without requiring negative pairs or heuristic momentum encoders.

Gradients from higher-level prediction losses flow directly through lower-level encoders, training the entire hierarchy jointly end-to-end.

## Hierarchical planning from coarse to fine

Planning in hierarchical world models optimizes actions across the temporal hierarchy in a top-down fashion. Instead of running expensive sample-based planning (such as the Cross-Entropy Method or Model Predictive Path Integral control) over thousands of primitive motor steps, the search space is factored hierarchically:

<div class="hjepa-interactive-frame">
  <iframe src="media/hjepa/interactive/hierarchical-planning.html?embed=1" style="width:100%; height:620px; border:none;" title="Interactive hierarchical planning animation" loading="lazy"></iframe>
</div>

1. **Top-Level Optimization**: Given current observation $o_0$ and target goal $o_g$, the highest level computes latent representations $z_0^{(L)} = \text{Enc}^{(L)}(o_0)$ and $z_g^{(L)} = \text{Enc}^{(L)}(o_g)$. The planner optimizes a sequence of macro-actions to minimize terminal latent distance:
$$\min_{\{a_t^{(L)}\}} \mathcal{D}\Big( \hat{z}_{H_L}^{(L)}, z_g^{(L)} \Big) .$$
2. **Subgoal Propagation**: The unrolled trajectory states $\hat{z}_1^{(L)}, \hat{z}_2^{(L)}, \dots$ serve as intermediate goal targets for Level $L-1$.
3. **Low-Level Motor Synthesis**: The base level unrolls continuous action primitives $\hat{a}_{0:K}^{(1)}$ to reach each short-horizon waypoint, executing actions on the physical robot in a closed feedback loop.

![Hierarchical planning overview](media/hjepa/figures/overview.png)

This temporal factoring reduces the effective search horizon from hundreds of raw timesteps to a small sequence of structured latent decisions, dramatically reducing planner compute while escaping local minima.

## Mathematics

### Joint-embedding predictive energy

Let an observation trajectory be denoted by $(o_1, a_1, o_2, a_2, \dots, o_T)$. For hierarchy level $l$, the forward prediction error measures alignment between predicted latent state and encoded future observation:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}^{(l)}(\theta, \phi) = \frac{1}{T_l - 1} \sum_{t=1}^{T_l - 1} \Big\| \text{Pred}_\theta^{(l)}\big(z_t^{(l)}, a_t^{(l)}\big) - z_{t+1}^{(l)} \Big\|_2^2 ,$$

where $z_t^{(l)} = \text{Enc}_\phi^{(l)}(s_t^{(l-1)})$ is the target embedding produced by the encoder.

### SIGReg: Collapse prevention without heuristics

In non-generative joint embeddings, minimizing predictive loss alone causes dimensional collapse: the encoder can trivially project all observations to a constant vector $z_t = \mathbf{c}$, reducing prediction error to zero.

The architecture utilizes SIGReg (Skew-Independent Gaussian Regularization). Rather than using data augmentation pairs or momentum target networks, SIGReg forces the distribution of encoded embeddings across the batch $Z = [z_1, \dots, z_B]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times d}$ to match an isotropic standard normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(0, I_d)$.

The regularizer decomposes into two penalty terms:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SIGReg}}(Z) = \lambda_{\mu} \|\bar{z}\|_2^2 + \lambda_{\Sigma} \Big\| \frac{1}{B-1} (Z - \bar{z})^\top (Z - \bar{z}) - I_d \Big\|_F^2 ,$$

where $\bar{z} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B z_i$ is the empirical batch mean, and $\|\cdot\|_F$ denotes the Frobenius norm. By constraining the empirical covariance to the identity matrix $I_d$, all latent dimensions remain mutually uncorrelated and non-degenerate.

### Inverse Dynamics Regularization for heterogeneous robot data

On multi-scene robotic datasets such as DROID, camera viewpoints, lighting, and table backgrounds vary drastically across demonstrations. A purely predictive objective can minimize loss by modeling the static scene background while ignoring small manipulator motions.

To guarantee that latent states retain control-relevant information about the robot's physical configuration, the architecture incorporates an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) objective:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{IDM}}^{(l)}(\psi) = \frac{1}{T_l - 1} \sum_{t=1}^{T_l - 1} \Big\| \text{IDM}_\psi^{(l)}\big(z_t^{(l)}, z_{t+1}^{(l)}\big) - a_t^{(l)} \Big\|_2^2 .$$

The complete joint training objective optimizes:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{l=1}^L \Big( \mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}^{(l)} + \alpha_l \mathcal{L}_{\text{SIGReg}}^{(l)} + \beta_l \mathcal{L}_{\text{IDM}}^{(l)} \Big) .$$

### Multimodal physical sensor fusion: RGB-D, LiDAR, Radar, and Raman

Physical robot perception operates across heterogeneous sensory bands beyond optical RGB cameras. When manipulating objects in industrial, chemical, or unstructured environments, optical vision alone is degraded by occlusions, specular reflections, ambient dust, and visually indistinguishable materials.

Hierarchical world models natively accommodate multimodal physical sensing by mapping distinct sensory streams into token embeddings before latent projection:

1. **3D RGB-D Vision**: High-resolution photometric pixels aligned with metric depth maps ($0.20$ to $2.50\,\text{m}$), providing global scene context and visual edge geometry: $e_{\text{RGB-D}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$.
2. **3D Time-of-Flight LiDAR**: 64-beam pulsed laser range returns measuring calibrated metric clearances and surface reflectivity intensity: $e_{\text{LiDAR}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$.
3. **77 GHz mmWave FMCW Radar**: Range-Doppler velocity spectrograms measuring radial velocity $v_r = \frac{c \Delta f_d}{2 f_0}$ and radar cross-section ($\text{RCS}$ in $\text{dBm}^2$), penetrating dust, smoke, and visual clutter to track moving bodies: $e_{\text{Radar}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$.
4. **Wrist-Mounted Raman Spectroscopy**: Inelastic molecular laser scattering ($\lambda = 785\,\text{nm}$) measuring vibrational intensity across wavenumbers ($400 - 3200\,\text{cm}^{-1}$). Identifies chemical composition (distinguishing polyethylene polymers from nylon polyamides and aluminum alloys) for material-grounded manipulation: $e_{\text{Raman}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$.
5. **Proprioception & Joint Telemetry**: 7-DOF joint angles $q$, joint velocities $\dot{q}$, and torque commands $\tau$: $e_{\text{proprio}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$.

The level-1 observation representation combines these modal tokens into a unified embedding:

$$z_t^{(1)} = \text{Enc}_{\text{fusion}}\big( [e_{\text{RGB-D}}, e_{\text{LiDAR}}, e_{\text{Radar}}, e_{\text{Raman}}, e_{\text{proprio}}] \big) .$$

#### The non-generative JEPA advantage

Generative world models fail when scaling to multimodal physical suites. Synthesizing raw radar chirp spectrograms, ToF range distributions, chemical Raman spectra, and pixel grids simultaneously requires modeling complex, multimodal noise characteristics across incompatible physics engines.

Because hierarchical world models predict strictly within learned latent embeddings without generative decoders, they fuse complementary physical sensors without generating raw sensor outputs. SIGReg regularizes the fused representation, preventing dominant modalities (such as dense pixels) from suppressing sparse modalities (such as Raman spectra or radar Doppler velocity).

## Code implementation

The Python companion script [`examples/l23_9_hjepa.py`](code/examples/l23_9_hjepa.py) provides a complete, self-contained PyTorch implementation of a two-level hierarchical world model architecture with SIGReg regularization and a top-down latent visual planner:

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F

class SIGRegLoss(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, lambd_mean=1.0, lambd_cov=1.0):
        super().__init__()
        self.lambd_mean = lambd_mean
        self.lambd_cov = lambd_cov

    def forward(self, z):
        b, d = z.shape
        mean = z.mean(dim=0)
        mean_loss = torch.sum(mean ** 2)
        z_centered = z - mean
        cov = (z_centered.T @ z_centered) / (b - 1)
        eye = torch.eye(d, device=z.device)
        cov_loss = torch.sum((cov - eye) ** 2)
        return self.lambd_mean * mean_loss + self.lambd_cov * cov_loss

class MultimodalSensorEncoder(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, token_dim=16):
        super().__init__()
        # Encoders for heterogeneous physical sensors: RGB-D, LiDAR, Radar, Raman, Proprio
        self.rgb_enc = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(32, 32), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(32, token_dim))
        self.depth_enc = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(16, 32), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(32, token_dim))
        self.lidar_enc = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(16, 32), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(32, token_dim))
        self.radar_enc = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(12, 32), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(32, token_dim))
        self.raman_enc = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(24, 32), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(32, token_dim))
        self.proprio_enc = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(14, 32), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(32, token_dim))
        self.fusion = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(6 * token_dim, 64), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(64, 32))

    def forward(self, rgb, depth, lidar, radar, raman, proprio):
        tokens = [self.rgb_enc(rgb), self.depth_enc(depth), self.lidar_enc(lidar),
                  self.radar_enc(radar), self.raman_enc(raman), self.proprio_enc(proprio)]
        return self.fusion(torch.cat(tokens, dim=-1))

class HierarchicalJEPA(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, token_dim=16, act_dim=7, l1_dim=32, l2_dim=16, stride=4):
        super().__init__()
        self.stride = stride
        self.sensor_encoder = MultimodalSensorEncoder(token_dim=token_dim)
        self.pred1 = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(l1_dim + act_dim, 64), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(64, l1_dim))
        self.idm1 = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(2 * l1_dim, 64), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(64, act_dim))
        self.enc2 = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(l1_dim, 32), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(32, l2_dim))
        self.act_enc2 = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(stride * act_dim, 32), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(32, act_dim))
        self.pred2 = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(l2_dim + act_dim, 32), nn.SiLU(), nn.Linear(32, l2_dim))
        self.sigreg = SIGRegLoss()

    def forward_loss(self, sensors, act_seq):
        z1 = self.sensor_encoder(sensors["rgb"], sensors["depth"], sensors["lidar"],
                                 sensors["radar"], sensors["raman"], sensors["proprio"])
        z1_pred = self.pred1(torch.cat([z1[:, :-1], act_seq], dim=-1))
        loss_pred1 = F.mse_loss(z1_pred, z1[:, 1:])
        loss_sigreg1 = self.sigreg(z1.reshape(-1, z1.shape[-1]))

        # Subsample coarse steps according to temporal stride
        z2 = self.enc2(z1[:, ::self.stride])
        macro_act = self.act_enc2(act_seq[:, :self.stride].reshape(act_seq.shape[0], -1))
        z2_pred = self.pred2(torch.cat([z2[:, 0], macro_act], dim=-1))
        loss_pred2 = F.mse_loss(z2_pred, z2[:, 1])
        loss_sigreg2 = self.sigreg(z2.reshape(-1, z2.shape[-1]))

        return loss_pred1 + 0.1 * loss_sigreg1 + loss_pred2 + 0.1 * loss_sigreg2
```
