## Core concept

Lesson 5.3 asked whether a grasp holds an object at rest. Manipulation asks whether it holds while the object is moved, and the answer comes from the same friction budget, now spent on acceleration as well as weight. Two pads squeezing with normal force $N$ and friction coefficient $\mu$ can supply at most $2\mu N$ of tangential force; carrying a mass $m$ with acceleration $a$ needs $m\,\|a - g\|$ of it. Lifting adds to the demand, lowering subtracts from it, and moving sideways adds in quadrature.

Two practical facts make the budget harder to respect than the formula suggests. The acceleration the object actually feels is not the planned one but whatever the controller delivers, overshoot included. And slip is not all-or-nothing: a brief excess costs a fraction of a millimetre, a sustained one drops the object. This lesson measures both, then works through MuJoCo's documented checklist for preventing slip.

> [!established] MuJoCo's checklist for slip, in its documented order
> First, the slip-preventing forces must lie inside the friction cone: enough normal force (maximum squeeze times $\mu$ well above the weight), a high enough $\mu$, and torsional friction (`condim` 4 or 6) when torques about the contact normal are needed. Second, the geometry must support the forces: enough contact points (keep `multiccd` and `nativeccd` enabled for convex meshes). Third, no high-frequency vibration: lower gains, add armature, use `implicitfast` or `implicit` against explicit damping. Last, slow slippage: elliptic cones with higher `impratio`, then NoSlip.

## Visual intuition

**Grasp and lift** picks up the cube, raises it 3 cm, then lifts it a further 10 cm with a minimum-jerk profile of the duration you choose. The readouts compare the acceleration the carriage delivered with the grasp's upward budget.

1. With the default 1 s lift, the delivered peak is under 1 m/s² against a budget of about 13.7 m/s². The slip readout still creeps up to a couple of millimetres over the next seconds: that is creep (Lesson 9.3), not the lift.
2. Shorten the lift to 0.2 s: the delivered peak rises above the budget for a moment, yet the slip ends up no larger than after the slow lift. A brief excess costs almost nothing.
3. Shorten it to 0.15 s: the cube slides about a centimetre down between the pads. Raise the cube mass to 2 kg and repeat at 0.3 s: the budget shrinks with $m$ and the same lift now fails.

## Mathematics

### The budget under acceleration

In the frame of the moving gripper, the object feels gravity plus an inertial force $-ma$. Both lie in the pads' tangent plane when the grasp axis is horizontal, as here, so the grasp holds while

$$m\,\|a - g\| \le 2\mu N .$$

For pure vertical motion this is $m(g + a_\uparrow) \le 2\mu N$, giving the largest upward acceleration $a_\uparrow = 2\mu N/m - g$. For pure horizontal motion the two add in quadrature: $a_\rightarrow = \sqrt{(2\mu N/m)^2 - g^2}$. With this gripper's 7.83 N per pad, $\mu$ = 1.5 and a 1 kg cube: 13.7 m/s² up, 21.3 m/s² sideways (output 1).

### From a planned motion to a delivered acceleration

A minimum-jerk move of distance $d$ in time $T$ has a peak acceleration of $5.77\,d/T^2$, so a budget becomes a shortest allowed duration: $T_\text{min} = \sqrt{5.77\,d/a_\text{max}}$, or 0.21 s for a 10 cm lift here. But the gantry's servo tracks the plan imperfectly, and its overshoot raised the delivered peak 25 to 70% above the planned one (outputs 2 and 3). Budget against the delivered acceleration, or leave a margin of at least that size.

### Brief excess versus sustained excess

Above the budget, the cube accelerates relative to the pads by the excess, and slip is that excess integrated twice over the time it lasts. A 0.2 s lift exceeded the budget by about 30% for a moment and slipped 0.45 mm, no more than creep. A 0.15 s lift exceeded it threefold and slipped 11 mm; a 0.1 s lift, 37 mm. The same holds sideways, where a 0.15 s carry dropped the cube.

> [!recommendation] Plan with the budget, then verify on the delivered motion
> Compute $a_\text{max}$ for the heaviest object and lowest $\mu$ you expect, choose durations with a margin for controller overshoot, and log the slip during evaluation as Lesson 10.2 does. If faster motion is needed, raise $N$ (stiffer servo or force-limited clamp, Lesson 5.3), raise $\mu$, or reorient the grasp so gravity is carried by normal force rather than friction (a gripper underneath, or a cage grasp).

### The slip audit

Output (4) evaluates the documented checklist for this grasp and passes every item: the friction available (11.7 N per pad) exceeds the weight per pad (4.9 N); pad priority fixes $\mu$ at 1.5; `condim` 4 provides torsional friction; four contact points per pad; `implicitfast`; elliptic cones with `impratio` 10. The audit is static: it says the grasp can hold the cube at rest, which is necessary and, as outputs (2) and (3) show, not sufficient for fast motion.

```python file=examples/l10_1_grasping.py
"""Lesson 10.1: grasping under motion: the friction budget, fast lifts and transports, a slip audit.

INPUT   gantry_gripper.xml (parallel gripper on a Cartesian gantry), a 1 kg cube
PROCESS (1) the grasp's friction budget: pad normal force, friction coefficient, the
            static capacity and the largest vertical and horizontal accelerations a
            1 kg cube can take;
        (2) lift the cube 0.1 m with minimum-jerk profiles of decreasing duration:
            planned and delivered peak acceleration, slip, outcome;
        (3) carry it 0.2 m sideways the same way;
        (4) the slip audit: MuJoCo's documented checklist evaluated for this grasp
OUTPUT  printed tables

Run:  python examples/l10_1_grasping.py
"""

import math

import mujoco
import numpy as np

from mjcourse import model_path

G, MASS, GRIP = 9.81, 1.0, 3                      # cube mass (kg), gripper actuator index


def scene() -> tuple[mujoco.MjModel, mujoco.MjData]:
    model = mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_path(str(model_path("gantry_gripper")))
    model.body_mass[model.body("cube").id] = MASS
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    mujoco.mj_setConst(model, data)
    return model, data


def run(model, data, seconds: float) -> None:
    for _ in range(round(seconds / model.opt.timestep)):
        mujoco.mj_step(model, data)


def min_jerk(s: float) -> float:
    s = min(max(s, 0.0), 1.0)
    return 10 * s**3 - 15 * s**4 + 6 * s**5


def grasp(model, data) -> None:
    """Settle, descend 0.33 m, close, then raise 3 cm slowly so the cube hangs free."""
    mujoco.mj_resetDataKeyframe(model, data, 0)
    run(model, data, 0.3)
    for k in range(100):
        data.ctrl[2] = -0.33 * min_jerk((k + 1) / 100)
        run(model, data, 0.01)
    run(model, data, 0.3)
    data.ctrl[GRIP] = 0.0
    run(model, data, 0.6)
    for k in range(100):
        data.ctrl[2] = -0.33 + 0.03 * min_jerk((k + 1) / 100)
        run(model, data, 0.01)
    run(model, data, 0.3)


def pad_normal(model, data) -> tuple[float, float, int]:
    pad, cube = model.geom("gripper/pad_left").id, model.geom("cube").id
    total, mu, n, f = 0.0, 0.0, 0, np.zeros(6)
    for i in range(data.ncon):
        c = data.contact[i]
        if {c.geom1, c.geom2} == {pad, cube}:
            mujoco.mj_contactForce(model, data, i, f)
            total, mu, n = total + f[0], c.friction[0], n + 1
    return total, mu, n


def move(axis: int, distance: float, duration: float) -> tuple[float, float, str]:
    """Move the gantry along one axis with a minimum-jerk profile, hold 0.5 s; return
    (delivered peak acceleration, slip in mm, outcome)."""
    model, data = scene()
    grasp(model, data)
    cube, tcp = model.body("cube").id, model.site("gripper/tcp").id
    offset = data.site_xpos[tcp] - data.xpos[cube]
    start, n = data.ctrl[axis], max(1, round(duration / model.opt.timestep))
    peak = 0.0
    for k in range(n + 250):
        data.ctrl[axis] = start + distance * min_jerk((k + 1) / n)
        mujoco.mj_step(model, data)
        peak = max(peak, abs(data.qacc[axis]))              # the carriage's actual acceleration
    slip = 1000 * float(np.linalg.norm((data.site_xpos[tcp] - data.xpos[cube]) - offset))
    outcome = "dropped" if data.xpos[cube][2] < 0.025 else ("slipped" if slip > 5 else "held")
    return peak, slip, outcome


def budget() -> tuple[float, float]:
    model, data = scene()
    grasp(model, data)
    normal, mu, n = pad_normal(model, data)
    capacity = 2 * mu * normal
    a_up = capacity / MASS - G
    a_side = math.sqrt((capacity / MASS) ** 2 - G**2)
    print(f"  pad normal force {normal:.2f} N ({n} contacts per pad), contact friction {mu}, friction budget 2 mu N = {capacity:.2f} N")
    print(f"  static capacity 2 mu N / g = {capacity / G:.2f} kg; for the {MASS} kg cube: largest upward acceleration "
          f"2 mu N / m - g = {a_up:.2f} m/s^2, largest horizontal sqrt((2 mu N / m)^2 - g^2) = {a_side:.2f} m/s^2")
    return a_up, a_side


def sweep(axis: int, distance: float, durations, limit: float, name: str) -> None:
    print(f"  {'duration (s)':>12}{'planned peak (m/s^2)':>22}{'delivered peak (m/s^2)':>24}{'slip (mm)':>11}{'outcome':>10}")
    for duration in durations:
        planned = 5.7735 * distance / duration**2               # peak of a minimum-jerk acceleration
        peak, slip, outcome = move(axis, distance, duration)
        flag = " (over budget)" if peak > limit else ""
        print(f"  {duration:>12.2f}{planned:>22.1f}{peak:>24.1f}{slip:>11.2f}{outcome:>10}{flag}")
    print(f"  {name} budget: {limit:.2f} m/s^2")


def audit() -> None:
    model, data = scene()
    grasp(model, data)
    normal, mu, n = pad_normal(model, data)
    pad = model.geom("gripper/pad_left").id
    force_limit = model.actuator_forcerange[GRIP, 1]
    checks = [
        ("normal force: squeeze x mu vs weight", f"{mu * normal:.1f} N of friction per pad in use, {mu * force_limit / 2:.1f} N "
         f"if the servo saturated, weight per pad {MASS * G / 2:.2f} N", mu * normal > MASS * G / 2),
        ("friction coefficient", f"{mu} (pad priority {model.geom_priority[pad]} fixes it at the pad's value)", mu >= 1.0),
        ("torsional friction", f"condim {model.geom_condim[pad]}, torsional coefficient {model.geom_friction[pad][1]} m", model.geom_condim[pad] >= 4),
        ("contact points", f"{n} per pad (box-box collider)", n >= 3),
        ("integrator (explicit damping causes vibration)", mujoco.mjtIntegrator(model.opt.integrator).name,
         model.opt.integrator in (int(mujoco.mjtIntegrator.mjINT_IMPLICITFAST), int(mujoco.mjtIntegrator.mjINT_IMPLICIT))),
        ("slow slippage: cone and impratio", f"{mujoco.mjtCone(model.opt.cone).name}, impratio {model.opt.impratio:g}, "
         f"noslip {model.opt.noslip_iterations}", model.opt.cone == int(mujoco.mjtCone.mjCONE_ELLIPTIC) and model.opt.impratio > 1),
    ]
    for name, value, ok in checks:
        print(f"  [{'ok' if ok else 'check'}] {name:<48} {value}")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(f"(1) the friction budget of the gantry grasp, {MASS} kg cube")
    a_up, a_side = budget()
    print("(2) lift 0.1 m, minimum-jerk")
    sweep(2, 0.1, (1.0, 0.5, 0.3, 0.2, 0.15, 0.1), a_up, "upward")
    print("(3) carry 0.2 m along x, minimum-jerk")
    sweep(0, 0.2, (1.0, 0.5, 0.3, 0.25, 0.2, 0.15), a_side, "horizontal")
    print("(4) slip audit: MuJoCo's documented checklist for preventing slip, applied to this grasp")
    audit()
```

## Debugging

**A grasp holds when lifted slowly and drops on fast moves.** Compare the delivered acceleration with $2\mu N/m - g$ (and its horizontal counterpart). Slow the motion, raise $N$, or change the grasp.

**Slip grows steadily during long holds or slow motions.** Creep, not acceleration (Lesson 9.3): it scales with hold time, not with speed. Raise `impratio` or enable NoSlip.

**The object rotates in the grasp during a sideways move.** The inertial force does not pass through the line between the pads, so it makes a torque about the grasp axis. Add torsional friction (`condim` 4), grasp closer to the centre of mass, or slow down.

**The grasp fails only on the real robot.** The simulated $N$, $\mu$ and controller overshoot differ from the robot's; identify them (Level 18) before tuning motion speed in simulation.

## Research connection

> [!research] Speed limits learned policies should know about
> Learned pick-and-place policies are often evaluated at fixed slow speeds and then run faster on hardware. The budget above makes the limit explicit: it depends on mass, squeeze, friction and the controller's overshoot, all of which vary between objects and robots. A policy that learned fast motions on light simulated objects can drop heavy real ones with no change in its inputs (inference). Randomizing mass and friction (Level 17) or giving the policy access to the grasp's force state are two ways to make that limit visible to it.
