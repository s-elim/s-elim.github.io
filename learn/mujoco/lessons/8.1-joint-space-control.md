## Core concept

A joint-space controller decides each joint's torque from where the joint is and where it should be. The simplest useful one is PD: push towards the target in proportion to the error, and resist velocity.

$$\tau = k_p\,(q^\star - q) - k_d\,\dot q .$$

Two numbers, and nearly every question about them has a quantitative answer. How much does it overshoot? The damping ratio says. Why does it stop short of the target under gravity? The stiffness says by how much. How do you remove that error? With a model of gravity, or with integral action, which brings its own failure at the torque limit. And should you write this controller yourself, or use MuJoCo's servo actuators? It depends on the integrator, for a reason Lesson 5.2 already measured.

This lesson answers each with the pendulum (1 kg at 0.5 m, inertia 0.2501 kg m² about the hinge, a motor limited to ±3 N m), where every prediction is a short formula.

> [!established] MuJoCo's servo actuators
> `<position kp kv>` applies $k_p(u - q) - k_v\dot q$ with $u$ the control, `<velocity kv>` applies $k_v(u - \dot q)$, and since 3.12 `<pid kp kv ki imax>` adds integral action, with the integral of the position error stored in the actuator's activation (`act`) and clamped at `imax` (anti-windup). Under `implicitfast` and `implicit`, MuJoCo integrates the velocity-dependent part of these actuators implicitly; under `Euler` it does not.

## Mathematics

### PD as a mass-spring-damper

Without gravity, the joint obeys $I\ddot q = k_p(q^\star - q) - k_d\dot q$: a mass on a spring with a damper. Its natural frequency and damping ratio are

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{k_p / I}, \qquad \zeta = \frac{k_d}{2\sqrt{k_p I}},$$

and for $\zeta < 1$ a step response overshoots by $e^{-\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}$ of the step, peaking at $t_p = \pi / (\omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2})$. Output (1) checks both for five damping ratios: the measured overshoot is within 0.2 percentage points and the peak time within 2 ms of the formulas. Choose $k_p$ for speed (bandwidth $\approx \omega_n$) and $k_d$ for the overshoot you accept; $\zeta \approx 0.7$ is the usual compromise.

### The error gravity leaves

With gravity, PD settles where its spring balances the weight: $k_p(q^\star - q) = mgL\sin q$. Linearizing around the target gives

$$e_{ss} \approx \frac{mgL\sin q^\star}{k_p + mgL\cos q^\star},$$

which shrinks with $k_p$ but never vanishes. Output (2) measures 162, 96 and 43 mrad for $k_p$ = 10, 20 and 50 against predictions of 164, 97 and 43.

Two remedies. **Gravity compensation** adds the model's gravity torque, $mgL\sin q$ here or `qfrc_bias` on a general robot (Lesson 7.1): exact when the model is exact, and it removes the error instantly (output 2: zero to the printed precision). **Integral action** adds $k_i\int e\,dt$: it needs no model and removes any constant disturbance, but only gradually, and it has a failure mode of its own.

### Windup

While the motor is saturated, the error stays large and the integral keeps growing. When the joint finally reaches the target, the stored integral pushes it past. Output (3): with $k_i$ = 40 and a 0.55 rad target, the arm overshoots by 87 mrad. **Anti-windup** clamps the integral. The clamp must exceed the integral the hold actually needs ($mgL\sin q^\star / k_i$ = 0.064 rad s here): at 0.07 the overshoot falls to 9 mrad and settling from 1.6 s to 0.5 s; at 0.05 the controller can never supply the holding torque and stops 23 mrad short. MuJoCo's `<pid>` with `imax` = 0.07 behaves almost identically (10.5 mrad), with the integral visible in `act`.

### Computing torques yourself versus a servo actuator

The same PD can be computed in your code and written to `ctrl` for a `motor`, or delegated to a `<position>` actuator. The torque is the same; what differs is how the integrator treats the damping. A torque you compute is a constant for the step, so its damping is explicit and stable only for $k_d < 2I/h$ (Lesson 1.3), whatever the integrator. A `<position>` actuator's `kv` is integrated implicitly under `implicitfast`. Output (4) uses $k_v$ = 300 N m s/rad, above the explicit limit of 250: the Python PD diverges under both integrators, the actuator diverges under `Euler` and is stable under `implicitfast`.

> [!recommendation] Which to use
> Use MuJoCo's servo actuators (with `implicitfast`) when the controller is a fixed PD or PID per joint, especially with stiff or heavily damped gains: they are cheaper and more stable. Compute torques yourself when the controller needs the model (gravity compensation, computed torque, operational space: Lessons 8.2 and 8.3), couples joints, or must match a specific real controller's discrete implementation, and keep its damping below $2I/h$.

```python file=examples/l8_1_joint_space.py
"""Lesson 8.1: joint-space control of one joint: P, PD, PID and MuJoCo's servo actuators.

INPUT   pendulum.xml (1 kg at 0.5 m on a hinge, torque motor limited to +-3 N m)
PROCESS (1) gravity off: PD step responses against the damping-ratio predictions;
        (2) gravity on: the steady-state error of PD, and removing it with gravity
            compensation or with integral action;
        (3) integral windup at the torque limit, a hand-written anti-windup clamp, and
            MuJoCo's <pid> actuator with imax;
        (4) the same PD written in Python versus MuJoCo's <position> actuator, under
            Euler and implicitfast, at a damping gain beyond the explicit limit
OUTPUT  printed tables

Run:  python examples/l8_1_joint_space.py
"""

import math

import mujoco
import numpy as np

from mjcourse import model_path

M, L, G = 1.0, 0.5, 9.81
I_PIVOT = M * L**2 + 1e-4                 # kg m^2, from pendulum.xml's <inertial>


def pendulum(gravity: bool = True, torque_limit: float = 3.0):
    model = mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_path(str(model_path("pendulum")))
    if not gravity:
        model.opt.gravity[:] = 0
    model.actuator_ctrlrange[0] = [-torque_limit, torque_limit]
    return model, mujoco.MjData(model)


def run(model, data, policy, seconds: float) -> np.ndarray:
    """Step with ctrl = policy(data); return the angle after each step."""
    out = np.zeros(round(seconds / model.opt.timestep))
    for k in range(len(out)):
        data.ctrl[0] = policy(data)
        mujoco.mj_step(model, data)
        out[k] = data.qpos[0]
    return out


def step_responses() -> None:
    kp, target = 20.0, 0.5
    omega = math.sqrt(kp / I_PIVOT)
    print(f"  kp {kp} N m/rad, I {I_PIVOT} kg m^2 -> natural frequency {omega:.3f} rad/s; step of {target} rad")
    print(f"  {'zeta':>6}{'kd':>8}{'overshoot predicted':>21}{'measured':>10}{'peak time predicted':>21}{'measured':>10}")
    for zeta in (0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0):
        kd = 2 * zeta * math.sqrt(kp * I_PIVOT)
        model, data = pendulum(gravity=False, torque_limit=100.0)
        q = run(model, data, lambda d: kp * (target - d.qpos[0]) - kd * d.qvel[0], 3.0)
        over = 100 * max(q.max() - target, 0) / target
        pred = 100 * math.exp(-math.pi * zeta / math.sqrt(1 - zeta**2)) if zeta < 1 else 0.0
        tpk_pred = f"{math.pi / (omega * math.sqrt(1 - zeta**2)):.3f} s" if zeta < 1 else "none"
        tpk = f"{(np.argmax(q) + 1) * model.opt.timestep:.3f} s" if zeta < 1 else "none"
        print(f"  {zeta:>6.1f}{kd:>8.3f}{pred:>19.1f} %{over:>8.1f} %{tpk_pred:>21}{tpk:>10}")


def gravity_error() -> None:
    target = 0.5
    tau_g = M * G * L * math.sin(target)
    for kp in (10.0, 20.0, 50.0):
        kd = 2 * math.sqrt(kp * I_PIVOT)
        model, data = pendulum()
        q = run(model, data, lambda d: kp * (target - d.qpos[0]) - kd * d.qvel[0], 6.0)
        # Equilibrium: kp (target - q) = m g L sin q; to first order e = m g L sin(target) / (kp + m g L cos(target)).
        pred = tau_g / (kp + M * G * L * math.cos(target))
        print(f"  PD, kp {kp:>4.0f}: steady-state error {1000 * (target - q[-1]):6.1f} mrad, "
              f"first-order prediction {1000 * pred:6.1f} mrad")
    kp = 20.0
    kd = 2 * math.sqrt(kp * I_PIVOT)
    model, data = pendulum()
    q = run(model, data, lambda d: kp * (target - d.qpos[0]) - kd * d.qvel[0] + M * G * L * math.sin(d.qpos[0]), 6.0)
    print(f"  PD + gravity compensation m g L sin q, kp {kp:.0f}: steady-state error {1000 * (target - q[-1]):.4f} mrad")
    integral = [0.0]

    def pid(d, ki=20.0):
        e = target - d.qpos[0]
        integral[0] += e * model.opt.timestep
        return kp * e - kd * d.qvel[0] + ki * integral[0]

    model, data = pendulum()
    q = run(model, data, pid, 6.0)
    print(f"  PID, kp {kp:.0f}, ki 20: error after 6 s {1000 * (target - q[-1]):.4f} mrad")


def windup() -> None:
    target, kp, ki = 0.55, 20.0, 40.0     # holding 0.55 rad takes 2.56 N m of the 3 N m available
    kd = 2 * math.sqrt(kp * I_PIVOT)
    print(f"  holding {target} rad needs an integral of m g L sin(target) / ki = {M * G * L * math.sin(target) / ki:.4f} rad s")
    for clamp in (None, 0.05, 0.07):
        integral = [0.0]
        model, data = pendulum()

        def pid(d):
            e = target - d.qpos[0]
            integral[0] += e * model.opt.timestep
            if clamp is not None:
                integral[0] = min(max(integral[0], -clamp), clamp)
            return kp * e - kd * d.qvel[0] + ki * integral[0]

        q = run(model, data, pid, 8.0)
        label = "no anti-windup" if clamp is None else f"integral clamped at +-{clamp} rad s"
        settle = np.flatnonzero(np.abs(q - target) > 0.01)
        print(f"  Python PID, {label:<32}: peak {1000 * (q.max() - target):+6.1f} mrad from target, "
              f"inside 10 mrad after {(settle[-1] + 1) * model.opt.timestep if len(settle) else 0:.2f} s"
              f"{'' if settle[-1] + 1 < len(q) else ' (never: the clamp is below the integral the hold needs)'}")
    xml = model_path("pendulum").read_text().replace(
        '<motor name="torque" joint="hinge" gear="1" ctrlrange="-3 3"/>',
        f'<pid name="pid" joint="hinge" kp="{kp}" kv="{kd:.6f}" ki="{ki}" imax="0.07" input="pos" forcerange="-3 3"/>')
    model = mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_string(xml)
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    q = run(model, data, lambda d: target, 8.0)
    settle = np.flatnonzero(np.abs(q - target) > 0.01)
    print(f"  MuJoCo <pid> actuator, ki {ki:.0f}, imax 0.07{'':<15}: peak {1000 * (q.max() - target):+6.1f} mrad from target, "
          f"inside 10 mrad after {(settle[-1] + 1) * model.opt.timestep:.2f} s; act holds the integral: {data.act[0]:.4f}")


def servo_versus_python() -> None:
    kp, kv, target = 100.0, 300.0, 0.3       # kv beyond the explicit limit 2 I / h = 250 N m s/rad
    print(f"  kp {kp}, kv {kv} N m s/rad, h 2 ms: explicit damping is stable only for kv < 2 I / h = {2 * I_PIVOT / 0.002:.0f}")
    for integrator in ("mjINT_EULER", "mjINT_IMPLICITFAST"):
        model, data = pendulum(gravity=False, torque_limit=1e6)
        model.opt.integrator = getattr(mujoco.mjtIntegrator, integrator)
        q = run(model, data, lambda d: kp * (target - d.qpos[0]) - kv * d.qvel[0], 4.0)
        xml = model_path("pendulum").read_text().replace(
            '<motor name="torque" joint="hinge" gear="1" ctrlrange="-3 3"/>',
            f'<position name="servo" joint="hinge" kp="{kp}" kv="{kv}"/>')
        smodel = mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_string(xml)
        smodel.opt.gravity[:] = 0
        smodel.opt.integrator = model.opt.integrator
        sdata = mujoco.MjData(smodel)
        qs = run(smodel, sdata, lambda d: target, 4.0)
        name = integrator[6:].lower()
        print(f"  {name:<13} PD in Python: angle after 4 s {q[-1]:.4g} rad (max |q| {np.abs(q).max():.3g}); "
              f"<position> actuator: {qs[-1]:.4g} rad (max |q| {np.abs(qs).max():.3g})")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    print("(1) PD step responses, gravity off")
    step_responses()
    print("(2) under gravity: steady-state error, and two ways to remove it (target 0.5 rad)")
    gravity_error()
    print("(3) integral windup at the 3 N m limit (target 0.55 rad, kp 20, ki 40)")
    windup()
    print("(4) the same PD in Python and as a <position> actuator, gravity off, step to 0.3 rad")
    servo_versus_python()
```

## Debugging

**The joint oscillates forever at a moderate gain.** No damping, or $k_d$ set without regard to the inertia. Compute $\zeta$ from the joint's actual inertia (`mj_fullM` diagonal plus armature) and set $k_d = 2\zeta\sqrt{k_pI}$.

**The joint stops a few degrees short of the target.** Gravity against a finite $k_p$ (output 2). Add gravity compensation, or integral action with a clamp.

**After a large move, the joint overshoots and then crawls back.** Integral windup during saturation (output 3). Clamp the integral above the steady-state value it needs, or stop integrating while the motor is saturated.

**Raising $k_d$ makes the joint blow up.** Explicit damping beyond $2I/h$. Use a `<position>` actuator under `implicitfast`, lower $k_d$, or reduce the timestep (output 4).

## Research connection

> [!research] Low-level controllers shape what policies learn
> Most learned manipulation policies output joint or end-effector targets for a PD loop like this one, not torques. The gains decide how fast and how compliantly targets are reached, so a policy trained with one set of gains can fail with another on the same robot, and reported results depend on gains that papers often omit (inference). Domain randomization over controller gains (Level 17) and identifying the real robot's servo behaviour (Level 18) both start from the formulas in this lesson.
