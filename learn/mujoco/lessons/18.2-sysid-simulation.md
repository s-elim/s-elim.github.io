## Core concept

Regression (Lesson 18.1) needs the dynamics written linearly in the parameters and needs velocities and accelerations differentiated from noisy positions. Identification by simulation needs neither. Pick parameters, simulate the recorded commands from the recorded initial state, compare the simulated sensor readings with the measured ones, and adjust the parameters to shrink the difference. Any parameter the simulator exposes can be fitted this way: contact softness, actuator delay, friction loss. The price is a simulation per evaluation, and a cost that is only as smooth as the simulation. This lesson fits the 18.1 pendulum with MuJoCo's own toolbox, adds an actuator delay the model does not have, and then tries a contact parameter, where the method's weaknesses show.

> [!established] mujoco.sysid
> MuJoCo 3.14.0 ships a system identification toolbox, `mujoco.sysid`, installed with the extra `pip install "mujoco[sysid]==3.14.0"` (the course's `pip install -e ".[sysid]"`). Without the extra, `import mujoco.sysid` still succeeds but the module exports nothing; check for `mujoco.sysid.optimize`. A `Parameter` carries bounds and a `modifier` that writes its value into an `MjSpec`. `ModelSequences` pairs the spec with measured initial states, commands and sensor readings, and `build_residual_fn` turns them into a function that recompiles the model, runs `mujoco.rollout` and returns the sensor errors. `optimize` minimizes those errors by box-bounded nonlinear least squares (`mujoco.minimize.least_squares`, a regularized Gauss-Newton method with a finite-difference Jacobian), and `calculate_intervals` turns the Jacobian at the optimum into 95% intervals.

For a bouncing ball the same surface is jagged, and the optimizer stops wherever its local slope says it should.

## Mathematics

### Simulation error

For parameters $\theta$, simulate the recorded commands $u_{0:N}$ from the recorded initial state $x_0$ and read the predicted sensors $\hat y_k(\theta)$. Minimize

$$J(\theta) = \sum_k \lVert \hat y_k(\theta) - y_k \rVert^2 .$$

Each evaluation is one simulation, and a finite-difference Jacobian costs one simulation per parameter, so a fit takes tens to hundreds of simulations. The errors $\hat y_k - y_k$ are not linear in $\theta$, so there is no closed form and no guarantee that a minimum the optimizer finds is the global one.

### (1) The pendulum: no derivatives, no friction model to get wrong

On the 18.1 record (same hidden values, same multisine, same noise), starting from 1.0 kg, 0.01 and 0.01:

| Estimate | Mass (kg) | Damping | Friction loss (N m) | Simulations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| truth | 1.30000 | 0.08000 | 0.05000 | |
| sysid, samples stamped at their measurement time | 1.30449 ± 0.00021 | 0.08276 ± 0.00027 | 0.04536 ± 0.00030 | 39 |
| sysid, samples stamped one step later | 1.30001 ± 0.00008 | 0.07995 ± 0.00010 | 0.05004 ± 0.00011 | 35 |
| 18.1 regression (Savitzky-Golay) | 1.32655 | 0.08676 | 0.04166 | |

The correctly stamped fit recovers every parameter within its interval, and on the held-out excitation its predicted angle differs from the measurement by 0.50 mrad RMS, which is the measurement noise (0.5 mrad) and what the true parameters also give. The 18.1 regression on the same record gave 2.57 mrad. Simulation error has no derivative noise and uses MuJoCo's own friction-loss constraint, so neither of the two biases of 18.1 can occur.

This is an inverse crime: the data come from the model class being fitted, so exactness is expected. What it shows is that the method adds no bias of its own; the errors of 18.1 came from differentiation and from the friction model, not from the noise. Real data afford neither luxury, which is what parts (2) and (3) are about.

> [!established] Stamp samples the way the simulator does
> For step $k$, `mujoco.rollout` returns the state after the step, at time $(k+1)h$, and the sensor values computed during the step, which MuJoCo evaluates from the state at time $kh$ before integrating: after two steps from rest the pendulum's `qpos` is 3.504e-05 rad while its angle sensor reads 1.168e-05, and `mj_forward` brings the sensor to 3.504e-05. The toolbox times the sensor readings by the state's time. A measured angle taken at $kh$ and stamped $kh$ is therefore compared with a prediction one step older. Measured: that 2 ms offset moves the friction loss by 9% and the damping by 3.5%, with intervals 10 to 20 times narrower than the errors. Stamping the measurements one step later removes it.

### (2) An actuator delay the model does not have

The measured pendulum now applies its torque 10 ms late. MuJoCo 3.14.0 models this per actuator: `delay` (s), a history buffer of `nsample` past commands, and `interp` (0 holds the last sample, 1 interpolates linearly). Fitted without a delay, and then with the delay as a fourth parameter:

| Model | Mass (kg) | Damping | Friction loss (N m) | Delay (s) | Held-out angle error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| without a delay | 1.27683 | 0.06585 | 0.07384 | | 11.32 mrad |
| delay bounded to 0 to 30 ms | 1.28112 | 0.06753 | 0.07050 | 0.00000 ± inf | 9.16 mrad |
| delay bounded to 2 to 30 ms | 1.29999 | 0.07993 | 0.05007 | 0.00999 ± 0.00002 | 0.50 mrad |

Without the delay the fit absorbs the lag into the other parameters: damping 18% low and friction loss 48% high, with intervals of 1.5% or less. A delayed torque $\tau(t-d) \approx \tau(t) - d\,\dot\tau(t)$ adds a term the model can only mimic with other velocity-dependent terms (inference). The estimates are confidently wrong, and the held-out error, about 23 times the noise, is the only sign.

The second row shows why a delay is awkward to fit. Driving the actuator with a ramp and measuring how far its force lags behind gives, with linear interpolation and a 2 ms step:

| Delay | 0 | 1 ms | 2 ms | 3 ms | 4 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lag of the force (steps) | 0 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 |

Any delay between zero and one step acts as exactly one step, so the cost is flat there. The optimizer, started at 5 ms with a lower bound of 0, ended at the bound, where the Jacobian column of the delay is zero and its interval infinite. With the lower bound at one step, it finds 9.99 ms and the other three parameters to within 0.15%. With `interp="0"` the lag is rounded up to whole steps (1, 1, 2 and 2 steps for 1 to 4 ms), so the cost is a staircase in the delay and a finite-difference gradient is zero almost everywhere; fit delays with interpolation on.

### (3) Contact: a jagged cost

A ball dropped from 0.5 m bounces with a contact softness `solref` = (time constant 0.015 s, damping ratio 0.25) to identify from 1.2 s of height measured with 0.5 mm noise. The height error along each parameter, with the other at its true value:

| Time constant (s) | 0.010 | 0.011 | 0.012 | 0.013 | 0.014 | 0.015 | 0.016 | 0.017 | 0.018 | 0.019 | 0.020 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| height RMS error (mm) | 8.2 | 18.0 | 3.6 | 4.4 | 6.1 | 0.5 | 6.9 | 4.1 | 4.3 | 2.9 | 3.2 |

| Damping ratio | 0.20 | 0.21 | 0.22 | 0.23 | 0.24 | 0.25 | 0.26 | 0.27 | 0.28 | 0.29 | 0.30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| height RMS error (mm) | 23.8 | 23.5 | 21.6 | 14.6 | 9.3 | 0.5 | 3.2 | 4.5 | 7.3 | 10.6 | 14.0 |

The truth is a sharp minimum at the noise level, and along the time constant the cost jumps up and down between neighbouring values. A plausible cause (inference): each bounce ends on a whole step, and a small change in softness moves the step on which the ball leaves the floor, which shifts every later bounce. A gradient taken by finite differences sees only the local wiggle. Fits from five starting points ended at five different places:

| Start | Time constant (s) | Damping ratio | Height RMS error | Highest point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (0.02, 1.0) | 0.0040 | 0.6190 | 13.72 mm | 500 mm |
| (0.02, 0.5) | 0.0103 | 0.2122 | 29.14 mm | 500 mm |
| (0.02, 0.1) | 0.0193 | 0.1035 | 88.23 mm | 500 mm |
| (0.005, 0.25) | 0.0055 | 0.2569 | 333.12 mm | 666 mm |
| (0.04, 0.05) | 0.0585 | 0.0231 | 104.10 mm | 500 mm |

None is near the truth, whose error is 0.50 mm. The first stopped at the 4 ms lower bound, which is there because MuJoCo clamps a time constant below two timesteps: at a damping ratio of 0.25 a time constant of 1 ms and one of 4 ms give the same highest point after the drop, so the cost is flat below 4 ms. That row also shows where the contact stops being physical. The ball, dropped from 500 mm, rises to 4001 mm at 1 and 4 ms, to 1324 mm at 5 ms, and no higher than its drop from 6 ms up: a stiff, lightly damped contact this close to the step size adds energy. The fourth fit stopped in that region, with the ball bouncing to 666 mm. A 40 × 40 grid over both parameters on log scales did better, (0.0127, 0.2687) with a 1.28 mm error, and still missed the basin of the truth.

### Fit what the data constrain

The first two apex heights of the measured bounces, 152.5 and 53.8 mm, are a smooth function of the parameters. Fitting them alone, by a scan and a one-dimensional refinement of the damping ratio with the time constant fixed, gives 0.2473, 0.2514 and 0.2467 for time constants fixed at 0.01, 0.02 and 0.03 s: within 1.3% of the true 0.25 whichever value is fixed. Bounce heights measure the restitution, which these numbers show depends mainly on the damping ratio; the time constant is barely constrained by them. On a held-out drop from 0.3 m:

| Parameters | Apex heights | Height RMS error |
|---|---|---|
| measured | 82.8, 45.2 mm | |
| best raw-trajectory fit (0.0127, 0.2687) | 72.0, 39.9 mm | 5.75 mm |
| apex fit, time constant fixed at 0.01 (0.2473) | 77.8, 37.7 mm | 4.06 mm |
| apex fit, time constant fixed at 0.02 (0.2514) | 84.4, 40.8 mm | 2.18 mm |
| apex fit, time constant fixed at 0.03 (0.2467) | 88.1, 44.5 mm | 5.72 mm |

The apex fits predict the held-out drop at least as well as the best raw fit, with a 1-D search instead of a 2-D one, and the remaining error depends on the time constant they could not identify. Pinning that down takes data that depend on it, such as contact duration or penetration, which needs a faster sensor or a force measurement (inference).

> [!recommendation] A checklist for identification by simulation
> Stamp measurements by the simulator's convention, then check the fit on noise-free simulated data before using real data. Bound every parameter to its physical and numerical range: below one step for a delay and below two steps for a contact time constant the cost is flat, and some ranges add energy. For contact, plot the cost along each parameter before trusting a gradient method; if it is jagged, fit smooth features of the motion, or search on a grid and then refine. Intervals from the Jacobian measure noise only: they were at most 1.5% wide around estimates 18% and 48% wrong. Validate on held-out motion and compare the error with the noise level.

```io
INPUT: pendulum.xml with hidden parameters and the 18.1 record; the same pendulum with a 10 ms actuator delay; a bouncing ball with hidden solref
PROCESS: mujoco.sysid fits with two time stampings; delay as a parameter with two lower bounds; the contact cost along each parameter, five starts, a grid, an apex-height fit; validation on held-out motion
OUTPUT: printed tables
```

The backend prints a line per iteration, and `optimize(verbose=False)` does not silence it, because the backend's own `verbose` flag cannot be passed through `optimize`'s keyword arguments; the script captures standard output and reads the number of simulations from it. In 3.14.0 a joint's `damping` in `MjSpec` is a polynomial (three coefficients, the linear one first), so it is written as `[b, 0, 0]`.

        the same pendulum with a 10 ms actuator delay that the model does not have;

PROCESS (1) mujoco.sysid simulates the record and minimizes the angle error over (mass, damping,

            mujoco.rollout stamps its sensors; next to the 18.1 regression on the same record;
        (2) the delayed pendulum, fitted without a delay and with the delay as a fourth parameter

OUTPUT  printed tables

Run:  python examples/l18_2_sysid_simulation.py          (about 2 minutes)

# requires: sysid

import io
import os
import re

import mujoco
import numpy as np
from mujoco import sysid
from scipy.optimize import minimize_scalar
from scipy.signal import find_peaks, savgol_filter

from mjcourse import model_path

FAST = os.environ.get("MJC_FAST") == "1"
TRUE = {"mass": 1.3, "damping": 0.08, "frictionloss": 0.05}
GUESS = {"mass": 1.0, "damping": 0.0, "frictionloss": 0.0}
L, I_C, G, NOISE, H = 0.5, 1e-4, 9.81, 0.0005, 0.002


def pendulum_spec(p: dict, delay: float | None = None) -> mujoco.MjSpec:
    """pendulum.xml with parameters p; with `delay`, the actuator keeps a 20-sample history (linear)."""
    spec = mujoco.MjSpec.from_file(str(model_path("pendulum")))
    spec.body("pole").mass = p["mass"]
    spec.joint("hinge").damping = [p["damping"], 0.0, 0.0]       # polynomial damping: linear term first
    spec.joint("hinge").frictionloss = p["frictionloss"]
    if delay is not None:
        act = spec.actuator("torque")
        act.nsample, act.interp, act.delay = 20, 1, delay
    return spec


def multisine(t: np.ndarray, amplitude: float, seed: int) -> np.ndarray:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    freqs, phases = rng.uniform(0.2, 3.0, 5), rng.uniform(0, 2 * np.pi, 5)
    return amplitude * sum(np.sin(2 * np.pi * f * t + p) for f, p in zip(freqs, phases)) / 5


def simulate(spec: mujoco.MjSpec, torque: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """Angle at t = k h, measured before step k applies torque[k] (as in Lesson 18.1)."""
    model = spec.compile()
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    q = np.empty(len(torque))
    for k, tau in enumerate(torque):
        q[k] = data.qpos[0]
        data.ctrl[0] = tau
        mujoco.mj_step(model, data)
    return q


def setter(path: str):
    """Modifier writing a scalar parameter into the spec (sysid calls it before each compile)."""
    def apply(spec, p):
        v = float(p.value[0])
        if path == "mass":
            spec.body("pole").mass = v
        elif path == "damping":
            spec.joint("hinge").damping = [v, 0.0, 0.0]
        elif path == "frictionloss":
            spec.joint("hinge").frictionloss = v
        else:
            spec.actuator("torque").delay = v
    return apply


def run_optimize(params, residual_fn) -> tuple[sysid.ParameterDict, object, int]:
    """sysid.optimize, with the backend's per-iteration log captured; returns the residual evaluations."""
    log = io.StringIO()
    with contextlib.redirect_stdout(log):
        opt, result = sysid.optimize(initial_params=params, residual_fn=residual_fn, optimizer="mujoco", verbose=False)
    found = re.search(r"Residual evals: (\d+)", log.getvalue())
    return opt, result, int(found.group(1)) if found else -1


def fit_pendulum(t, torque, q, stamp_shift: float, delay_bounds=None) -> tuple[dict, np.ndarray, int]:
    """Fit (mass, damping, friction loss[, delay]) to a measured angle; returns estimate, 95% half-widths, evals."""
    spec = pendulum_spec(GUESS, None if delay_bounds is None else 0.0)
    model = spec.compile()
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    x0 = sysid.create_initial_state(model, data.qpos, data.qvel, data.act)
    sequences = sysid.ModelSequences(
        "pendulum", spec, "record", x0, sysid.TimeSeries(t, torque[:, None]),
        sysid.TimeSeries.from_names(t + stamp_shift, q[:, None], model, names=["angle"]),
        allow_missing_sensors=True)                                     # the angular-velocity sensor is not measured
    params = sysid.ParameterDict()
    params.add(sysid.Parameter("mass", 1.0, 0.2, 5.0, modifier=setter("mass")))
    params.add(sysid.Parameter("damping", 0.01, 0.0, 1.0, modifier=setter("damping")))
    params.add(sysid.Parameter("frictionloss", 0.01, 0.0, 0.5, modifier=setter("frictionloss")))
    if delay_bounds is not None:
        params.add(sysid.Parameter("delay", 0.005, delay_bounds[0], delay_bounds[1], modifier=setter("delay")))
    residual_fn = sysid.build_residual_fn(models_sequences=[sequences])
    opt, result, evals = run_optimize(params, residual_fn)
    residuals, _, _ = residual_fn(result.x, opt)
    _, half_widths = sysid.calculate_intervals(residuals, result.jac)
    return {k: float(opt[k].value[0]) for k in opt.keys()}, half_widths, evals


def regression_sg(q, torque) -> np.ndarray:
    """The Lesson 18.1 estimate: Savitzky-Golay derivatives and least squares on the inverse dynamics."""
    qf, qd, qdd = (savgol_filter(q, 51, 3, deriv=k, delta=H) for k in range(3))
    sl = slice(30, len(q) - 30)
    x = np.c_[L * L * qdd + G * L * np.sin(qf), qd, np.sign(qd)][sl]
    return np.linalg.lstsq(x, (torque - I_C * qdd)[sl], rcond=None)[0]


def row(label: str, est: dict, hw=None) -> str:
    cells = [f"{est[k]:.5f}" + (f" +- {w:.5f}" if hw is not None else "") for k, w in
             zip(est, hw if hw is not None else [None] * len(est))]
    return f"  {label:<46}" + "".join(f"{c:>21}" for c in cells)


def rms_mrad(a, b) -> float:
    return 1000 * float(np.sqrt(np.mean((a - b) ** 2)))


BALL = """
<mujoco model="bounce">
  <option timestep="0.002"/>
  <worldbody>
    <geom name="floor" type="plane" size="1 1 0.05"/>
    <body name="ball" pos="0 0 0.5">
      <joint name="z" type="slide" axis="0 0 1"/>
      <geom name="ball" type="sphere" size="0.03" mass="0.1"/>
    </body>
  </worldbody>
  <sensor><jointpos name="height" joint="z"/></sensor>
</mujoco>"""
BALL_TRUE = (0.015, 0.25)


def ball_spec(tc: float, dr: float, z0: float = 0.5) -> mujoco.MjSpec:
    spec = mujoco.MjSpec.from_string(BALL)
    for g in ("floor", "ball"):
        spec.geom(g).solref = [tc, dr]
    spec.body("ball").pos = [0, 0, z0]
    return spec


def drop(tc: float, dr: float, z0: float = 0.5, seconds: float = 1.2) -> np.ndarray:
    """Height of the ball's centre (m) at t = k h, before step k."""
    model = ball_spec(tc, dr, z0).compile()
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    z = np.empty(round(seconds / H))
    for k in range(len(z)):
        z[k] = z0 + data.qpos[0]
        mujoco.mj_step(model, data)
    return z


def apexes(z: np.ndarray, count: int) -> np.ndarray:
    """Heights of the first `count` bounce apexes of a smoothed height record (fewer if it stops bouncing)."""
    smooth = savgol_filter(z, 15, 2)
    peaks, _ = find_peaks(smooth, prominence=0.005)
    return smooth[peaks[:count]]


def ball_fit(start, z_meas, t) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """sysid fit of (time constant, damping ratio) to the raw height record from a starting point."""
    spec = ball_spec(*start)
    model = spec.compile()
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    x0 = sysid.create_initial_state(model, data.qpos, data.qvel, data.act)
    sequences = sysid.ModelSequences(
        "ball", spec, "drop", x0, sysid.TimeSeries(t, np.zeros((len(t), 0))),
        sysid.TimeSeries.from_names(t + H, (z_meas - 0.5)[:, None], model, names=["height"]))

    def solref(i):
        def apply(s, p):
            for g in ("floor", "ball"):
                ref = list(s.geom(g).solref)
                ref[i] = float(p.value[0])
                s.geom(g).solref = ref
        return apply
    params = sysid.ParameterDict()
    params.add(sysid.Parameter("timeconst", start[0], 0.004, 0.1, modifier=solref(0)))   # MuJoCo clamps below 2 h
    params.add(sysid.Parameter("dampratio", start[1], 0.01, 2.0, modifier=solref(1)))
    opt, _, _ = run_optimize(params, sysid.build_residual_fn(models_sequences=[sequences]))
    return float(opt["timeconst"].value[0]), float(opt["dampratio"].value[0])

              f"{results[-1][0]:.2f} mm, highest point {1000 * drop(tc, dr).max():.0f} mm")
    n = 10 if FAST else 40
    grid = [(rms_mm(a, b), a, b) for a in np.geomspace(0.004, 0.1, n) for b in np.geomspace(0.02, 2.0, n)]
    g_best = min(grid)
    print(f"  best of a {n} x {n} log grid: time constant {g_best[1]:.4f} s, damping ratio {g_best[2]:.4f}, "

        scan = np.geomspace(0.02, 1.5, 60)

        dr = minimize_scalar(cost, bounds=(scan[max(i - 1, 0)], scan[min(i + 1, 59)]), method="bounded",
                             options={"xatol": 1e-5}).x
        candidates[f"apex fit, time constant fixed at {tc}"] = (tc, float(dr))

          + ", ".join(f"{1000 * a:.1f}" for a in apexes(z_held, 2)) + " mm):")
    for label, (tc, dr) in candidates.items():
        z = drop(tc, dr, z0=0.3)
        print(f"    {label:<40} ({tc:.4f}, {dr:.4f}): apexes "
              + ", ".join(f"{1000 * a:.1f}" for a in apexes(z, 2)) + f" mm, height RMS error {1000 * np.sqrt(np.mean((z - z_held) ** 2)):.2f} mm")

## Debugging

**`mujoco.sysid` has no attribute `optimize`.** The extras are missing: `pip install "mujoco[sysid]==3.14.0"`.

**The fit is good but every estimate is off by a few percent with tight intervals.** A timing convention: compare a fit on simulated data stamped both ways.

**A parameter ends at a bound with an infinite interval.** The cost is flat there: a delay shorter than one step, a held (`interp="0"`) delay, a time constant under two steps. Move the bound or change how the parameter acts.

**Different starts give different answers.** The cost has many local minima. Plot it along each parameter, search coarsely first, or fit features that vary smoothly.

**The identified contact bounces higher than it fell.** The optimizer found a numerically unstable region. Bound the parameters away from it and check energy on validation.
