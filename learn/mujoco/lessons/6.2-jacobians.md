## Core concept

Forward kinematics gives a pose for each configuration. Its derivative gives a velocity for each joint velocity: the **Jacobian** $J(q)$ is the matrix with $v = J(q)\,\dot q$. Each column is the velocity of the point when one joint turns at 1 rad/s and the others are still.

Almost everything in the rest of the course goes through $J$. Inverse kinematics inverts it (Lesson 6.3). Operational-space control turns forces at the tool into joint torques with $J^\top$ (Level 8). Contact constraints are built from the Jacobians of contact points (Level 9). Where $J$ loses rank, at a **singularity**, all of them run into trouble at once.

> [!established] MuJoCo's Jacobians
> Every `mj_jac*` function returns two $3 \times n_v$ matrices: `jacp`, the linear velocity of a point, and `jacr`, the angular velocity of the body it is attached to, both in **world** coordinates, and both multiplying `qvel` (so free and ball joints contribute angular-velocity columns, not quaternion derivatives). The functions differ only in the point: `mj_jac` takes any world point attached to a given body; `mj_jacBody` uses the body frame's origin, `mj_jacBodyCom` its centre of mass, `mj_jacSite` and `mj_jacGeom` a site's or geom's frame origin, and `mj_jacPointAxis` a point plus an axis. `mj_jacSubtreeCom` gives the velocity of a subtree's centre of mass. `mj_jacDot` gives $\dot J$. All of them need `mj_kinematics` and `mj_comPos` to have run for the current `qpos` (Lesson 6.1).

## Visual intuition

The two arrows at the tip are the Jacobian's columns: the tip's velocity for a unit speed of each joint. The dashed ellipse is every tip velocity you can get from joint velocities of unit length, $\{J\dot q : \|\dot q\| = 1\}$. Its axes are the singular values.

1. Bend the elbow to about $-1.5$ rad: the two arrows point in very different directions, and the ellipse is fat. The tip can move about equally well in every direction.
2. Straighten the elbow towards 0. The arrows line up and the ellipse collapses into a line segment. With the arm straight, the tip can only move sideways: no joint velocity moves it towards or away from the shoulder. The table shows $\det J = L_1L_2\sin q_2$ going to zero.
3. Watch the table: the hand-derived Jacobian (and $\det J$ from $L_1L_2\sin q_2$) and MuJoCo's `mj_jacSite` agree to every printed digit.

## Mathematics

### The two-link Jacobian

Differentiating $x = L_1\cos q_1 + L_2\cos(q_1+q_2)$ and $z = L_1\sin q_1 + L_2\sin(q_1+q_2)$,

$$J(q) = \begin{bmatrix} -L_1 s_1 - L_2 s_{12} & -L_2 s_{12} \\ L_1 c_1 + L_2 c_{12} & L_2 c_{12}\end{bmatrix}, \qquad \det J = L_1 L_2 \sin q_2 ,$$

with $s_{12} = \sin(q_1 + q_2)$ and so on. The determinant vanishes at $q_2 = 0$ (arm straight) and $q_2 = \pm\pi$ (folded back, outside `arm2`'s range). The second column is the velocity the elbow joint gives the tip: perpendicular to the second link, with length $L_2$.

### Singular values and what they cost

With $J = U\Sigma V^\top$, a joint velocity along $v_i$ of unit length moves the tip along $u_i$ at speed $\sigma_i$. To move the tip at speed $s$ along the weakest direction $u_{\min}$ takes $\|\dot q\| = s / \sigma_{\min}$. Yoshikawa's manipulability $w = \sqrt{\det JJ^\top} = \prod_i \sigma_i$ is the area of the ellipse up to $\pi$; for the planar arm $w = |\det J| = L_1 L_2 |\sin q_2|$. Output (2) prints all three for an elbow straightening from $-1.5$ rad to 0: at $q_2 = -0.01$ rad, a 0.1 m/s tip motion along the weak direction needs 49 rad/s at the joints, far beyond any motor.

### Moving a Jacobian along a body

Two points $a$ and $b$ fixed to the same body share its angular velocity $\omega$, and their linear velocities differ by $\omega \times (b - a)$. In matrix form, with $[\cdot]_\times$ the cross-product matrix,

$$J_p(b) = J_p(a) - [\,b - a\,]_\times\, J_r, \qquad J_r(b) = J_r(a).$$

This is why one function, `mj_jac`, suffices for any point: the others are convenience wrappers for common points. Output (3) checks each wrapper against `mj_jac` and the shift rule between two points of `link7`.

### World and local frames

`jacr` gives the angular velocity in world coordinates. In the body's own frame it is $R^\top J_r \dot q$, with $R$ the body's (or site's) rotation matrix. `mj_objectVelocity` returns either, with its `flg_local` argument; output (3) shows both agree with the Jacobian.

### The derivative of the Jacobian

Differentiating $v = J\dot q$ once more, $a = J\ddot q + \dot J \dot q$. The second term is the acceleration of the point when the joints move at constant speed, a centripetal and Coriolis-like effect of the geometry. Task-space controllers that track accelerations need it (Level 8); `mj_jacDot` computes it, and output (4) checks it against a finite difference of $J$ along the motion.

```python file=examples/l6_2_jacobians.py
"""Lesson 6.2: Jacobians, velocity kinematics and singularities.

INPUT   arm2.xml (planar two-link arm), arm7.xml (7-DOF arm)
PROCESS (1) the two-link Jacobian derived by hand, from mj_jacSite and from central
            finite differences, over random poses;
        (2) singular values, manipulability and the joint speed needed for a
            0.1 m/s tip motion as the elbow straightens;
        (3) the point each mj_jac* function refers to, the rule that moves a
            Jacobian from one point of a body to another, and world versus local frames;
        (4) mj_jacDot against a finite difference of J along the motion
OUTPUT  printed tables

Run:  python examples/l6_2_jacobians.py
"""

import os

import mujoco
import numpy as np

from mjcourse import kinematics, model_path, spatial

N = 200 if os.environ.get("MJC_FAST") == "1" else 1000
L1, L2 = 0.5, 0.4


def planar_jacobian(q: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """d(x, z)/d(q1, q2) for the two-link arm."""
    s1, c1 = np.sin(q[0]), np.cos(q[0])
    s12, c12 = np.sin(q[0] + q[1]), np.cos(q[0] + q[1])
    return np.array([[-L1 * s1 - L2 * s12, -L2 * s12],
                     [L1 * c1 + L2 * c12, L2 * c12]])


def three_ways() -> None:
    model = mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_path(str(model_path("arm2")))
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
    e_mj = e_fd = 0.0
    for _ in range(N):
        q = rng.uniform(model.jnt_range[:, 0], model.jnt_range[:, 1])
        data.qpos[:] = q
        mujoco.mj_kinematics(model, data)
        mujoco.mj_comPos(model, data)
        jacp, _ = kinematics.site_jacobian(model, data, "ee")
        fdp, _ = kinematics.fd_site_jacobian(model, data, "ee")
        hand = planar_jacobian(q)
        e_mj = max(e_mj, np.abs(jacp[[0, 2]] - hand).max())
        e_fd = max(e_fd, np.abs(fdp[[0, 2]] - hand).max())
    print(f"  {N} random poses: max |mj_jacSite - hand| = {e_mj:.1e}, "
          f"max |finite difference (eps 1e-6) - hand| = {e_fd:.1e} m/rad")


def singular_values() -> None:
    print(f"  {'q2 (rad)':>9}{'sigma1':>9}{'sigma2':>10}{'w = s1 s2':>11}{'L1 L2 |sin q2|':>16}{'cond':>9}{'|qdot| for 0.1 m/s':>21}")
    for q2 in (-1.5, -0.5, -0.1, -0.01, 0.0):
        jac = planar_jacobian(np.array([0.4, q2]))
        u, s, _ = np.linalg.svd(jac)
        speed = np.inf if s[1] < 1e-12 else 0.1 / s[1]          # along the weakest direction u[:, 1]
        cond = np.inf if s[1] < 1e-12 else s[0] / s[1]
        print(f"  {q2:>9.2f}{s[0]:>9.4f}{s[1]:>10.5f}{s[0] * s[1]:>11.5f}{L1 * L2 * abs(np.sin(q2)):>16.5f}"
              f"{cond:>9.1f}{speed:>16.2f} rad/s")


def reference_points() -> None:
    model = mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_path(str(model_path("arm7")))
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    data.qpos[:] = [0.3, 0.6, -0.4, 1.4, 0.2, 0.9, 0.1]
    data.qvel[:] = np.random.default_rng(2).normal(size=model.nv)
    mujoco.mj_forward(model, data)
    nv, body = model.nv, model.body("link7").id
    site, geom = model.site("ee").id, model.geom("flange").id

    def jac_at(point):
        p, r = np.zeros((3, nv)), np.zeros((3, nv))
        mujoco.mj_jac(model, data, p, r, point, body)
        return p, r

    cases = {
        "mj_jacBody      -> xpos (body frame origin)": (mujoco.mj_jacBody, body, data.xpos[body]),
        "mj_jacBodyCom   -> xipos (body centre of mass)": (mujoco.mj_jacBodyCom, body, data.xipos[body]),
        "mj_jacSite      -> site_xpos": (mujoco.mj_jacSite, site, data.site_xpos[site]),
        "mj_jacGeom      -> geom_xpos": (mujoco.mj_jacGeom, geom, data.geom_xpos[geom]),
    }
    for name, (fn, idx, point) in cases.items():
        p, r = np.zeros((3, nv)), np.zeros((3, nv))
        fn(model, data, p, r, idx)
        ref_p, ref_r = jac_at(point)
        print(f"  {name:<46} equals mj_jac at that point: {np.abs(p - ref_p).max():.0e}, rotation part {np.abs(r - ref_r).max():.0e}")

    a, b = data.xpos[body], data.site_xpos[site]
    pa, ra = jac_at(a)
    pb, _ = jac_at(b)
    shifted = pa - spatial.hat(b - a) @ ra                     # v_b = v_a + w x (b - a)
    print(f"  shift rule J_p(b) = J_p(a) - [b - a]x J_r, link7 origin -> ee site: max error {np.abs(shifted - pb).max():.0e}")

    sub = np.zeros((3, nv))
    mujoco.mj_jacSubtreeCom(model, data, sub, model.body("link4").id)
    mujoco.mj_subtreeVel(model, data)
    print(f"  mj_jacSubtreeCom(link4) @ qvel = {np.round(sub @ data.qvel, 5)}, subtree_linvel after mj_subtreeVel = "
          f"{np.round(data.subtree_linvel[model.body('link4').id], 5)} m/s")

    _, r = np.zeros((3, nv)), np.zeros((3, nv))
    mujoco.mj_jacSite(model, data, None, r, site)
    w_world = r @ data.qvel
    vel = np.zeros(6)
    mujoco.mj_objectVelocity(model, data, mujoco.mjtObj.mjOBJ_SITE, site, vel, 0)
    vel_local = np.zeros(6)
    mujoco.mj_objectVelocity(model, data, mujoco.mjtObj.mjOBJ_SITE, site, vel_local, 1)
    rot = data.site_xmat[site].reshape(3, 3)
    print(f"  angular velocity: J_r qvel (world) vs mj_objectVelocity world: {np.abs(w_world - vel[:3]).max():.0e}; "
          f"R^T J_r qvel vs local: {np.abs(rot.T @ w_world - vel_local[:3]).max():.0e} rad/s")


def jac_dot() -> None:
    model = mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_path(str(model_path("arm7")))
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    data.qpos[:] = [0.3, 0.6, -0.4, 1.4, 0.2, 0.9, 0.1]
    data.qvel[:] = np.random.default_rng(3).normal(size=model.nv)
    mujoco.mj_forward(model, data)
    site, nv = model.site("ee").id, model.nv
    dp, dr = np.zeros((3, nv)), np.zeros((3, nv))
    mujoco.mj_jacDot(model, data, dp, dr, data.site_xpos[site], model.site_bodyid[site])

    def site_jac(qpos):
        d = mujoco.MjData(model)
        d.qpos[:] = qpos
        mujoco.mj_kinematics(model, d)
        mujoco.mj_comPos(model, d)
        return kinematics.site_jacobian(model, d, site)

    h = 1e-6
    q_plus, q_minus = data.qpos.copy(), data.qpos.copy()
    mujoco.mj_integratePos(model, q_plus, data.qvel, h)
    mujoco.mj_integratePos(model, q_minus, data.qvel, -h)
    (pp, rp), (pm, rm) = site_jac(q_plus), site_jac(q_minus)
    print(f"  max |mj_jacDot - (J(q + h qdot) - J(q - h qdot)) / 2h|: translation {np.abs(dp - (pp - pm) / (2 * h)).max():.1e}, "
          f"rotation {np.abs(dr - (rp - rm) / (2 * h)).max():.1e}")
    print(f"  the velocity-product term Jdot qdot at the ee site: {np.round(dp @ data.qvel, 4)} m/s^2")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    print("(1) the two-link Jacobian three ways")
    three_ways()
    print("(2) singular values as the elbow straightens (q1 = 0.4 rad)")
    singular_values()
    print("(3) reference points and frames of MuJoCo's Jacobians (arm7, link7)")
    reference_points()
    print("(4) the time derivative of the Jacobian")
    jac_dot()
```

Reading the output:

- **(1)** MuJoCo's Jacobian is analytic: it matches the hand derivation to $10^{-16}$. The finite-difference one matches to $10^{-10}$, the expected truncation and rounding error of a central difference with step $10^{-6}$. Use finite differences to test a Jacobian, never to replace one.
- **(2)** $\sigma_1 \sigma_2$ equals $L_1 L_2|\sin q_2|$ in every row. $\sigma_1$ barely changes while $\sigma_2$ falls in proportion to $q_2$: the singularity removes one direction and leaves the other intact.
- **(3)** Each wrapper is `mj_jac` at the point named in its row, exactly (zero difference, the same arithmetic); the shift rule holds to $10^{-16}$; `mj_jacSubtreeCom` times `qvel` equals `subtree_linvel` after `mj_subtreeVel`; and the angular velocity agrees with `mj_objectVelocity` in both frames.
- **(4)** `mj_jacDot` agrees with the finite difference to $10^{-10}$. For this random joint velocity, $\dot J\dot q$ at the tool is several m/s², the same order as gravity: not a correction to ignore in fast motions.

## Debugging

**IK or control blows up near one pose.** Print the singular values of $J$ there. If $\sigma_{\min}$ is near zero, any method that inverts $J$ demands huge joint speeds; damping (Lesson 6.3) is the standard fix.

**The tool's angular velocity is "wrong by a rotation".** `jacr` is in world coordinates. If you compare with a gyroscope or a body-frame velocity, rotate it: $R^\top J_r \dot q$.

**A Jacobian check against finite differences fails at $10^{-6}$.** The step is too large or too small for the problem's scale, or the perturbation was applied to `qpos` by addition for a free or ball joint. Perturb with `mj_integratePos`, as `kinematics.fd_site_jacobian` does, and use central differences.

**A free-floating object's Jacobian has fewer columns than its `qpos` has entries.** Correctly so: Jacobians multiply `qvel` ($n_v$ entries), not `qpos` ($n_q$). A free joint has 7 position and 6 velocity coordinates.

> [!research] Learning in the null space and away from singularities
> Policies that output end-effector velocities rely on the controller to invert $J$, so their behaviour near singular poses is the controller's, not the policy's (Lesson 5.2). Manipulability and its gradient appear in learning as auxiliary rewards and as constraints in motion generation; this course's position is that such terms are design choices to be tested, not defaults, since $w$ depends on the units of the task and on which rows of $J$ are included (inference from the scaling exercise above).
