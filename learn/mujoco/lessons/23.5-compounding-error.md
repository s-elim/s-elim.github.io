## Core concept

A learned dynamics model is trained to predict one step. A planner uses it for many: each candidate plan is rolled out by feeding the model its own predictions, so the error of step $k$ becomes part of the input of step $k+1$. That is compounding error, and it is the standard explanation for why model-based control fails. This lesson tests the explanation instead of assuming it. It trains three models with the same network and the same training, changing only the data, and measures four things separately: the one-step error, the open-loop error over 1 to 50 steps, the success of MPC as the planning horizon grows, and how far the plans the planner chooses fall short of what the model promised.

The result has two parts, and the second is the less familiar one. A modest difference in one-step error goes with the difference between solving every task and solving almost none. Yet for the good model, the planning horizon did not matter at all: errors of 2 cm at the end of a 2 s plan did not cost a single task. Compounding error is real and measurable, but whether it reaches the controller depends on how the model is used.

## Mathematics

### Why errors compound

Let the true dynamics be $s_{t+1} = f(s_t, a_t)$ and the model $\hat f$, with one-step error $\|\hat f(s,a) - f(s,a)\| \le \varepsilon$ on the states that matter, and let $\hat f$ be Lipschitz in the state with constant $L$. The error $e_k$ of a $k$-step open-loop prediction then obeys

$$e_{k+1} = \|\hat f(\hat s_k, a_k) - f(s_k, a_k)\| \le \|\hat f(\hat s_k, a_k) - \hat f(s_k, a_k)\| + \|\hat f(s_k, a_k) - f(s_k, a_k)\| \le L\,e_k + \varepsilon,$$

so $e_k \le \varepsilon\,(L^k - 1)/(L - 1)$: linear growth if $L = 1$, geometric if $L > 1$.

> [!derivation] What the bound does and does not say
> The bound follows from the triangle inequality and the two assumptions. It assumes the one-step error holds on the predicted states $\hat s_k$, which drift away from the data the model was trained on; that is exactly where $\varepsilon$ is not controlled. The measured curve (below) grows faster than linearly beyond 10 steps, consistent with the error leaving the training distribution as it grows, but the bound alone cannot tell the two causes apart.

### Three models, the same network

The three models share the MLP of Lesson 23.2 (3 hidden layers of 256, SiLU, predicting a normalized state change) and its training (20 000 steps of the one-step objective). Only the data differ:

| Model | Training data |
|---|---|
| guided | 1000 episodes of the demonstrator plus noise, with 20% random actions (the model of Lesson 23.2) |
| small | 50 episodes of the same policy |
| random | 1000 episodes of uniformly random actions |

Output (1), the one-step error of the next positions (pusher and three pucks), on held-out data of both kinds:

| Training data | One-step error, random-action test (mm) | One-step error, guided test (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| guided | 0.55 | 0.66 |
| small | 1.15 | 1.58 |
| random | 0.77 | 1.65 |

On random-action data, the random model is 0.22 mm worse than the guided one. Measured on guided data, all three are below 2 mm.

Output (2), the open-loop error of the pushed puck on held-out guided episodes:

| Training data | 1 step | 5 | 10 | 20 | 50 steps (0.1 s each) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| guided | 1.0 mm | 3.8 | 8.1 | 20.6 | 76.7 |
| small | 2.7 | 15.1 | 30.6 | 60.7 | 131.3 |
| random | 2.2 | 14.1 | 30.7 | 66.1 | 179.0 |

For the guided model, the local slope of $\log e$ against $\log k$ is 0.8, 1.1, 1.3 and 1.4 over the four intervals of this table (computed from it): the growth is faster than linear and accelerating, as the derivation allows.

### Success against the planning horizon

Output (3), the planner of Lesson 23.4 (CEM, 64 samples, 3 iterations, replanning every 0.1 s) with planning horizons of 0.4 to 2 s, 30 held-out tasks per cell:

| Model | $H = 4$ | $H = 8$ | $H = 12$ | $H = 20$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MuJoCo | 1.00 [0.89, 1.00] | 1.00 [0.89, 1.00] | 1.00 [0.89, 1.00] | 1.00 [0.89, 1.00] |
| guided | 1.00 [0.89, 1.00] | 1.00 [0.89, 1.00] | 1.00 [0.89, 1.00] | 1.00 [0.89, 1.00] |
| small | 0.03 [0.01, 0.17] | 0.13 [0.05, 0.30] | 0.07 [0.02, 0.21] | 0.00 [0.00, 0.11] |
| random | 0.10 [0.03, 0.26] | 0.23 [0.12, 0.41] | 0.10 [0.03, 0.26] | 0.10 [0.03, 0.26] |

Two facts, and they point in different directions.

First, one-step error is a poor predictor of control. The random model's one-step error on the action distribution it was trained on is 0.77 mm against the guided model's 0.55; its success is 0.10 against 1.00. Even on the right test distribution (guided episodes), a factor of 2.5 in one-step error (1.65 against 0.66 mm) separates every task solved from nine in ten failed. This is the objective mismatch named by Lambert et al. (arXiv:2002.04523): a model trained for one-step likelihood is evaluated on control, and the two need not agree.

Second, longer horizons did not hurt the guided model. At $H = 20$ its own prediction of the pushed puck is off by about 2 cm at the end of the plan, and it still solves all 30 tasks. Two mechanisms are at work. The planner applies only the first action of each plan and replans from the measured state (Lesson 23.4), so late-plan errors only matter through their effect on which first action wins. And candidates that share a start share most of their error: what decides the choice is the error in the differences between candidates, which is smaller than the error in any one of them (inference: consistent with the rank correlations of Lesson 23.4, not measured here as a separate quantity).

> [!research] Hypothesis, falsifier, outcome
> Hypothesis: on this task, MPC success with a learned model is determined by its one-step error. Falsifier: two models whose one-step errors on the evaluation distribution differ by less than a factor of 3 but whose success rates differ by more than 0.5. Outcome: falsified, twice (random against guided, small against guided). A defensible replacement: success is determined by whether the model predicts how the puck responds to the pushes the planner will try, which output (5) supports and output (4) begins to measure.

### Does the planner exploit the model?

A planner chooses the plan the model scores best. If the model is wrong in some direction, the search finds the plans that profit from that error, and the chosen plan does worse in reality than the model promised. Output (4) measures this at every decision of 10 learned-model episodes (horizon 12): how far the chosen plan is predicted to move the target puck toward its zone, and how far the same plan moves it in MuJoCo from the same state.

| Model | Decisions | Predicted progress (mm) | Progress in MuJoCo (mm) | Over-promised by more than 10 mm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| guided | 642 | 26.7 | 23.7 | 0.08 |
| small | 1200 | 4.2 | 2.9 | 0.14 |
| random | 1200 | 6.6 | −0.5 | 0.24 |

All three models over-promise on average, the signature of optimizing against a noisy score. The guided model's plans deliver 89% of the progress it predicts. The random model's plans are predicted to make progress and, in MuJoCo, move the puck slightly the wrong way on average; one decision in four is over-promised by more than a centimetre.

### What the failures look like

Output (5), at $H = 12$:

| Model | Success | Pusher reached the target puck | Mean progress of the puck toward its zone (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| MuJoCo | 1.00 | 1.00 | 225.9 |
| guided | 1.00 | 1.00 | 223.2 |
| small | 0.07 | 0.90 | 25.6 |
| random | 0.10 | 0.97 | 52.6 |

The weak models reach the puck in 90% and 97% of episodes. They fail after that, in contact: they move the puck a tenth to a quarter of the distance the good planners do. The pusher's own motion is easy to learn from any data (velocity commands, no contact); the puck's response to a push is what the guided data cover and random actions rarely produce.

> [!recommendation] Evaluating a world model for control
> Report the one-step error on the distribution the planner will visit, the open-loop error over several horizons, and closed-loop success with the simulator as reference, and keep them separate: none of them predicts the others reliably. Add one measurement of exploitation (promised against delivered progress) and one of where failures occur. A single number for model quality hides all of this.

```python file=examples/l23_5_compounding_error.py
import multiprocessing
import os
from concurrent.futures import ProcessPoolExecutor
from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np
import torch

from mjcourse import stats
from mjcourse import tabletop as tt
from mjcourse.worldmodel import dynamics, mpc, standard

OUT = Path(__file__).resolve().parents[1] / "runs" / "l23_5"
FAST = os.environ.get("MJC_FAST") == "1"
MODELS = {"guided": "one_step", "small": "small_data", "random": "random_data"}
PLAN_HORIZONS = (4, 8, 12, 20)
TASKS = 6 if FAST else 30
PRED_HORIZONS = (1, 5, 10, 20, 50)


def task_and_layout(i: int):
    rng = np.random.default_rng([5, i])
    task = (tt.OBJECTS[rng.integers(3)], list(tt.ZONES)[rng.integers(4)])
    return task, tt.sample_layout(rng, task)


def control(args) -> dict:
    name, horizon, i = args
    torch.set_num_threads(1)
    task, layout = task_and_layout(i)
    cfg = mpc.PlannerConfig(horizon=horizon)
    if name == "MuJoCo":
        factory = lambda scene: mpc.MujocoModel(scene)
    else:
        model = standard.model(MODELS[name])
        factory = lambda scene: mpc.LearnedModel(model)
    r = mpc.run_episode(factory, task, layout, cfg=cfg)
    i_t = 4 + 4 * tt.OBJECTS.index(task[0])
    states, zone = r["states"], np.array(tt.ZONES[task[1]])
    gap = np.linalg.norm(states[:, 0:2] - states[:, i_t:i_t + 2], axis=1).min() - tt.PUCK_RADIUS - tt.PUSHER_RADIUS
    progress = np.linalg.norm(states[0, i_t:i_t + 2] - zone) - np.linalg.norm(states[-1, i_t:i_t + 2] - zone)
    return {"model": name, "horizon": horizon, "i": i, "success": bool(r["success"]), "touched": bool(gap < 0.003),
            "progress": float(progress)}


def exploitation(args) -> list[tuple[float, float]]:
    name, i = args
    torch.set_num_threads(1)
    task, layout = task_and_layout(i)
    scene = tt.Tabletop()
    scene.reset(layout)
    planner = mpc.Planner(mpc.LearnedModel(standard.model(MODELS[name])), task, mpc.PlannerConfig(horizon=12))
    truth = mpc.MujocoModel(scene)
    i_t, zone = 4 + 4 * tt.OBJECTS.index(task[0]), np.array(tt.ZONES[task[1]])
    pairs = []
    for _ in range(tt.EPISODE_STEPS):
        state = scene.state()
        a = planner.act(state)
        best = planner.last["best"]
        chosen = planner.last["actions"][best:best + 1]
        start = np.linalg.norm(state[i_t:i_t + 2] - zone)
        predicted = start - np.linalg.norm(planner.last["states"][best, -1, i_t:i_t + 2] - zone)
        true = start - np.linalg.norm(truth.predict(state, chosen)[0, -1, i_t:i_t + 2] - zone)
        pairs.append((float(predicted), float(true)))
        scene.step(a)
        if scene.success(task):
            break
    scene.close()
    return pairs


def one_step_error(model, episodes) -> float:
    s = np.concatenate([e["states"][:-1] for e in episodes])
    a = np.concatenate([e["actions"] for e in episodes])
    s2 = np.concatenate([e["states"][1:] for e in episodes])
    with torch.no_grad():
        p = model(torch.as_tensor(s), torch.as_tensor(a)).numpy()
    idx = [0, 1, 4, 5, 8, 9, 12, 13]
    return float(np.linalg.norm((p - s2)[:, idx].reshape(-1, 4, 2), axis=-1).mean())


def figure(errors: dict, success: dict) -> None:
    import matplotlib
    matplotlib.use("Agg")
    import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

    fig, (ax1, ax2) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9.5, 3.8), constrained_layout=True)
    colours = {"guided": "#0072B2", "small": "#E69F00", "random": "#D55E00"}
    for name, err in errors.items():
        ax1.plot(PRED_HORIZONS, [1000 * err[h]["target"] for h in PRED_HORIZONS], marker="o", color=colours[name], label=name)
    ax1.set_xscale("log")
    ax1.set_yscale("log")
    ax1.set_xlabel("prediction horizon (steps of 0.1 s)")
    ax1.set_ylabel("open-loop error of the pushed puck (mm)")
    ax1.grid(alpha=0.3, which="both")
    ax1.legend(frameon=False)
    ax1.set_title("prediction error on held-out data", fontsize=10)
    for name in ("MuJoCo", "guided", "small", "random"):
        y = [success[(name, h)] for h in PLAN_HORIZONS]
        kw = {"color": "#000000", "marker": "s", "mfc": "none"} if name == "MuJoCo" else {"color": colours[name], "marker": "o"}
        ax2.plot([h * 0.1 for h in PLAN_HORIZONS], y, **kw, label=name)
    ax2.set_xlabel("planning horizon (s)")
    ax2.set_ylabel("MPC success rate")
    ax2.set_ylim(-0.05, 1.05)
    ax2.grid(alpha=0.3)
    ax2.legend(frameon=False)
    ax2.set_title("control success against planning horizon", fontsize=10)
    fig.savefig(OUT / "horizons.png", dpi=150)
    plt.close(fig)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    OUT.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)
    models = {k: standard.model(v) for k, v in MODELS.items()}
    guided_test, random_test = standard.dataset("guided_test"), standard.dataset("random_test")
    print("(1) one-step error on held-out transitions (mm)")
    print(f"  {'model':<12}{'random test':>16}{'guided test':>16}")
    for name, m in models.items():
        print(f"  {name:<12}{1000 * one_step_error(m, random_test):>16.2f}{1000 * one_step_error(m, guided_test):>16.2f}")
    pred_errors = {name: dynamics.horizon_errors(m, guided_test, PRED_HORIZONS) for name, m in models.items()}
    print(f"(2) open-loop error of the pushed puck against horizon (mm)")
    print(f"  {'model':<12}" + "".join(f"{h:>9d}" for h in PRED_HORIZONS) + "   (steps of 0.1 s)")
    for name, err in pred_errors.items():
        print(f"  {name:<12}" + "".join(f"{1000 * err[h]['target']:9.1f}" for h in PRED_HORIZONS))
    with ProcessPoolExecutor(min(32, os.cpu_count() or 1), mp_context=multiprocessing.get_context("spawn")) as pool:
        runs = list(pool.map(control, [(m, h, i) for m in ("MuJoCo", "guided", "small", "random") for h in PLAN_HORIZONS
                                       for i in range(TASKS)]))
        expl = {name: list(pool.map(exploitation, [(name, i) for i in range(TASKS)])) for name in MODELS}
    print(f"(3) MPC success against planning horizon, {TASKS} tasks per cell [95% Wilson interval]")
    print(f"  {'model':<12}" + "".join(f"{f'H = {h * 0.1:.1f} s':>22}" for h in PLAN_HORIZONS))
    success_by_cell = {}
    for name in ("MuJoCo", "guided", "small", "random"):
        cells = []
        for h in PLAN_HORIZONS:
            k = sum(r["success"] for r in runs if r["model"] == name and r["horizon"] == h)
            lo, hi = stats.wilson_interval(k, TASKS)
            cells.append(f"{k / TASKS:.2f} [{lo:.2f}, {hi:.2f}]")
            success_by_cell[(name, h)] = k / TASKS
        print(f"  {name:<12}" + "".join(f"{c:>22}" for c in cells))
    print(f"(4) model exploitation at planning horizon 12: predicted against true progress (m) per decision")
    print(f"  {'model':<12}{'decisions':>12}{'mean predicted':>16}{'mean true':>14}{'true < predicted':>18}")
    for name in MODELS:
        flat = [p for ep in expl[name] for p in ep]
        pred, true = np.array([p[0] for p in flat]), np.array([p[1] for p in flat])
        print(f"  {name:<12}{len(flat):12d}{pred.mean():>16.3f}{true.mean():>14.3f}{(true < pred).mean():>18.1%}")
    print(f"(5) failures at planning horizon 12: {TASKS} tasks")
    print(f"  {'model':<12}{'never reached puck':>20}{'mean progress (m)':>20}")
    for name in MODELS:
        h12 = [r for r in runs if r["model"] == name and r["horizon"] == 12]
        print(f"  {name:<12}{sum(not r['touched'] for r in h12):>20d}{np.mean([r['progress'] for r in h12]):>20.3f}")
    figure(pred_errors, success_by_cell)
    print(f"wrote {OUT / 'horizons.png'}")
```

## Debugging

**An exploitation measure that shows nothing.** The first version of output (4) compared the predicted and true cost of the chosen plan and found ratios of 1.00 to 1.01 for every model, including the random one. The cost is dominated by the puck's distance to its zone, which hardly changes within 1.2 s, so any model scores it well. The progress of the puck over the plan is the part of the prediction the choice depends on; measured that way, the difference between models appears.

**Comparing one-step errors on the wrong data.** On random-action test data the random model looks almost as good as the guided one (0.77 against 0.55 mm). Always evaluate a model for planning on the state and action distribution the planner will produce; here, the guided test set is the closer proxy.

**A flat success curve read as "horizon does not matter".** It does not matter for this task, this cost (which tells the planner where to push from) and this replanning rate. With a cost that rewards only the final puck position, short horizons would see no reward at all; with open-loop execution, late-plan errors are acted on. Lesson 23.6 removes the replanning and measures the difference.

## Research connection

> [!research] Compounding error in model-based learning
> Talvitie (arXiv:1612.06018) shows that a model's ability to correct its own errors is more tightly related to model-based performance than its one-step error, and trains models on their own predictions to that end; the multi-step objective of the exercise is in the same family. MBPO (Janner et al., arXiv:1906.08253) keeps model rollouts short and branched from real states for the same reason: long imagined rollouts accumulate error. PETS (Chua et al., arXiv:1805.12114) plans with an ensemble and propagates its uncertainty, one answer to exploitation. The experiments here are on one task with one network family; how far their conclusions transfer to latent models of images (Lesson 23.3) is an open question for the reader's own project (Lesson 23.7).
