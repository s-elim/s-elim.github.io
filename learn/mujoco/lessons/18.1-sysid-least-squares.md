## Core concept

System identification estimates a model's parameters from measured motion. For a rigid-body system the inverse dynamics are linear in the inertial parameters and in viscous and Coulomb friction coefficients, so given positions, velocities, accelerations and torques, the parameters follow from ordinary least squares. The difficulty is everything around that one line of algebra: velocities and accelerations are not measured but differentiated from noisy positions; the friction model in the regression may not be the friction in the system; and the data must actually excite each parameter. This lesson identifies the mass, damping and friction loss of a pendulum whose parameters are hidden from the estimator, and measures what goes wrong at each of those three points.

> [!established] The model being identified
> The pendulum of `pendulum.xml` is a point mass $m$ at $L$ = 0.5 m with a small rotor inertia $I_c = 10^{-4}$ kg m², a joint damping $b$ and a joint friction loss $f$. With torque $\tau$, $(mL^2 + I_c)\ddot q + b\dot q + mgL\sin q + \text{friction loss} = \tau$. MuJoCo implements friction loss as a constraint with a bounded force $f$ (its documentation calls it dry friction), softened like every MuJoCo constraint, so near zero velocity it is not exactly $f\,\text{sign}(\dot q)$.

## Visual intuition

**New hidden pendulum** draws a mass, damping and friction loss you cannot see; **Record 10 s** drives it with a multisine torque and measures its angle with noise; **Fit** estimates the parameters and validates them on a different excitation, plotting the measured angle against the estimated model's prediction.

1. Fit with the defaults (0.5 mrad noise, Savitzky-Golay derivatives): the prediction follows the measured angle for the whole 10 s.
2. Switch to finite differences and fit again: the mass estimate collapses toward zero and the prediction is useless. Set the noise to 0: finite differences work again.
3. Set the excitation to 0.25 N m and record: the pendulum barely moves, and the estimate is wrong even with filtered derivatives.
4. With full excitation, compare fits with and without the slow samples, then **Reveal** the hidden values.

## Mathematics

### A linear regression

Move the known terms to the left and the unknowns to the right:

$$\tau - I_c\ddot q = m\,(L^2\ddot q + gL\sin q) + b\,\dot q + f\,\text{sign}(\dot q) = \phi(q, \dot q, \ddot q)^\top \theta,\qquad \theta = (m, b, f).$$

Stacking 5 000 samples gives $Y = \Phi\theta + \epsilon$ and $\hat\theta = (\Phi^\top\Phi)^{-1}\Phi^\top Y$. The hidden values are $m$ = 1.3 kg, $b$ = 0.08 N m s/rad, $f$ = 0.05 N m; the torque is a sum of five sines (peak 2.5 N m) for 10 s; the angle is measured with Gaussian noise of 0.5 mrad.

### Derivatives decide the answer

| Derivatives | $m$ | $b$ | $f$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| finite differences of the noisy angle | 0.0007 | 0.1006 | 0.0514 |
| Savitzky-Golay (cubic fits over 0.1 s) | 1.3266 | 0.0868 | 0.0417 |
| finite differences of the noise-free angle | 1.2999 | 0.0835 | 0.0460 |

Differencing twice at a 2 ms step multiplies the angle noise by about $\sqrt 6/h^2 \approx 6\times 10^5$ s⁻², so 0.5 mrad of noise becomes roughly 300 rad/s² of noise in $\ddot q$, far larger than the true accelerations. The regressor of $m$ is then mostly noise, and least squares, which assumes the regressors are exact, shrinks its coefficient toward zero: the errors-in-variables bias. The mass estimate is 0.0007 kg. A Savitzky-Golay filter fits a cubic to each 0.1 s window and differentiates the fit, which recovers the mass within 2%.

### Uncertainty that is honest, and bias that is not covered

The least-squares standard errors assume independent residuals. Filtered derivatives make neighbouring residuals strongly correlated (autocorrelation 0.906 at one sample), and a block bootstrap that resamples contiguous stretches of the record gives standard errors about twice as large (0.0028 against 0.0016 kg for the mass, 0.0038 against 0.0017 for the damping). Even the bootstrap interval misses the truth for damping and friction loss by about two standard errors. That is not noise but model error: refitting without the 9% of samples slower than 0.1 rad/s gives 1.3238, 0.0797 and 0.0501, essentially the hidden values. Near zero velocity MuJoCo's friction loss is a soft constraint, not $f\,\text{sign}(\dot q)$, and those samples pulled damping and friction loss toward each other. No standard error accounts for a wrong model; only a check of the residuals and of the assumptions does.

### Excitation

With a tenth of the torque (0.25 N m), the pendulum never moves faster than 0.10 rad/s, and the estimate is $m$ = 0.61, $b$ = 0.75, $f$ = 0.017: wrong by factors of 2 to 9, with standard errors that claim a precision of a few percent. The condition number of the column-normalized regressor is 4 for both excitations, so it did not warn. Every sample is in the regime where the friction model is wrong and the acceleration signal is small next to its noise. Excitation design is part of the identification: the trajectory must move every parameter's regressor well above its noise, and away from regimes the model does not describe.

### Validation

A held-out trajectory, driven by a different multisine and measured with noise, is simulated open loop with each estimate:

| Parameters | Angle RMS error over 10 s | Over the first second |
|---|---|---|
| Savitzky-Golay estimate | 2.57 mrad | 1.12 mrad |
| the same, without slow samples | 2.53 mrad | 1.11 mrad |
| finite-difference estimate | 907 mrad | 949 mrad |
| a nominal guess (1.0 kg, no damping or friction) | 129 mrad | 24.4 mrad |

The two good estimates differ by 8% in damping and 17% in friction loss and predict the held-out motion equally well: this trajectory constrains a combination of the two more than each one. Validation can reject a bad model; agreement on one trajectory does not prove every parameter right.

> [!recommendation] An identification checklist
> Filter before differentiating, and check the filter on noise-free simulated data. Excite every parameter, and check the speeds and accelerations the data actually reach. Report block-bootstrap intervals, then look at the residuals against velocity for model error. Validate on a trajectory not used for fitting, and for parameters that trade off, test on a trajectory that separates them.

```python file=examples/l18_1_sysid_least_squares.py
"""Lesson 18.1: identifying a pendulum's mass, damping and friction loss by least squares (Level 18 checkpoint).

INPUT   pendulum.xml with hidden parameters: mass 1.3 kg, joint damping 0.08 N m s/rad,
        friction loss 0.05 N m; the angle measured with Gaussian noise (0.5 mrad), the
        torque known (it is the command)
PROCESS the inverse dynamics tau = m (L^2 qdd + g L sin q) + I_c qdd + b qd + f sign(qd) are
        linear in (m, b, f) given q, qd, qdd. From 10 s of multisine excitation:
        (1) qd and qdd by finite differences of the noisy angle, and by a Savitzky-Golay
            filter; least-squares estimates from each;
        (2) uncertainty: the ordinary least-squares standard errors against a block bootstrap;
        (3) identifiability: a weak excitation (a tenth of the amplitude), the condition
            number of the regressor, how much of the record is slow, and a fit that leaves out
            samples slower than 0.1 rad/s, where the friction model is wrong;
        (4) validation: a held-out trajectory with a different excitation, simulated open loop
            with the true and with the estimated parameters
OUTPUT  printed tables

Run:  python examples/l18_1_sysid_least_squares.py
"""

import mujoco
import numpy as np
from scipy.signal import savgol_filter

from mjcourse import model_path

TRUE = {"mass": 1.3, "damping": 0.08, "frictionloss": 0.05}
L, I_C, G = 0.5, 1e-4, 9.81
NOISE = 0.0005                                       # angle noise (rad)


def pendulum(params: dict) -> tuple[mujoco.MjModel, mujoco.MjData]:
    model = mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_path(str(model_path("pendulum")))
    body, joint = model.body("pole").id, model.joint("hinge").id
    model.body_mass[body] = params["mass"]
    model.dof_damping[joint] = params["damping"]
    model.dof_frictionloss[joint] = params["frictionloss"]
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    mujoco.mj_setConst(model, data)                  # derived constants after editing the mass (Lesson 17.1)
    mujoco.mj_resetData(model, data)
    return model, data


def multisine(t: np.ndarray, amplitude: float, seed: int) -> np.ndarray:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    freqs, phases = rng.uniform(0.2, 3.0, 5), rng.uniform(0, 2 * np.pi, 5)
    return amplitude * sum(np.sin(2 * np.pi * f * t + p) for f, p in zip(freqs, phases)) / 5


def simulate(params: dict, torque: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    model, data = pendulum(params)
    q = np.empty(len(torque))
    for k, tau in enumerate(torque):
        q[k] = data.qpos[0]
        data.ctrl[0] = tau
        mujoco.mj_step(model, data)
    return q


def regressor(q, qd, qdd) -> np.ndarray:
    """Columns multiply (m, b, f); the known rotor inertia term goes to the left-hand side."""
    return np.c_[L * L * qdd + G * L * np.sin(q), qd, np.sign(qd)]


def derivatives(q: np.ndarray, h: float, method: str) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
    if method == "finite differences":
        qd = np.gradient(q, h)
        return q, qd, np.gradient(qd, h)
    window = 51                                       # 0.1 s at 2 ms, cubic fits
    return (savgol_filter(q, window, 3), savgol_filter(q, window, 3, deriv=1, delta=h),
            savgol_filter(q, window, 3, deriv=2, delta=h))


def fit(q, qd, qdd, tau, trim: int = 30, min_speed: float = 0.0) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
    """Least squares on the samples away from the ends (and, optionally, faster than min_speed);
    returns (estimate, standard errors, residuals)."""
    sl = np.arange(trim, len(q) - trim)
    sl = sl[np.abs(qd[sl]) >= min_speed]
    x, y = regressor(q[sl], qd[sl], qdd[sl]), tau[sl] - I_C * qdd[sl]
    theta, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(x, y, rcond=None)
    resid = y - x @ theta
    cov = resid.var(ddof=3) * np.linalg.inv(x.T @ x)
    return theta, np.sqrt(np.diag(cov)), resid


def block_bootstrap(q, qd, qdd, tau, blocks: int = 20, n: int = 500, seed: int = 0) -> np.ndarray:
    """Refit on resampled contiguous blocks of the record: errors that are correlated in time stay together."""
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    idx = np.array_split(np.arange(30, len(q) - 30), blocks)
    estimates = []
    for _ in range(n):
        pick = np.concatenate([idx[i] for i in rng.integers(0, blocks, blocks)])
        x, y = regressor(q[pick], qd[pick], qdd[pick]), tau[pick] - I_C * qdd[pick]
        estimates.append(np.linalg.lstsq(x, y, rcond=None)[0])
    return np.std(estimates, axis=0)


def row(name: str, theta, se=None) -> str:
    cells = [f"{v:.4f}" + (f" +- {s:.4f}" if se is not None else "") for v, s in zip(theta, se if se is not None else theta)]
    return f"  {name:<44}" + "".join(f"{c:>22}" for c in cells)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    model, _ = pendulum(TRUE)
    h = model.opt.timestep
    t = np.arange(round(10.0 / h)) * h
    rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
    torque = multisine(t, 2.5, seed=1)
    q_true = simulate(TRUE, torque)
    q = q_true + rng.normal(0.0, NOISE, len(q_true))
    print(f"MuJoCo {mujoco.__version__}; 10 s at {1000 * h:.0f} ms, angle noise {1000 * NOISE} mrad, "
          f"angle range {q_true.min():.2f} to {q_true.max():.2f} rad, peak speed {np.abs(np.gradient(q_true, h)).max():.2f} rad/s")
    print(f"  {'':<44}" + "".join(f"{k:>22}" for k in ("mass (kg)", "damping (N m s/rad)", "friction loss (N m)")))
    print(row("truth", list(TRUE.values())))
    print("(1) derivatives and estimates")
    for method in ("finite differences", "Savitzky-Golay"):
        theta, se, _ = fit(*derivatives(q, h, method), torque)
        print(row(method, theta))
    theta, se, resid = fit(*derivatives(q_true, h, "finite differences"), torque)
    print(row("finite differences of the noise-free angle", theta))
    print("(2) uncertainty of the Savitzky-Golay estimate")
    qs = derivatives(q, h, "Savitzky-Golay")
    theta, se, resid = fit(*qs, torque)
    print(row("ordinary least-squares standard errors", theta, se))
    print(row("block bootstrap standard errors", theta, block_bootstrap(*qs, torque)))
    lag1 = np.corrcoef(resid[:-1], resid[1:])[0, 1]
    print(f"  residual autocorrelation at one sample: {lag1:.3f}")
    print("(3) excitation and identifiability")
    for label, amplitude in (("full excitation (2.5 N m)", 2.5), ("weak excitation (0.25 N m)", 0.25)):
        tq = multisine(t, amplitude, seed=1)
        qq = simulate(TRUE, tq) + np.random.default_rng(1).normal(0.0, NOISE, len(t))
        dq = derivatives(qq, h, "Savitzky-Golay")
        x = regressor(*(d[30:-30] for d in dq))
        theta, _, _ = fit(*dq, tq)
        print(row(f"{label}, cond {np.linalg.cond(x / np.abs(x).max(axis=0)):.0f}", theta, block_bootstrap(*dq, tq)))
        speed = np.abs(dq[1][30:-30])
        fast = np.sum(speed >= 0.1)
        refit = (", ".join(f"{v:.4f}" for v in fit(*dq, tq, min_speed=0.1)[0]) if fast >= 100
                 else f"only {fast} samples, too few to fit")
        print(f"    peak speed {speed.max():.2f} rad/s; {np.mean(speed < 0.1):.0%} of samples slower than 0.1 rad/s; "
              f"fit without them: {refit}")
    print("(4) validation on a held-out trajectory (different multisine), open-loop simulation")
    estimate = dict(zip(TRUE, fit(*derivatives(q, h, "Savitzky-Golay"), torque)[0]))
    held_torque = multisine(t, 2.5, seed=7)
    q_held = simulate(TRUE, held_torque) + np.random.default_rng(2).normal(0.0, NOISE, len(t))
    filtered = dict(zip(TRUE, fit(*derivatives(q, h, "Savitzky-Golay"), torque, min_speed=0.1)[0]))
    for label, params in (("estimated parameters", estimate), ("estimated without samples slower than 0.1 rad/s", filtered),
                          ("finite-difference estimate", dict(zip(TRUE, fit(*derivatives(q, h, "finite differences"), torque)[0]))),
                          ("nominal guess (1.0 kg, no damping, no friction)", {"mass": 1.0, "damping": 0.0, "frictionloss": 0.0})):
        q_sim = simulate(params, held_torque)
        err = q_sim - q_held
        print(f"  {label:<48} angle RMS error {1000 * np.sqrt(np.mean(err**2)):7.2f} mrad, "
              f"after 1 s {1000 * np.sqrt(np.mean(err[:round(1 / h)]**2)):6.2f} mrad")
```

## Debugging

**An inertial parameter comes out near zero.** Noisy accelerations in its regressor. Filter, or lower the noise, or use a derivative-free formulation (Lesson 18.2).

**Intervals are tight and the estimate is still wrong.** Correlated residuals (use a block bootstrap) or a wrong model (look at residuals against velocity, position and time).

**Damping and friction come out traded against each other.** They are hard to separate from motion that rarely changes speed. Excite a wide range of speeds, leave out the regime the friction model does not describe, or fix one from a separate experiment.

**The estimate validates well on one trajectory and fails on another.** The first did not excite what the second needs. Validate on trajectories that differ from the identification data in speed and amplitude.

## Research connection

> [!research] Identification as a sim-to-real tool
> Inverse-dynamics least squares is the classic first step in identifying robot arms, where the inertial parameters enter linearly through a regressor matrix and friction is added per joint; excitation trajectories are often optimized to make that regressor well conditioned. The lesson's results carry over directly (inference): filtering decides whether inertial parameters survive, friction models fail near zero velocity, and residual correlation makes naive intervals too narrow. When parameters do not enter linearly, such as contact stiffness or friction coefficients between objects, identification has to simulate, which is Lesson 18.2.
