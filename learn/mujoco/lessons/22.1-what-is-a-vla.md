## Core concept

A vision-language-action (VLA) policy is a policy whose inputs include camera images and a natural-language instruction, usually together with the robot's own state, and whose output is a robot action:

$$a_t \sim \pi(\,\cdot \mid I_t, \ell, q_t) \qquad\text{or, for a chunk,}\qquad (a_t, \dots, a_{t+H-1}) \sim \pi(\,\cdot \mid I_t, \ell, q_t),$$

with $I_t$ the images at time $t$, $\ell$ the instruction and $q_t$ the proprioceptive state. The model is trained on robot data, almost always demonstrations, so that the mapping from these inputs to actions is learned rather than written by hand.

That definition is functional: it says what goes in and what comes out. Three other properties often travel with the word in papers and press releases, and this course keeps them separate:

- **Scale.** OpenVLA has 7 billion parameters; the MiniVLA built in this level has 110 050. Both map images and words to actions.
- **Pretraining.** Many VLAs start from a vision-language model trained on web data. A VLA can also be trained from scratch on robot data, as this level's is.
- **Generality.** A policy trained on many robots and tasks, and meant to be adapted, is a robot foundation model. A VLA trained on one task family is not.

When one of these matters to a claim, state it explicitly, for example "a 7B VLA pretrained on web data", rather than letting "VLA" imply it.

> [!established] Where the term comes from
> RT-2 (Brohan et al., arXiv:2307.15818) introduced the name. Its authors co-fine-tune vision-language models on robot trajectories and web vision-language tasks, express actions as text tokens, and write: "We refer to such category of models as vision-language-action models (VLA)." Their category is VLMs that output actions. Later work, including OpenVLA (arXiv:2406.09246) and π0 (arXiv:2410.24164), keeps the pretrained-VLM backbone. The functional definition above is broader: it is this course's convention, so that a small policy can be studied with the same vocabulary.

## Mathematics

### Six kinds of system, distinguished by their interfaces

| System | Input | Output | What makes it one |
|---|---|---|---|
| vision-language model (VLM) | images and text | text | it answers, describes or plans in words; it does not command motors |
| vision-language-action policy (VLA) | images, an instruction, usually robot state | an action or a chunk of actions | language and vision in, robot commands out |
| imitation-learning policy | any observation | an action | it was trained from demonstrations; vision and language are optional |
| multimodal policy | two or more modalities, such as images and joint angles | an action | more than one kind of input, with or without language |
| robot foundation model | varies | an action | trained on large, diverse robot data to be adapted to new robots and tasks |
| agentic policy | observation and goal; may call models and keep memory | an action chosen by a loop | it decides through proposals, predictions and selection, not through one forward pass |

The rows are not exclusive, because they classify along different axes. The interface (what goes in, what comes out) makes a VLA, a VLM or a multimodal policy. The training method makes an imitation-learning policy. The data and purpose make a foundation model. The decision procedure makes an agentic policy. The course's MiniVLA is a VLA (by interface), an imitation-learning policy (by training), a multimodal policy, and not a foundation model. Lesson 23.8 builds an agentic policy that contains it.

### What the representative systems are

Each of the following facts is taken from the cited paper's text; none is a measurement of this course.

| System | Inputs | Action output | Model | Robot data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RT-1 (arXiv:2212.06817) | a history of 6 images (300 × 300) and an instruction | arm displacement and rotation, gripper, base and mode; each dimension in 256 uniform bins, trained with cross-entropy | FiLM-conditioned EfficientNet-B3, TokenLearner, transformer; 35M parameters; runs at 3 Hz | about 130 000 episodes, 13 robots, 17 months, over 700 tasks |
| RT-2 (arXiv:2307.15818) | images and an instruction | actions as text tokens: continuous dimensions in 256 uniform bins, 8 integers per action | the VLMs PaLI-X and PaLM-E, up to 55B parameters, co-fine-tuned on robot and web data | RT-1's robot data |
| Open X-Embodiment (arXiv:2310.08864) | (a dataset, and RT-X models trained on it) | | | 22 robots, 21 institutions, 527 skills (160 266 tasks) |
| Octo (arXiv:2405.12213) | images, and a language instruction or a goal image | action chunks from a diffusion head that reads transformer "readout" tokens | transformer; checkpoints of 27M and 93M parameters | 800 000 trajectories from Open X-Embodiment |
| OpenVLA (arXiv:2406.09246) | a single image and an instruction | 7 action dimensions, each in 256 bins spanning the 1st to 99th percentile of the training actions, as language-model tokens | Prismatic-7B: a 600M-parameter visual encoder fusing DINOv2 and SigLIP features, a 2-layer projector, Llama 2 7B | 970 000 episodes from Open X-Embodiment |
| π0 (arXiv:2410.24164) | several RGB images, a language command, proprioceptive state | chunks of 50 continuous actions from a flow-matching "action expert"; control at up to 50 Hz | PaliGemma (3B) plus a 300M-parameter action expert, 3.3B in total | over 10 000 hours of robot data, plus Open X-Embodiment |

Read the "Action output" column against Lesson 22.3. Three different answers to the question "what does the network output?" appear in it: tokens over discretized bins (RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA), continuous chunks from a generative head (Octo, π0), and, in this level, continuous regression.

> [!recommendation] Describing a system as a VLA
> Name the interface (cameras, instruction, state in; action space and rate out), then scale, pretraining and training data separately. "A VLA" alone tells the reader only the interface.

### The course's VLA, and what it is not

The MiniVLA of Lessons 22.2 and 22.4 maps a 64 × 64 image, an instruction from a 24-word vocabulary and the pusher's position and velocity to a pusher velocity. Its 110 050 parameters split as follows (output 3): vision encoder 40 032, word embeddings 1 536, state encoder 320, fusion transformer 66 944, action head 130. It has no pretrained component, so it knows no word that its training instructions did not contain: a synonym such as "crimson" is an unknown token to it (Lesson 22.5 measures what that costs). It is a pedagogical VLA-style architecture, and it reproduces none of the systems in the table. It exists so that every stage of the pipeline those systems share (encode, fuse, decode, act, evaluate) can be run, inspected and broken on a laptop.

```python file=examples/l22_1_vla_interface.py
import numpy as np
import torch

from mjcourse import tabletop as tt
from mjcourse.vla import language
from mjcourse.vla.model import MiniVLA, VLAConfig
from mjcourse.vla.train import STATE_SCALE


def part1_2() -> None:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
    text, task = language.instructions("train")[0]
    scene = tt.Tabletop()
    scene.reset(tt.sample_layout(rng, task))
    image, state = scene.render("top", 64), scene.state()
    tokenizer = language.default_tokenizer()
    words = tokenizer(text)
    print("(1) one observation")
    print(f"  image        {image.shape} {image.dtype}, values {image.min()} to {image.max()}  (top camera, RGB)")
    print(f"  instruction  {text!r} -> token ids {words.tolist()}  (vocabulary of {len(tokenizer.vocab)} words)")
    print(f"  robot state  {np.round(state[:4], 3).tolist()}  (pusher x, y in m; vx, vy in m/s)")
    print(f"  not observed {state.size - 4} numbers: the pucks' positions and velocities, which the image must supply")
    torch.manual_seed(0)
    model = MiniVLA(VLAConfig(vocab=len(tokenizer.vocab), chunk=1)).eval()
    with torch.no_grad():
        action = model(torch.as_tensor(image).permute(2, 0, 1).float().unsqueeze(0) / 255.0,
                       torch.as_tensor(words).unsqueeze(0),
                       torch.as_tensor((state[:4] * STATE_SCALE)[None], dtype=torch.float32))
    print("(2) the policy's output")
    print(f"  action       {tuple(action.shape)} = (batch, chunk, 2): a pusher velocity in [-1, 1] x {tt.MAX_SPEED} m/s"
          f" (untrained: {[round(float(v), 3) for v in action[0, 0]]})")
    scene.close()


def part3() -> None:
    model = MiniVLA(VLAConfig(vocab=len(language.default_tokenizer().vocab), chunk=1))
    parts = {"vision encoder": model.vision, "word embeddings": model.words, "state encoder": model.state,
             "fusion transformer": model.fusion, "action head": model.head}
    print("(3) parameters")
    for name, module in parts.items():
        print(f"  {name:<20} {sum(p.numel() for p in module.parameters()):>8,d}")
    print(f"  {'total':<20} {sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters()):>8,d}")


def part4() -> None:
    rows = [("vision-language model", "image(s) + text", "text"),
            ("vision-language-action policy", "image(s) + instruction (+ robot state)", "robot action (or a chunk of them)"),
            ("imitation-learning policy", "any observation", "robot action, learned from demonstrations"),
            ("multimodal policy", "two or more modalities, e.g. image + joint angles", "robot action"),
            ("robot foundation model", "varies; trained on large, diverse robot data", "robot action; meant to be adapted"),
            ("agentic policy", "observation + goal; may call models and keep memory",
             "robot action chosen by a loop of proposals, predictions and selection")]
    print("(4) interfaces")
    for name, inputs, outputs in rows:
        print(f"  {name:<31} in: {inputs:<53} out: {outputs}")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    part1_2()
    part3()
    part4()
```

## Debugging

**A system is called a VLA but outputs a plan in words.** That is a VLM used as a planner, followed by a controller. The distinction matters for evaluation: the controller's failures and the planner's are separable, as in Lesson 16.1's parser and executor.

**A "language-conditioned" policy receives a task index.** If the instruction reaches the network as a one-hot task identifier, the policy has no language input, and paraphrase and synonym evaluations (Lesson 22.5) are meaningless for it.

**A comparison between "VLAs" mixes scales and pretraining.** Report parameters, pretraining and training data for every system compared; otherwise a difference may come from any of them.

## Research connection

> [!research] The word and the evidence
> Because "VLA" names an interface, claims about VLAs as a class are claims about very different systems: a 35M-parameter transformer trained on one fleet's data (RT-1), a 55B VLM co-fine-tuned on web and robot data (RT-2), a 3.3B model with a flow-matching action expert (π0). A result measured on one does not transfer to another without an argument about which property caused it (inference).
