## Core concept

In MuJoCo a contact is a soft constraint: it produces force only by being violated a little, and how much force a given violation produces is set by parameters you choose. Three ideas carry the whole model:

- **The reference acceleration** $a_\text{ref} = -b\,v - k\,r$: the motion the constraint asks for, a spring-damper acting on the violation $r$ (negative when penetrating) and its velocity $v$. `solref` sets $k$ and $b$.
- **The impedance** $d \in (0, 1)$: how strongly the constraint pulls the actual acceleration towards $a_\text{ref}$, away from the unconstrained acceleration $a_0$. `solimp` sets $d$ as a function of the violation.
- **One convex optimization per step** that finds all constraint forces together, contacts, joint limits, equalities and friction, with the regularizer $R = \frac{1-d}{d}\hat A$ making each constraint soft.

Every contact number earlier in the course follows from these: the 0.367 mm a resting ball sinks (Lesson 0.1), why that does not depend on its mass, why a joint stop sinks further under a heavier arm (Lesson 5.1), and why inverse dynamics with contact is well defined (Lesson 7.2).

> [!established] The model, as MuJoCo's documentation states it
> For a single scalar constraint, approximately, $a_1 + d\,(b\,v + k\,r) = (1-d)\,a_0$, where $a_1$ is the constrained and $a_0$ the unconstrained acceleration. With `solref = (timeconst, dampratio)`, $b = 2/(d_w\,\text{timeconst})$ and $k = d(r)/(d_w^2\,\text{timeconst}^2\,\text{dampratio}^2)$; with the direct format `solref = (-stiffness, -damping)`, $b = \text{damping}/d_w$ and $k = \text{stiffness}\cdot d(r)/d_w^2$. `solimp = (d_0, d_w, width, midpoint, power)` makes $d$ rise from $d_0$ at zero violation to $d_w$ at `width` along a sigmoid. The time constant must be at least twice the timestep, which MuJoCo enforces unless the `refsafe` flag is disabled. A contact between geoms with different `solref`/`solimp` uses the higher-priority geom's values, or the `solmix`-weighted average (or, if either `solref` is in the direct format, the element-wise minimum).

## Mathematics

### Resting penetration

At rest, $a_1 = 0$ and $v = 0$, so the constraint equation reduces to $d\,k\,r = (1-d)\,a_0$. With the time-constant format,

$$|r| = \frac{(1-d)\,a_0\,d_w^2\,\text{timeconst}^2\,\text{dampratio}^2}{d^2}, \qquad d = d(r),$$

an implicit equation because the impedance depends on the violation; a few fixed-point iterations solve it. For the default parameters it gives $|r|$ = 0.3672 mm, with $d$ = 0.9135, exactly what MuJoCo measures (output 1).

> [!derivation] Why the penetration does not depend on mass, but a joint stop's does
> For a body resting on a plane, the unconstrained acceleration along the normal is $a_0 = g$ whatever the mass, so $r$ is the same for 0.1, 1 and 10 kg (output 1). For a joint stop, $a_0$ is the joint's acceleration if the stop were removed: the gravity torque divided by the effective inertia about that joint. Making one link heavier raises both, but not in proportion, which is the +26% of Lesson 5.1's quiz. The parameters set a *reference motion*, not a stiffness in newtons per metre.

### From parameters to force

The regularizer is $R_{ii} = \frac{1-d_i}{d_i}\hat A_{ii}$, with $\hat A$ a diagonal approximation of $A = JM^{-1}J^\top$ computed once at `qpos0`. When the unconstrained minimizer of the solver's problem is admissible, the force is

$$f = (A + R)^{-1}(a_\text{ref} - a_0), \qquad a_1 = A(A+R)^{-1}a_\text{ref} + R(A+R)^{-1}a_0 ,$$

so the constrained acceleration interpolates between the reference and the unconstrained one: hard as $R \to 0$ ($d \to 1$), absent as $R \to \infty$ ($d \to 0$). Output (2) reads every quantity for the resting ball out of MuJoCo (`efc_KBIP`, `efc_aref`, `efc_R`, `efc_force`) and recomputes each from these formulas. Two details show up: `efc_KBIP` stores $k$ without the factor $d(r)$, which MuJoCo applies when it forms $a_\text{ref} = -b v - d\,k\,r$; and for this free ball $\hat A = A = 1/m$, so the regularizer matches its definition exactly.

### The optimization MuJoCo solves

All constraint forces come from one convex problem, a generalization of Gauss's principle of least constraint:

$$\min_{x,\,y}\ \|x - M^{-1}(\tau - c)\|_M^2 + \|y - a_\text{ref}\|^2_{R^{-1}} \quad \text{s.t.}\ \ J_\mathcal{E}x - y_\mathcal{E} = 0,\ \ J_\mathcal{F}x - y_\mathcal{F} = 0,\ \ J_\mathcal{C}x - y_\mathcal{C} \in \mathcal{K}^*,$$

where $x$ is the joint acceleration, $y$ a slack in constraint space, $\mathcal{K}^*$ the dual of the friction cones, and the norm on $y$ becomes a Huber function for friction loss. The first term keeps the motion close to what it would be without constraints, measured in the inertia metric; the second pulls each constraint towards its reference acceleration, as strongly as $R^{-1}$ says. Because the problem is convex, its solution is unique and the solvers (Newton by default, also CG and PGS) find it reliably; because $R > 0$, it is never the hard, possibly ill-posed rigid-contact problem.

### Bounce

The damping ratio is the same in the effective constraint-space oscillator as in `solref` (MuJoCo absorbs one factor of $d$ into $k$ but not into $b$ for that reason). Lower ratios bounce: output (3) shows rebound heights of 0, 7%, 33% and 57% of the drop for ratios 1, 0.5, 0.2 and 0.1, and a fully elastic bounce with zero damping in the direct format. The linear-oscillator formula for restitution underestimates these because a contact releases as soon as its force would turn negative, before the spring has returned its energy symmetrically (inference from the unilateral constraint).

```python file=examples/l9_2_soft_contact.py
"""Lesson 9.2: the soft contact model: solref, solimp, the regularizer and the solver.

INPUT   a ball on a plane, written below (frictionless contacts where friction would
        only add rows to the comparison)
PROCESS (1) resting penetration predicted from the model's equations and measured, for
            several time constants, a constant impedance, the direct solref format and
            three masses;
        (2) MuJoCo's own constraint quantities for the resting contact: impedance d,
            stiffness k, damping b, reference acceleration, regularizer R, and the force
            (A + R)^-1 (aref - a0) against efc_force;
        (3) bounce: rebound height against the damping ratio;
        (4) refsafe: a time constant below twice the timestep is raised to 2h
OUTPUT  printed tables

Run:  python examples/l9_2_soft_contact.py
"""

import mujoco
import numpy as np

G = 9.81
BALL = """<mujoco><option timestep="{h}"/><worldbody>
  <geom name="plane" type="plane" size="1 1 .1" condim="1" solref="{plane_solref}" solimp="{plane_solimp}"/>
  <body name="ball" pos="0 0 {z}"><freejoint/>
    <geom name="ball" size="0.05" mass="{m}" condim="1" solref="{solref}" solimp="{solimp}"/></body>
</worldbody></mujoco>"""
DEFAULT_SOLIMP = "0.9 0.95 0.001 0.5 2"


def impedance(r: float, solimp: list[float]) -> float:
    """d(r) from solimp = (d0, dw, width, midpoint, power), as in MuJoCo's documentation."""
    d0, dw, width, mid, power = solimp
    x = min(abs(r) / width, 1.0)
    if x >= 1.0:
        return dw
    y = x**power / mid ** (power - 1) if x <= mid else 1 - (1 - x) ** power / (1 - mid) ** (power - 1)
    return d0 + y * (dw - d0)


def predicted_rest(a0: float, solref: list[float], solimp: list[float]) -> float:
    """Solve d k r = (1 - d) a0 for the penetration r, with k from solref and d = d(r)."""
    r = 1e-4
    for _ in range(200):                                       # fixed point; converges quickly here
        d, dw = impedance(r, solimp), solimp[1]
        if solref[0] > 0:
            tc, dr = solref
            k = d / (dw**2 * tc**2 * dr**2)
        else:
            k = -solref[0] * d / dw**2
        r = (1 - d) * a0 / (d * k)
    return r


def ball_model(solref: str, solimp: str = DEFAULT_SOLIMP, mass: float = 1.0, h: float = 0.002, z: float = 0.05,
               plane: tuple[str, str] | None = None) -> mujoco.MjModel:
    """The ball scene; the plane gets the ball's contact parameters unless `plane` says otherwise."""
    plane_solref, plane_solimp = plane if plane else (solref, solimp)
    return mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_string(BALL.format(h=h, z=z, m=mass, solref=solref, solimp=solimp,
                                                      plane_solref=plane_solref, plane_solimp=plane_solimp))


def measured_rest(solref: str, solimp: str = DEFAULT_SOLIMP, mass: float = 1.0, h: float = 0.002,
                  plane: tuple[str, str] | None = None) -> float:
    model = ball_model(solref, solimp, mass, h, plane=plane)
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    for _ in range(round(3.0 / h)):
        mujoco.mj_step(model, data)
    return 0.05 - data.qpos[2]


def resting_penetration() -> None:
    print(f"  {'solref':<14}{'solimp':<22}{'mass (kg)':>10}{'predicted (mm)':>16}{'measured (mm)':>15}")
    cases = [("0.02 1", DEFAULT_SOLIMP, 1.0), ("0.01 1", DEFAULT_SOLIMP, 1.0), ("0.005 1", DEFAULT_SOLIMP, 1.0),
             ("0.02 1", "0.9 0.9 0.001 0.5 2", 1.0), ("-10000 -200", DEFAULT_SOLIMP, 1.0),
             ("0.02 1", DEFAULT_SOLIMP, 0.1), ("0.02 1", DEFAULT_SOLIMP, 10.0)]
    for solref, solimp, mass in cases:
        pred = predicted_rest(G, [float(v) for v in solref.split()], [float(v) for v in solimp.split()])
        meas = measured_rest(solref, solimp, mass)
        print(f"  {solref:<14}{solimp:<22}{mass:>10g}{1000 * pred:>16.4f}{1000 * meas:>15.4f}")
    # Mixing: ball 0.01 1, plane left at the default 0.02 1. Equal solmix: the contact uses the average.
    pred = predicted_rest(G, [0.015, 1.0], [0.9, 0.95, 0.001, 0.5, 2])
    meas = measured_rest("0.01 1", plane=("0.02 1", DEFAULT_SOLIMP))
    print(f"  ball 0.01 1 on a plane with the default 0.02 1: contact uses the average 0.015 1 -> "
          f"predicted {1000 * pred:.4f} mm, measured {1000 * meas:.4f} mm")


def internals() -> None:
    model = ball_model("0.02 1")
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    for _ in range(1500):
        mujoco.mj_step(model, data)
    mujoco.mj_forward(model, data)
    k, b, d, _ = data.efc_KBIP[0]
    r, v = data.efc_pos[0] - data.efc_margin[0], data.efc_vel[0]
    contact = data.contact[0]
    jacp = np.zeros((3, model.nv))
    mujoco.mj_jac(model, data, jacp, None, contact.pos, model.body("ball").id)
    jac = (contact.frame[:3] @ jacp).reshape(1, model.nv)      # normal row: the ball's velocity along the normal
    mass = np.zeros((model.nv, model.nv))
    mujoco.mj_fullM(model, data, mass)
    a_row = (jac @ np.linalg.solve(mass, jac.T)).item()        # A = J M^-1 J^T for this row
    a0 = (jac @ np.linalg.solve(mass, -data.qfrc_bias)).item()  # unconstrained acceleration along the normal
    f_formula = (data.efc_aref[0] - a0) / (a_row + data.efc_R[0])
    print(f"  residual r = {1000 * r:.4f} mm (negative: penetrating), velocity {v:.1e} m/s")
    print(f"  impedance d = {d:.5f}; d(r) from solimp = {impedance(r, [0.9, 0.95, 0.001, 0.5, 2]):.5f}")
    print(f"  efc_KBIP stiffness {k:.1f} = 1 / (dw^2 tc^2 dr^2) = {1 / (0.95**2 * 0.02**2):.1f}; the documentation's k = d * that = {d * k:.1f}")
    print(f"  efc_KBIP damping {b:.4f} = 2 / (dw tc) = {2 / (0.95 * 0.02):.4f}")
    print(f"  aref = {data.efc_aref[0]:.5f} m/s^2; -b v - d k r = {-b * v - d * k * r:.5f}")
    print(f"  R = {data.efc_R[0]:.6f}, (1 - d) / d * A_hat with A_hat = 1/m = 1: {(1 - d) / d:.6f}; A from J M^-1 J^T = {a_row:.6f}")
    print(f"  constraint force (A + R)^-1 (aref - a0) = {f_formula:.5f} N, efc_force = {data.efc_force[0]:.5f} N, weight m g = {G:.5f} N")


def bounce() -> None:
    print(f"  {'solref':<16}{'rebound / drop height':>22}")
    for solref in ("0.02 1", "0.02 0.5", "0.02 0.2", "0.02 0.1", "-10000 0"):
        model = ball_model(solref, h=0.0005, z=0.35)
        data = mujoco.MjData(model)
        peak, falling, contacted = 0.0, True, False
        for _ in range(round(1.5 / model.opt.timestep)):
            mujoco.mj_step(model, data)
            contacted |= data.ncon > 0
            if contacted and data.qvel[2] > 0:
                falling = False
            if not falling:
                peak = max(peak, data.qpos[2])
                if data.qvel[2] < 0:
                    break
        print(f"  {solref:<16}{(peak - 0.05) / 0.30:>22.3f}")


def refsafe() -> None:
    h = 0.002
    for solref in ("0.002 1", "0.004 1"):
        print(f"  timestep {h} s, solref {solref}: resting penetration {1000 * measured_rest(solref, h=h):.4f} mm")
    model = ball_model("0.002 1", h=h)
    model.opt.disableflags |= mujoco.mjtDisableBit.mjDSBL_REFSAFE
    data = mujoco.MjData(model)
    for _ in range(1500):
        mujoco.mj_step(model, data)
    print(f"  solref 0.002 1 with refsafe disabled: after 3 s the ball's centre is {data.qpos[2]:.3f} m high "
          f"(it started resting at 0.05 m): the contact is too stiff for the step and throws it into the air")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    print("(1) resting penetration of a ball on a plane (frictionless contact), predicted and measured")
    resting_penetration()
    print("(2) MuJoCo's constraint quantities for the resting contact (solref 0.02 1, default solimp)")
    internals()
    print("(3) drop from 30 cm: rebound height by damping ratio (timestep 0.5 ms)")
    bounce()
    print("(4) refsafe: time constants below 2 h")
    refsafe()
```

Reading the output:

- **(1)** Every prediction matches the measurement to four decimals: time constants of 0.02, 0.01 and 0.005 s (the penetration scales roughly with the square of the time constant), constant impedance, the direct format (stiffness $10^4$ gives the same 0.1078 mm as a time constant of 0.01 s, because $1/0.01^2 = 10^4$), and three masses. - **(2)** MuJoCo's internal quantities agree with the documented formulas, and the force computed from $(A+R)^{-1}(a_\text{ref} - a_0)$ is exactly the ball's weight, as it must be at rest.
- **(3)** Bounce is set by the damping ratio, from none at 1 to elastic with zero damping.
- **(4)** A time constant of 0.002 s at a 0.002 s step behaves exactly like 0.004 s: `refsafe` raised it to $2h$ without telling you. Disable `refsafe` and the same contact throws the ball 0.7 m into the air.

## Debugging

**Objects sink visibly into each other.** The time constant is large or the impedance low. Predict the penetration with the formula above before changing anything, then lower `timeconst` (not below $2h$) or raise $d_0$, $d_w$.

**Changing a geom's `solref` has less effect than expected.** The other geom's parameters are mixed in (output 1, last row). Set both, use `priority`, or define the pair explicitly with `<pair>`.

**Making contacts stiffer has no effect below some value.** `refsafe` is clamping the time constant at twice the timestep (output 4). Reduce the timestep if you really need stiffer contacts.

**A contact with `solref="-1000 -10"` behaves strangely.** The direct format: stiffness in (m/s²)/m and damping in (m/s²)/(m/s), applied as accelerations, not newtons. Mixing it with a positive-format geom takes the element-wise minimum.

**Gradients of the dynamics are rough near contact.** $d_0 > 0$ makes the impedance, and hence the force, jump as a contact activates. MuJoCo's documentation recommends $d_0 = 0$ (`solimp[0]=0`) for smooth, differentiable dynamics.
