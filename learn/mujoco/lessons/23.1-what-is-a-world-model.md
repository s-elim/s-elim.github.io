## Core concept

Every lesson in this course so far has had a perfect model of its world: MuJoCo, given an MJCF file. A planner that asked "what happens if I push this way?" could ask MuJoCo and get the right answer for the system described. A world model is what an agent uses when it does not have that: a model of how its world responds to its actions, learned from experience.

The phrase is used loosely, often for any network that predicts something. This level uses an operational definition, and every lesson tests one of its parts:

> A **world model** is a learned, action-conditioned predictive model: given what the agent has observed and a sequence of actions it might take, it predicts future observations or task-relevant quantities (states, rewards, outcomes) over several steps, well enough to be used for a decision (planning, policy learning or evaluation).

Three requirements follow, and each one excludes something:
- **Learned** from data. A simulator is a model written by people; it is a world model only in a loose sense, and this level keeps it separate.
- **Action-conditioned.** A video predictor that forecasts the next frames without being told the agent's actions cannot answer "what if I do this instead?". It is a predictive model, not a world model in this sense.
- **Multi-step and decision-relevant.** One-step accuracy is not the test; usefulness for decisions over several steps is. Lessons 23.2 and 23.5 show these are different.

> [!established] Where the term comes from
> Ha and Schmidhuber's "World Models" (arXiv:1803.10122) learns "a compressed spatial and temporal representation" of an environment without supervision, and trains an agent "entirely inside of its own hallucinated dream" before transferring it back. PlaNet (arXiv:1811.04551) learns dynamics from images and plans in latent space; its model "must accurately predict the rewards ahead for multiple time steps". Dreamer (arXiv:1912.01603) learns behaviours "purely by latent imagination", propagating gradients of learned state values through trajectories imagined in a learned world model; DreamerV3 (arXiv:2301.04104) applies the approach across domains. TD-MPC (arXiv:2203.04955) optimizes trajectories over a short horizon with "a learned task-oriented latent dynamics model" and a learned terminal value, and TD-MPC2 (arXiv:2310.16828) does so in "the latent space of a learned implicit (decoder-free) world model". None of these is reproduced in this level; its models demonstrate the concepts on one tabletop scene.

## Mathematics

### Seven words, kept apart

| Term | What it is | In this course |
|---|---|---|
| simulator | a program computing the next state from the current state, the action and a model written by people | MuJoCo with `tabletop.xml` |
| forward dynamics model | any function $s_{t+1} = f(s_t, a_t)$, analytic or learned | all four models of output (1) |
| system-identification model | a model whose structure comes from physics and whose few parameters are fitted to data | Level 18; MuJoCo with fitted friction below |
| predictive model | anything that predicts future observations, with or without actions | a video predictor |
| latent dynamics model | dynamics in a learned representation: $z = e(o)$, $z_{t+1} = g(z_t, a_t)$ | Lesson 23.3 |
| world model | the definition above: learned, action-conditioned, multi-step, decision-relevant | Lessons 23.2 to 23.6 |
| model-based reinforcement learning | using a model, learned or given, to plan or to train a policy | Lessons 21.4 and 23.4 |

### Four models of one scene

The tabletop scene of Level 22 has a 16-number planar state: the pusher's position and velocity, and each puck's. The truth comes from MuJoCo. Two hundred test segments are made by running the data-collection policy of Lesson 23.2 for a random time, saving MuJoCo's full state, then applying the policy's next ten actions (1 s). Four models predict the final state from the saved state and the same actions:

- **MuJoCo**, restarted from the saved state. It is the system that generated the truth, so it should be exact. It is exact only if the saved state includes the contact solver's warm start (`mjSTATE_INTEGRATION`): with `mjSTATE_FULLPHYSICS` the restart's pushed-puck prediction was off by 0.06 mm on average after a second of contact.
- **MuJoCo with the wrong friction**, 0.3 instead of 0.4. This is what a system-identification model looks like when one parameter is misidentified: the right equations, a wrong number.
- **A hand-written model.** The pusher follows its command with the velocity servo's time constant $m/k_v = 0.5/40 = 12.5$ ms, integrated exactly, and nothing else moves. It is what a person might write without modelling contact.
- **The learned model** of Lesson 23.2: an MLP trained on 120 000 transitions.
- **Nothing moves**, $s_{t+10} = s_t$, as a floor.

Output (1), mean error after 1 s (mm), split by whether the pusher touched a puck during the second (it did in 169 of 200):

| Model | pusher, no contact | pushed puck, no contact | pusher, contact | pushed puck, contact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MuJoCo | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| MuJoCo, wrong friction | 0.00 | 0.02 | 0.84 | 1.84 |
| hand-written | 6.33 | 0.28 | 4.84 | 36.14 |
| learned | 10.43 | 2.31 | 6.96 | 7.65 |
| nothing moves | 101.68 | 0.28 | 63.66 | 36.14 |

Four readings:
- **Each model is wrong where its maker simplified.** The hand-written model ignores contact, so on the pushed puck in contact it is no better than "nothing moves" (36.14 mm in both columns). Its 6.3 mm error on the free pusher plausibly comes from the joint damping and the force limit it leaves out (inference; the exercise measures it).
- **The learned model is better than the hand-written one exactly where people did not write the physics down,** contact (7.65 against 36.14 mm). It is worse where they did, the free pusher (10.43 against 6.33). It also hallucinates motion: 2.3 mm of puck movement when nothing touched the puck.
- **A misidentified simulator is still a strong model here,** better than the learned one in every column. That is a fact about this scene, where MuJoCo's equations are the truth; on a real robot the simulator's equations are themselves approximate (Levels 17 to 19).
- **MuJoCo is exact only with its full integration state.** A world-model evaluation that uses a simulator as ground truth must restore every piece of state the simulator integrates.

> [!recommendation] Calling something a world model
> Say what it predicts (states, observations, rewards), from what (states or images), conditioned on which actions, over how many steps, and for which decision. Evaluate it against the simulator where one exists, and against the simple baselines: "nothing moves" and the model a person would write.

## Implementation

```io
INPUT: tabletop.xml; the learned model of mjcourse.worldmodel.standard
PROCESS: 200 test segments; five predictions of the state 1 s later; errors split by contact
OUTPUT: the table above
```

```python file=examples/l23_1_kinds_of_model.py
import mujoco
import numpy as np

from mjcourse import tabletop as tt
from mjcourse.worldmodel import data, standard

H = 10


def segment(seed: int, rng: np.random.Generator):
    task = (tt.OBJECTS[rng.integers(3)], list(tt.ZONES)[rng.integers(4)])
    scene = tt.Tabletop()
    scene.reset(tt.sample_layout(rng, task))
    policy = lambda s: rng.uniform(-1, 1, 2) if rng.random() < 0.2 else np.clip(tt.expert(s, task) + rng.normal(0, 0.3, 2), -1, 1)
    for _ in range(int(rng.integers(0, 100))):
        scene.step(policy(scene.state()))
    full = np.zeros(mujoco.mj_stateSize(scene.model, mujoco.mjtState.mjSTATE_INTEGRATION))
    mujoco.mj_getState(scene.model, scene.data, full, mujoco.mjtState.mjSTATE_INTEGRATION)
    s0, actions, path = scene.state(), [], [scene.state()]
    for _ in range(H):
        actions.append(policy(path[-1]))
        scene.step(actions[-1])
        path.append(scene.state())
    scene.close()
    touched = data.contact_share([{"states": np.array(path)}]) > 0
    return s0, full, np.array(actions), path[-1], task, touched


def mujoco_prediction(full: np.ndarray, actions: np.ndarray, friction: float = 0.4) -> np.ndarray:
    scene = tt.Tabletop(tt.Variation(friction=friction))
    mujoco.mj_setState(scene.model, scene.data, full, mujoco.mjtState.mjSTATE_INTEGRATION)
    mujoco.mj_forward(scene.model, scene.data)
    for a in actions:
        scene.step(a)
    out = scene.state()
    scene.close()
    return out


def hand_written(s0: np.ndarray, actions: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    s = s0.copy()
    tau, dt = 0.5 / 40.0, tt.CONTROL_DT
    for a in actions:
        v_cmd = np.clip(a, -1, 1) * tt.MAX_SPEED
        v0 = s[2:4].copy()
        decay = np.exp(-dt / tau)
        s[2:4] = v_cmd + (v0 - v_cmd) * decay
        s[0:2] += v_cmd * dt + (v0 - v_cmd) * tau * (1 - decay)
        s[4:] = np.r_[[s[4 + 4 * i:6 + 4 * i].tolist() + [0.0, 0.0] for i in range(3)]].ravel()
    return s


def main() -> None:
    mlp = standard.model("one_step")
    rng = np.random.default_rng(23)
    rows = {name: {"contact": [], "free": []} for name in ("MuJoCo", "MuJoCo, wrong friction", "hand-written", "learned",
                                                           "nothing moves")}
    for _ in range(200):
        s0, full, a, true, task, touched = segment(23, rng)
        target = 4 + 4 * tt.OBJECTS.index(task[0])
        predictions = {"MuJoCo": mujoco_prediction(full, a), "MuJoCo, wrong friction": mujoco_prediction(full, a, 0.3),
                       "hand-written": hand_written(s0, a), "learned": mlp.rollout(s0[None], a[None].astype(np.float32))[0, -1],
                       "nothing moves": s0}
        for name, p in predictions.items():
            rows[name]["contact" if touched else "free"].append(
                (np.linalg.norm(p[:2] - true[:2]), np.linalg.norm(p[target:target + 2] - true[target:target + 2])))
    n_contact = len(rows["MuJoCo"]["contact"])
    print(f"200 test segments of 1 s; the pusher touched a puck in {n_contact} of them")
    print(f"  {'model':<24}{'pusher, no contact':>20}{'puck, no contact':>18}{'pusher, contact':>18}{'puck, contact':>16}"
          "   (mean error, mm)")
    for name, r in rows.items():
        free, contact = np.array(r["free"]) * 1000, np.array(r["contact"]) * 1000
        print(f"  {name:<24}{free[:, 0].mean():>20.2f}{free[:, 1].mean():>18.2f}{contact[:, 0].mean():>18.2f}"
              f"{contact[:, 1].mean():>16.2f}")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

## Debugging

**The simulator disagrees with itself.** Restarting MuJoCo from a saved state does not reproduce the original trajectory. Save `mjSTATE_INTEGRATION`, which includes the warm start, not only positions and velocities. Replaying recorded actions rounded to float32 also diverges in contact-rich scenes, by centimetres within seconds.

**A "world model" cannot answer counterfactuals.** If its predictions do not change when the actions do, it is not action-conditioned. Test with two different action sequences from the same state.

**A learned model is evaluated only where it is accurate.** Split the error by the situations that matter (contact against no contact here) before averaging.

## Research connection

> [!research] What counts as a world model
> The published systems differ in what they predict and why. PlaNet's model must predict rewards several steps ahead, and adds a multi-step objective, "latent overshooting", for it (arXiv:1811.04551). TD-MPC2's model has no decoder at all: it is never asked to reproduce an observation (arXiv:2310.16828). Lambert et al. name the gap between a model's training objective and its use "objective mismatch": a model trained for one-step likelihood need not be the one that controls best (arXiv:2002.04523). Lessons 23.3 and 23.5 measure versions of that gap; Lesson 23.7 asks you to study it.
