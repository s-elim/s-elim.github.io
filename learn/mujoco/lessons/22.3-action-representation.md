## Core concept

"The policy outputs an action" hides three decisions:
- what one output number means physically (a torque, a joint angle, a change of the tool's position);
- what runs between the policy and the motors to make it happen (nothing, a joint servo, inverse kinematics plus a servo);
- how long each output is held, and whether one output is one action or a sequence of them.

Together these are the action representation. The same motion can be written in any of them, and a learned policy only ever sees the written form. So the representation changes what function the network has to learn, which errors the controller underneath absorbs, and which changes in the world the policy survives.

This lesson measures that on the 7-DOF arm. Six representations are used to write down the same reference motions, and each is cloned with the same network, the same data size and the same training. The results are then tested under a payload, a slower control rate and targets outside the training region. One of the lesson's findings is a correction: the first explanation for why two representations failed was wrong, and an ablation showed what was really happening. The lesson ends with action chunks on the tabletop task of Lesson 22.4.

## Mathematics

### Six ways to write one motion, and what MuJoCo runs for each

| Representation | One action means | Between policy and motors, at 500 Hz | In MuJoCo |
|---|---|---|---|
| torque | joint torques, fractions of each motor's limit | nothing | `<motor>` actuators, `ctrl` = torque |
| absolute joint position | joint targets, fractions of each joint's range | PD + gravity compensation | `<position>` actuators, or PD on `qfrc_bias` |
| joint delta | target increments of up to 0.05 rad | the same servo, on an integrated target | as above |
| joint velocity | joint velocities of up to 1 rad/s | a velocity servo + gravity compensation | `<velocity>` actuators |
| Cartesian delta | tool-target increments: 2 cm in x, y, z and 0.1 rad of yaw | differential IK (Lesson 6.2), then PD | IK on `mj_jacSite`, then a servo |
| absolute Cartesian pose | the tool target in the workspace box, and yaw | differential IK, then PD | as above |

Gravity compensation always uses the nominal arm, as a real controller with a fixed model would, so a payload is unknown to every controller. The policy acts at 20 Hz. Each expert follows the same reference motion: a minimum-jerk tool path over 1.5 s, converted to joint angles by IK. Each expert's action is whatever makes its own representation follow that motion, so the demonstrations differ only in how the motion is written. Every expert succeeds on 20 of 20 test targets. The torque expert needed gentler gains than the others: with the servo gains of the target-based controllers (400 and 40, $\omega_n$ = 20 rad/s) it succeeded on 0.92 of targets. A torque command held for 50 ms closes its feedback loop at 20 Hz, too slowly for stiff gains (see Lesson 19.1 for delay margins). With 225 and 30 it succeeded on all.

The task: bring the tool within 1.5 cm and 0.1 rad of a target pose, starting from a perturbed home pose, in 2 s. Each representation is cloned from 1000 demonstrations (40 000 frames) by an MLP with two hidden layers of 256 units, 6000 steps, three seeds, and tested on 100 held-out targets per condition:
- **nominal:** targets from the training region;
- **payload 1 kg:** a 1 kg mass on the last link;
- **10 Hz:** the policy acts every 100 ms instead of 50, with the same 40 decisions;
- **new region:** targets with $y$ in [0.15, 0.25] m, outside the training region's [−0.25, 0.10].

### First result: observing joint velocities

Output (1), success (mean of three seeds; each seed's value in output):

| Representation | nominal | payload 1 kg | 10 Hz | new region | final error, nominal (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| torque | 0.63 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.11 | 12.8 |
| absolute joint position | 0.06 | 0.00 | 0.03 | 0.00 | 42.6 |
| joint delta | 0.47 | 0.41 | 0.01 | 0.08 | 15.9 |
| joint velocity | 0.13 | 0.00 | 0.09 | 0.01 | 29.8 |
| Cartesian delta | 0.97 | 0.83 | 0.03 | 0.20 | 5.1 |
| absolute Cartesian pose | 0.01 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 49.4 |

Read naively, this table says that Cartesian deltas are the right representation, that absolute targets are hopeless, and that every policy fails at 10 Hz. The two absolute representations reach the lowest training error of all (0.0001 in normalized units) and nearly never succeed. A plausible explanation offered itself: an absolute target must be predicted to a fraction of a millimetre of motion out of the whole workspace, so a small error relative to the output's range is a large one at the tool. That explanation was wrong, as the next experiment showed.

### The correction: remove the velocities

Output (1b) repeats everything with the 7 joint velocities removed from the observation (17 numbers instead of 24):

| Representation | nominal | payload 1 kg | 10 Hz | new region | final error, nominal (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| torque | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 253.3 |
| absolute joint position | 0.89 | 0.02 | 0.92 | 0.01 | 7.1 |
| joint delta | 0.74 | 0.70 | 0.65 | 0.22 | 9.4 |
| joint velocity | 0.96 | 0.70 | 0.97 | 0.49 | 4.1 |
| Cartesian delta | 0.99 | 0.84 | 1.00 | 0.59 | 3.8 |
| absolute Cartesian pose | 0.85 | 0.01 | 0.91 | 0.06 | 7.9 |

Absolute joint targets go from 0.06 to 0.89, joint velocities from 0.13 to 0.96, and the 10 Hz failures disappear. The representations were not the problem. The observation was. The arm's joint velocities change smoothly from step to step, so the current velocity nearly determines the next action. A network can reach a low training loss by continuing the motion it is already making, instead of reading the target and computing a path to it. That shortcut works on the demonstrations and fails in closed loop, where the motion the policy is "continuing" is its own. At 10 Hz the observed velocities differ from training (the arm covers each step's motion in twice the time), and the shortcut breaks completely.

This is a known failure of behaviour cloning. De Haan et al. call it causal misidentification and show that "access to more information can yield worse performance" (arXiv:1905.11979). Wen et al. describe the instance where expert actions are correlated over time and "the imitator learns to cheat by predicting the expert's previous action" (arXiv:2010.14876). The torque row is the instructive exception: without velocities it collapses from 0.63 to 0.00. A torque policy has to supply the damping itself, and damping is a velocity term, so for torque the velocity is part of the cause, not a nuisance.

### What remains true of the representations

With the shortcut removed, the representations still differ, and the differences now have mechanisms:
- **A payload breaks absolute targets but not increments** (0.02 and 0.01 against 0.70 and 0.84). A servo with gravity compensation for the wrong mass settles below its target. An absolute target repeats the same target every step, so the sag stays; an increment policy sees the remaining error and keeps moving its target until the tool arrives (inference from the controllers' structure).
- **Velocity commands are the most rate-independent.** Their meaning, rad/s, does not depend on how long they are held: 0.97 at 10 Hz against 0.96 at 20 Hz.
- **Cartesian deltas generalize furthest out of region** (0.59), then joint velocities (0.49), joint deltas (0.22) and the absolute representations (0.06 and 0.01). The Cartesian-delta mapping from (tool position, target) to action is close to a subtraction in the space where the region was moved. For joint representations the same change of target is a nonlinear change in joint space (inference).
- **No representation extrapolates well.** The best new-region success is 0.59. An MLP cloned on one region of the workspace interpolates; it does not extrapolate.

### Gripper commands

The gripper of Level 10 is a position servo on a tendon (one finger driven, the other mirrored). As an action, its command is usually written either as a continuous opening or as a binary open or close. A continuous gripper output cloned from demonstrations that only ever open or close is averaged near the transitions, giving half-closed commands. Discretizing it, as RT-1 does for every action dimension (256 bins, arXiv:2212.06817), or thresholding a continuous output, makes the command decisive (inference; Lesson 10.1 measures what the squeeze force of a closed command must carry). The pushing task of this level has no gripper.

### Action chunks

A policy can output a sequence of the next $H$ actions instead of one. ACT (arXiv:2304.13705), Octo (arXiv:2405.12213) and π0 (arXiv:2410.24164) all do; π0 uses chunks of 50. A chunk can be executed three ways: apply only its first action and predict again (receding horizon); apply the whole chunk open loop; or average, for each step, every chunk that predicted it (ACT's temporal ensemble). Output (2) measures this on the tabletop pushing task with state policies, 2000 demonstrations, three seeds and 100 held-out tasks:

| Chunk length | first action only | whole chunk, open loop | temporal ensemble |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.90 | | |
| 4 | 0.87 | 0.75 | 0.79 |
| 8 | 0.84 | 0.47 | 0.49 |

Here chunks cost success, and executing them open loop costs most. Pushing a round puck needs a correction at every step: the demonstrator corrects the pusher's sideways offset every 0.1 s, and a chunk executed open loop gives that up for 0.4 or 0.8 s. The demonstrator is a deterministic function of the current state, so there is no multimodality or hidden state for a chunk to resolve. That is the situation in which chunking helps in the papers above, which learn from human demonstrations (inference). Whether chunking helps is a property of the task and the data, to be measured, not assumed.

> [!recommendation] Choosing and reporting an action representation
> State what one action means, its units and range, what runs between the policy and the motors, and the control rate. Test the policy under at least one dynamics change (a payload) and one rate change. Ablate the observation as well as the action: a representation can look bad because of a shortcut in the inputs, as both absolute representations did here.

```python file=examples/l22_3_action_representation.py
import os

from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np

FAST = os.environ.get("MJC_FAST") == "1"
SEEDS = (0,) if FAST else (0, 1, 2)
DEMOS, TESTS = (40, 10) if FAST else (1000, 100)
TRAIN_LOW, TRAIN_HIGH = np.array([0.35, -0.25, 0.15]), np.array([0.60, 0.10, 0.45])
NEW_LOW, NEW_HIGH = np.array([0.35, 0.15, 0.15]), np.array([0.60, 0.25, 0.45])
CONDITIONS = ("nominal", "payload 1 kg", "10 Hz", "new region")
OUT = Path(__file__).resolve().parents[1] / "runs" / "l22_3"


def demonstrations(rep: str):
    from mjcourse.vla import actions as A

    env, rng = A.ArmReach(rep), np.random.default_rng(0)
    expert = A.Expert(env)
    obs, act = [], []
    for _ in range(DEMOS):
        env.reset(A.start_pose(rng, env.home), A.sample_target(rng, TRAIN_LOW, TRAIN_HIGH))
        expert.plan()
        for _ in range(A.STEPS):
            obs.append(env.obs())
            act.append(expert.act())
            env.step(act[-1])
    return np.array(obs), np.array(act)


def evaluate(policy, rep: str, condition: str) -> dict:
    from mjcourse.vla import actions as A

    env = A.ArmReach(rep, payload=1.0 if condition == "payload 1 kg" else 0.0,
                     frame_skip=2 * A.FRAME_SKIP if condition == "10 Hz" else A.FRAME_SKIP)
    steps = A.STEPS                                                          # 40 decisions in every condition
    low, high = (NEW_LOW, NEW_HIGH) if condition == "new region" else (TRAIN_LOW, TRAIN_HIGH)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(1000)                                        # held-out starts and targets
    ok, pos_err, jerk = [], [], []
    for _ in range(TESTS):
        env.reset(A.start_pose(rng, env.home), A.sample_target(rng, low, high))
        prev = None
        for _ in range(steps):
            a = np.clip(policy(env.obs()), -1, 1)
            if prev is not None:
                jerk.append(np.abs(a - prev).mean())
            prev = a
            env.step(a)
        ok.append(env.success())
        pos_err.append(env.errors()[0])
    return {"success": float(np.mean(ok)), "pos_err": float(np.median(pos_err)), "jerk": float(np.mean(jerk))}


KEEP_NO_VELOCITY = np.r_[np.ones(7), np.zeros(7), np.ones(10)].astype(bool)

def part2_job(args):
    chunk, seed = args
    import torch
    from torch import nn

    from mjcourse import tabletop as tt

    torch.set_num_threads(1)
    n_demos = 300 if FAST else 2000

    def onehot(task):
        v = np.zeros(7, np.float32)
        v[tt.OBJECTS.index(task[0])] = 1
        v[3 + list(tt.ZONES).index(task[1])] = 1
        return v

    xs, ys = [], []
    for i in range(n_demos):
        rng = np.random.default_rng([0, i])
        task = (tt.OBJECTS[rng.integers(3)], list(tt.ZONES)[rng.integers(4)])
        scene = tt.Tabletop()
        scene.reset(tt.sample_layout(rng, task))
        states, acts = [], []
        for _ in range(tt.EPISODE_STEPS):
            s = scene.state()
            states.append(np.r_[s, onehot(task)])
            acts.append(tt.expert(s, task))
            scene.step(acts[-1])
            if scene.success(task):
                break
        if not scene.success(task):
            continue
        padded = np.concatenate([acts, np.zeros((chunk, 2))])
        for t in range(len(states)):
            xs.append(states[t])
            ys.append(padded[t:t + chunk].ravel())
    torch.manual_seed(seed)
    x, y = torch.as_tensor(np.array(xs, np.float32)), torch.as_tensor(np.array(ys, np.float32))
    net = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(23, 256), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(256, 256), nn.ReLU(), nn.Linear(256, 2 * chunk), nn.Tanh())
    opt = torch.optim.Adam(net.parameters(), 1e-3)
    for _ in range(1500 if FAST else 15000):
        idx = torch.randint(0, len(x), (256,))
        loss = ((net(x[idx]) - y[idx]) ** 2).mean()
        opt.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        opt.step()
    out = {}
    for mode in ("first action", "open-loop chunk", "temporal ensemble"):
        if chunk == 1 and mode != "first action":
            continue
        ok = []
        for i in range(30 if FAST else 100):
            rng = np.random.default_rng([99, i])
            task = (tt.OBJECTS[rng.integers(3)], list(tt.ZONES)[rng.integers(4)])
            scene = tt.Tabletop()
            scene.reset(tt.sample_layout(rng, task))
            history, plan = [], []
            for t in range(tt.EPISODE_STEPS):
                with torch.no_grad():
                    c = net(torch.as_tensor(np.r_[scene.state(), onehot(task)], dtype=torch.float32)).numpy().reshape(chunk, 2)
                if mode == "first action":
                    a = c[0]
                elif mode == "open-loop chunk":
                    if not plan:
                        plan = list(c)
                    a = plan.pop(0)
                else:
                    history = [(t0, h) for t0, h in history if t - t0 < chunk] + [(t, c)]
                    a = np.mean([h[t - t0] for t0, h in history], axis=0)
                scene.step(a)
                if scene.success(task):
                    break
            ok.append(scene.success(task))
        out[mode] = float(np.mean(ok))
    return chunk, seed, out
```

```python file=src/mjcourse/vla/actions.py
from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass

import mujoco
import numpy as np

from mjcourse import kinematics, spatial
from mjcourse.paths import model_path

REPRESENTATIONS = ("torque", "joint_position", "joint_delta", "joint_velocity", "cartesian_delta", "cartesian_pose")
FRAME_SKIP, STEPS, MOVE_STEPS = 25, 40, 30            # 20 Hz policy, 2 s episodes, 1.5 s reference motion
BOX_LOW, BOX_HIGH = np.array([0.35, -0.25, 0.15]), np.array([0.60, 0.25, 0.45])
YAW_RANGE = 0.6
JOINT_STEP, JOINT_SPEED, EE_STEP, YAW_STEP = 0.05, 1.0, 0.02, 0.1
SUCCESS_POS, SUCCESS_YAW = 0.015, 0.1
OBS_DIM = 24


@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Target:
    pos: np.ndarray
    yaw: float


def sample_target(rng: np.random.Generator, low=BOX_LOW, high=BOX_HIGH) -> Target:
    return Target(rng.uniform(low, high), float(rng.uniform(-YAW_RANGE, YAW_RANGE)))


def _yaw_of(mat: np.ndarray) -> float:
    return float(np.arctan2(mat[1, 0], mat[0, 0]))


class ArmReach:
    def __init__(self, representation: str, payload: float = 0.0, frame_skip: int = FRAME_SKIP):
        if representation not in REPRESENTATIONS:
            raise ValueError(representation)
        self.rep, self.frame_skip = representation, frame_skip
        spec = mujoco.MjSpec.from_file(str(model_path("arm7")))
        if payload:
            body = spec.body("link7")
            body.add_geom(type=mujoco.mjtGeom.mjGEOM_SPHERE, size=[0.03, 0, 0], pos=[0, 0, 0.1], mass=payload,
                          contype=0, conaffinity=0, rgba=[0.9, 0.6, 0.1, 1])
        self.model = spec.compile()
        self.data = mujoco.MjData(self.model)
        # Gravity compensation uses the nominal arm, as a real controller with a fixed model would: a payload
        # is something the controller does not know about.
        self.nominal = mujoco.MjModel.from_xml_path(str(model_path("arm7")))
        self.nominal_data = mujoco.MjData(self.nominal)
        m = self.model
        self.site = m.site("ee").id
        self.lo, self.hi = m.jnt_range[:7].T
        self.limit = m.actuator_ctrlrange[:7, 1]
        mujoco.mj_resetDataKeyframe(m, self.data, m.key("home").id)
        mujoco.mj_forward(m, self.data)
        self.home = self.data.qpos[:7].copy()
        self.down = spatial.mat_to_quat(self.data.site_xmat[self.site].reshape(3, 3))     # tool down, yaw of home
        self.home_yaw = _yaw_of(self.data.site_xmat[self.site].reshape(3, 3))
        mass = np.zeros((m.nv, m.nv))
        mujoco.mj_fullM(m, self.data, mass)
        inertia = np.diag(mass)[:7]
        self.kp, self.kd = inertia * 400.0, inertia * 40.0

    # ------------------------------------------------------------------ state and observation
    def reset(self, q0: np.ndarray, target: Target) -> None:
        mujoco.mj_resetData(self.model, self.data)
        self.data.qpos[:7] = q0
        mujoco.mj_forward(self.model, self.data)
        self.target = target
        self.q_target = q0.copy()
        self.x_target = self.data.site_xpos[self.site].copy()
        self.yaw_target = self.yaw()

    def yaw(self) -> float:
        raw = _yaw_of(self.data.site_xmat[self.site].reshape(3, 3)) - self.home_yaw
        return float((raw + np.pi) % (2 * np.pi) - np.pi)

    def obs(self) -> np.ndarray:
        d, y, ty = self.data, self.yaw(), self.target.yaw
        return np.concatenate([d.qpos[:7], d.qvel[:7], d.site_xpos[self.site], [np.sin(y), np.cos(y)],
                               self.target.pos, [np.sin(ty), np.cos(ty)]]).astype(np.float32)

    def errors(self) -> tuple[float, float]:
        pos = float(np.linalg.norm(self.data.site_xpos[self.site] - self.target.pos))
        yaw = abs((self.yaw() - self.target.yaw + np.pi) % (2 * np.pi) - np.pi)
        return pos, yaw

    def success(self) -> bool:
        pos, yaw = self.errors()
        return pos < SUCCESS_POS and yaw < SUCCESS_YAW

    # ------------------------------------------------------------------ executing one action
    def _tool_quat(self, yaw: float) -> np.ndarray:
        return spatial.quat_mul(np.array([np.cos(yaw / 2), 0, 0, np.sin(yaw / 2)]), self.down)

    def _ik(self, pos: np.ndarray, yaw: float) -> np.ndarray:
        result = kinematics.solve_ik(self.model, "ee", pos, self._tool_quat(yaw), q_init=np.r_[self.q_target],
                                     joints=list(range(7)), max_iters=20)
        return np.clip(result.qpos[:7], self.lo, self.hi)

    def step(self, action: np.ndarray) -> None:
        a = np.clip(np.asarray(action, dtype=float), -1.0, 1.0)
        rep = self.rep
        if rep == "joint_position":
            self.q_target = self.lo + (a + 1) / 2 * (self.hi - self.lo)
        elif rep == "joint_delta":
            self.q_target = np.clip(self.q_target + JOINT_STEP * a, self.lo, self.hi)
        elif rep == "cartesian_delta":
            self.x_target = np.clip(self.x_target + EE_STEP * a[:3], BOX_LOW - 0.05, BOX_HIGH + 0.05)
            self.yaw_target = self.yaw_target + YAW_STEP * a[3]
            self.q_target = self._ik(self.x_target, self.yaw_target)
        elif rep == "cartesian_pose":
            self.x_target = BOX_LOW - 0.05 + (a[:3] + 1) / 2 * (BOX_HIGH - BOX_LOW + 0.1)
            self.yaw_target = a[3] * YAW_RANGE * 1.2
            self.q_target = self._ik(self.x_target, self.yaw_target)
        m, d, nd = self.model, self.data, self.nominal_data
        for _ in range(self.frame_skip):
            mujoco.mj_forward(m, d)
            nd.qpos[:], nd.qvel[:] = d.qpos[:7], d.qvel[:7]
            mujoco.mj_forward(self.nominal, nd)
            bias = nd.qfrc_bias[:7]                                        # the nominal model's gravity and Coriolis
            if rep == "torque":
                tau = a * self.limit
            elif rep == "joint_velocity":
                tau = bias + self.kd * 2 * (JOINT_SPEED * a - d.qvel[:7])
            else:
                tau = bias + self.kp * (self.q_target - d.qpos[:7]) - self.kd * d.qvel[:7]
            d.ctrl[:7] = np.clip(tau, -self.limit, self.limit)
            mujoco.mj_step(m, d)

    @property
    def action_dim(self) -> int:
        return 4 if self.rep.startswith("cartesian") else 7


def min_jerk(s: float) -> float:
    s = min(max(s, 0.0), 1.0)
    return 10 * s**3 - 15 * s**4 + 6 * s**5


class Expert:
    def __init__(self, env: ArmReach):
        self.env = env

    def plan(self) -> None:
        env = self.env
        x0, y0 = env.data.site_xpos[env.site].copy(), env.yaw()
        q = env.data.qpos[:7].copy()
        self.x_ref, self.yaw_ref, self.q_ref = [], [], []
        saved = env.q_target.copy()
        for k in range(STEPS + 2):
            s = min_jerk(k / MOVE_STEPS)
            x = x0 + s * (env.target.pos - x0)
            yaw = y0 + s * ((env.target.yaw - y0 + np.pi) % (2 * np.pi) - np.pi)
            env.q_target = q
            q = env._ik(x, yaw)
            self.x_ref.append(x)
            self.yaw_ref.append(yaw)
            self.q_ref.append(q)
        env.q_target = saved
        self.k = 0

    def act(self) -> np.ndarray:
        env, k = self.env, self.k
        self.k += 1
        q_next, x_next, y_next = self.q_ref[k + 1], self.x_ref[k + 1], self.yaw_ref[k + 1]
        dt = env.frame_skip * env.model.opt.timestep
        rep = env.rep
        if rep == "joint_position":
            a = 2 * (q_next - env.lo) / (env.hi - env.lo) - 1
        elif rep == "joint_delta":
            a = (q_next - env.q_target) / JOINT_STEP
        elif rep == "joint_velocity":
            a = ((q_next - env.data.qpos[:7]) / dt) / JOINT_SPEED
        elif rep == "cartesian_delta":
            a = np.r_[(x_next - env.x_target) / EE_STEP, (y_next - env.yaw_target) / YAW_STEP]
        elif rep == "cartesian_pose":
            a = np.r_[2 * (x_next - (BOX_LOW - 0.05)) / (BOX_HIGH - BOX_LOW + 0.1) - 1, y_next / (YAW_RANGE * 1.2)]
        else:                                                               # torque: computed torque on the reference
            m, d = env.model, env.data
            mujoco.mj_forward(m, d)
            mass = np.zeros((m.nv, m.nv))
            mujoco.mj_fullM(m, d, mass)
            qd_ref = (q_next - self.q_ref[k]) / dt
            acc = 225.0 * (q_next - d.qpos[:7]) + 30.0 * (qd_ref - d.qvel[:7])    # 15 rad/s: a 20 Hz torque loop cannot be stiffer
            a = (mass[:7, :7] @ acc + d.qfrc_bias[:7]) / env.limit
        return np.clip(a, -1.0, 1.0)


def start_pose(rng: np.random.Generator, home: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    return home + rng.uniform(-0.15, 0.15, 7)
```

## Debugging

**A cloned policy has a tiny training loss and fails in closed loop.** Look for a shortcut: an input that predicts the expert's action on the demonstrations without being its cause (here the joint velocities). Ablate it and compare closed-loop success, not loss.

**A policy works at one control rate and not at another.** Check both the action's meaning (increments scale with the step, velocities do not) and the observation's distribution (velocities and accelerations change with the rate).

**A torque policy sags under load.** It has learned the nominal arm's gravity torques. Any representation with a servo underneath absorbs part of a load error; a torque policy absorbs none.
