## Core concept

A benchmark is a set of choices with a name on it. Any of those choices can change between versions while the name stays almost the same:

- the task;
- what the agent observes;
- what it controls and how often;
- when an episode ends, and whether that end counts as failure or only as a time limit;
- how episodes start;
- how a method is evaluated;
- what number is reported.

This lesson reads benchmarks along those seven axes, and measures how far apart two versions of "the same" environments are: Gymnasium's MuJoCo locomotion and control tasks in versions 4 and 5, run side by side on the same MuJoCo release.

> [!established] Seven questions to ask of any benchmark
>
> | Axis | Question |
> |---|---|
> | task | which model (file and version), which objective, which variations |
> | observation | which quantities, in which order, with what noise, from which state (Lesson 20.1: positions read after `mj_step` are one step old) |
> | action | which space (torque, position target, end-effector delta), its limits, the control period |
> | episode | the time limit, the termination conditions, and whether a time limit is reported as truncation (Lesson 13.1) |
> | initialization | the distribution of initial states, and their seeds |
> | evaluation | which starts or seeds, how many episodes, train against held-out splits, deterministic or stochastic policy |
> | metric | return or success, its exact definition, and its aggregation across seeds and tasks (Lesson 21.2) |

## Mathematics

### Same names, different benchmarks

Gymnasium 1.3.0 ships each MuJoCo task in several versions; versions 4 and 5 both run on MuJoCo 3.14.0. As specified:

| Task | Observation v4 / v5 | Actions | Control period | Time limit | Same compiled model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hopper | 11 / 11 | 3 | 0.008 s | 1000 | yes |
| Walker2d | 17 / 17 | 6 | 0.008 s | 1000 | **no** |
| HalfCheetah | 17 / 17 | 6 | 0.050 s | 1000 | yes |
| Ant | **27 / 105** | 8 | 0.050 s | 1000 | yes |
| Humanoid | **376 / 348** | 17 | 0.015 s | 1000 | yes |
| Swimmer | 8 / 8 | 2 | 0.040 s | 1000 | yes |
| Reacher | **11 / 10** | 2 | 0.020 s | 50 | yes |
| InvertedPendulum | 4 / 4 | 1 | 0.040 s | 1000 | yes |

"Same compiled model" compares `mjcourse.experiment.model_fingerprint` of the two environments' models, which covers every number of the compiled model. Run with uniform random actions and with zero actions, 50 seeds each, the versions give these mean episode lengths and returns:

| Task | random v4 | random v5 | zero v4 | zero v5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hopper | 23.3 steps, 19.1 | 23.3 steps, 18.1 | 152.2 steps, 152.0 | 152.2 steps, 151.0 |
| Walker2d | 22.2 steps, 3.4 | 21.9 steps, 2.2 | 109.2 steps, 94.1 | 115.5 steps, 93.6 |
| Ant | 216.8 steps, −75.6 | 216.8 steps, −76.8 | 1000 steps, 1000.2 | 1000 steps, 994.2 |
| Humanoid | 23.1 steps, 116.8 | 23.1 steps, 108.7 | 40.3 steps, 207.9 | 40.3 steps, 198.7 |
| InvertedPendulum | 6.8 steps, 6.8 | 6.8 steps, 5.8 | 27.0 steps, 27.0 | 27.0 steps, 26.0 |

HalfCheetah, Swimmer and Reacher gave identical numbers in both versions for these two policies. The reset distributions are identical too: the mean standard deviation of the initial `qpos` and `qvel` over 200 resets matches to five decimals for every task, and the stored termination ranges (Hopper's healthy height 0.7 and up, Walker2d's 0.8 to 2.0, and so on) are the same.

Each difference has a cause, and Gymnasium's own version history in the installed source names it:

- **The terminal step.** In v4 the survival reward was paid on every step, including the one on which the robot fell; v5 pays it only while healthy. Equal lengths, one point less return: InvertedPendulum's 27 steps return 27.0 in v4 and 26.0 in v5.
- **Walker2d's model.** In v4 the two feet had different friction (1.9 and 0.9); v5 makes them equal. The fingerprints differ, and episodes under zero actions last 115.5 instead of 109.2 steps.
- **Reward terms.** Ant v5 always includes the contact cost; Humanoid v5 restores it. Zero-action Ant returns 994.2 instead of 1000.2.
- **Observations.** Ant v4 left contact forces out by default and v5 puts them in, minus the world body's always-zero entries. Humanoid v5 removes always-zero entries. Reacher v5 drops the fingertip's height, which is always zero. A policy trained on one version cannot even be run on the other.

None of these is visible in a results table that says "Hopper" or "Ant". A return on Humanoid differs by about 8 to 9 between the versions for the same behaviour here, and an observation space can change size.

### The same problem in robot learning benchmarks

Manipulation benchmarks built on MuJoCo and other simulators have the same structure and the same hazard. Meta-World's task definitions changed between releases, CALVIN's splits (training on environments A, B and C and testing on D, against training on all four) answer different questions, LIBERO-PRO and LIBERO-Plus perturb LIBERO's initial states and instructions to test what the original protocol could not, and SimplerEnv was built to measure how well simulated success predicts real success. The reading list's benchmark section names each paper and the protocol question to ask of it.

> [!recommendation] Before comparing two numbers
> Check, for both:
> - the environment id with its version;
> - the library and MuJoCo versions;
> - a fingerprint of the compiled model;
> - the observation and action spaces;
> - the control period and the time limit;
> - the reward terms and termination;
> - the reset distribution;
> - the evaluation protocol, meaning seeds, episodes and splits.
>
> If any differ, the numbers come from different benchmarks; say so in the table, or rerun the baseline. When you build a benchmark, version it, write each of the seven axes into its documentation, and record them in every run (Lesson 21.1).

```python file=examples/l21_3_benchmark_anatomy.py
"""Lesson 21.3: one task name, two benchmarks: Gymnasium's MuJoCo environments v4 against v5 (Level 21).

INPUT   Gymnasium's MuJoCo environments in versions 4 and 5 (the installed gymnasium release, MuJoCo 3.14.0):
        Hopper, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Ant, Humanoid, Swimmer, Reacher, InvertedPendulum
PROCESS (1) for each pair: observation and action sizes, control timestep, frame skip, time limit, and whether
            the compiled models are identical (mjcourse.experiment.model_fingerprint);
        (2) episodes under uniform random actions and under zero actions, 50 seeds each: mean length and return;
        (3) the initial-state distribution: the standard deviation of qpos and qvel over 200 resets;
        (4) the termination settings the two versions store (healthy z range, termination on unhealthy states)
OUTPUT  printed tables

Run:  python examples/l21_3_benchmark_anatomy.py          (about 2 minutes)
"""

import os
import warnings

import gymnasium as gym
import numpy as np

from mjcourse.experiment import model_fingerprint

warnings.filterwarnings("ignore")                               # v4 environments announce their deprecation
FAST = os.environ.get("MJC_FAST") == "1"
TASKS = ("Hopper", "Walker2d", "HalfCheetah", "Ant", "Humanoid", "Swimmer", "Reacher", "InvertedPendulum")
EPISODES = 5 if FAST else 50


def episodes(env_id: str, policy: str) -> tuple[float, float]:
    env = gym.make(env_id)
    lengths, returns = [], []
    for seed in range(EPISODES):
        obs, _ = env.reset(seed=seed)
        env.action_space.seed(seed)
        done, steps, total = False, 0, 0.0
        while not done:
            action = env.action_space.sample() if policy == "random" else np.zeros(env.action_space.shape)
            obs, reward, terminated, truncated, _ = env.step(action)
            total, steps, done = total + reward, steps + 1, terminated or truncated
        lengths.append(steps)
        returns.append(total)
    env.close()
    return float(np.mean(lengths)), float(np.mean(returns))


def reset_spread(env_id: str, n: int) -> tuple[float, float]:
    env = gym.make(env_id)
    qpos, qvel = [], []
    for seed in range(n):
        env.reset(seed=seed)
        qpos.append(env.unwrapped.data.qpos.copy())
        qvel.append(env.unwrapped.data.qvel.copy())
    env.close()
    return float(np.std(qpos, axis=0).mean()), float(np.std(qvel, axis=0).mean())


if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(f"gymnasium {gym.__version__}; {EPISODES} episodes per row")
    print("(1) the environments as specified")
    print(f"    {'task':<18}{'obs v4/v5':>11}{'act':>5}{'dt v4/v5':>14}{'frame skip':>12}{'time limit':>12}  same compiled model")
    for task in TASKS:
        envs = [gym.make(f"{task}-v{v}") for v in (4, 5)]
        u = [e.unwrapped for e in envs]
        same = model_fingerprint(u[0].model) == model_fingerprint(u[1].model)
        print(f"    {task:<18}{envs[0].observation_space.shape[0]:>5}/{envs[1].observation_space.shape[0]:<5}"
              f"{envs[0].action_space.shape[0]:>5}{u[0].dt:>8.3f}/{u[1].dt:<6.3f}{u[0].frame_skip:>6}/{u[1].frame_skip:<5}"
              f"{envs[0].spec.max_episode_steps:>6}/{envs[1].spec.max_episode_steps:<5}  {'yes' if same else 'no'}")
        for e in envs:
            e.close()
    print("(2) mean episode length and return, uniform random actions and zero actions")
    print(f"    {'task':<18}{'random v4':>22}{'random v5':>22}{'zero v4':>22}{'zero v5':>22}")
    for task in TASKS:
        cells = []
        for policy in ("random", "zero"):
            for v in (4, 5):
                length, ret = episodes(f"{task}-v{v}", policy)
                cells.append(f"{length:7.1f} steps {ret:9.1f}")
        print(f"    {task:<18}" + "".join(f"{c:>22}" for c in cells))
    print("(3) initial states: mean standard deviation over 200 resets, qpos and qvel")
    for task in TASKS:
        cells = [reset_spread(f"{task}-v{v}", 20 if FAST else 200) for v in (4, 5)]
        print(f"    {task:<18} v4 {cells[0][0]:.5f}, {cells[0][1]:.5f}   v5 {cells[1][0]:.5f}, {cells[1][1]:.5f}")
    print("(4) termination settings stored by the environments")
    for task in ("Hopper", "Walker2d", "Ant", "Humanoid"):
        cells = []
        for v in (4, 5):
            u = gym.make(f"{task}-v{v}").unwrapped
            z = getattr(u, "_healthy_z_range", None)
            cells.append(f"v{v}: healthy z {z}, terminate when unhealthy {getattr(u, '_terminate_when_unhealthy', None)}")
        print(f"    {task:<10} " + "; ".join(cells))
```

## Debugging

**Your reproduction scores lower than the paper by a constant.** Check the version: a reward term, such as the survival reward on the terminal step, may differ.

**A trained policy cannot be loaded into the new environment.** The observation space changed size between versions (Ant, Humanoid, Reacher).

**Two runs of "the same" environment disagree with the same seeds.** Compare model fingerprints and library versions; Walker2d's feet changed friction between versions.

**A baseline from a paper cannot be matched at all.** The paper may not say which version, split or protocol it used. Write to the authors, or report the mismatch.

## Research connection

> [!research] Reporting benchmark results
> Towers et al. (Gymnasium, arXiv:2407.17032, in the reading list) present the library as a standard interface with tools for reproducible research. The version suffix of an environment id works as one such tool: as measured here, v4 and v5 coexist and differ, so a version change is a warning that numbers across it are not comparable (inference). Every results table in a paper should carry the version, and every comparison against a published number should check it first.
