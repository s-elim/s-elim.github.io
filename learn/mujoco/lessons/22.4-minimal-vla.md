## Core concept

A VLA is trained like any imitation policy: collect demonstrations of a task described in words, then fit the network so that its action matches the demonstrator's on every recorded frame. The MiniVLA of Lesson 22.2 is trained this way on 3000 demonstrations of the tabletop, in about 14 minutes on 16 CPU cores, and evaluated by running it in MuJoCo on layouts it has never seen.

The first version failed completely: 1 success in 100 episodes, for all three vision encoders, after reaching a training loss lower than that of models that later succeeded 96% of the time. This lesson reproduces that failure and finds its cause, a shortcut through the pusher's velocity that the network took because the data allowed it. The cause is a standard one in imitation learning, and finding it took the same steps a research project would: an oracle that rules out vision, an offline-against-online comparison, and an intervention on one input.

## Visual intuition

One held-out task, "get the green puck into the right zone", run for 6 s from the same start by the demonstrator and by two patch MiniVLAs that differ only in whether the pusher's velocity is part of their input. The demonstrator (left) and the position-only model (right) go around the green puck and push it into the zone. The model that sees its velocity (centre) draws a smooth curve of about the right shape and length, past the puck, without touching it: it reproduces how the demonstrator moves, not where it moves to.

## Mathematics

### Data and objective

Each demonstration samples one of the 216 training instructions (9 object-zone pairs × 6 templates × 4 name variants; Lesson 22.5 holds out the rest), a layout, and runs the demonstrator of `mjcourse.tabletop.expert` for up to 12 s, recording at 10 Hz the 64 × 64 top-camera image $o_t$, the instruction $\ell$, the pusher's state $s_t$ and the demonstrator's action $a^*_t \in [-1,1]^2$ (velocity as a fraction of 0.2 m/s). Of 3000 episodes, 2995 succeed and are kept: 169 337 frames. Behaviour cloning minimizes

$$\mathcal L(\theta) = \mathbb E_{(o_t,\ell,s_t,a^*_t)}\big\|\pi_\theta(o_t,\ell,s_t) - a^*_t\big\|^2$$

with AdamW (learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, cosine decay), batches of 64, gradient clipping at 1, and random image shifts of up to 4 pixels, for 30 000 steps (11 passes over the frames).

### Three encoders, two state inputs, three seeds

Output (1) of the diagnosis script collects every trained variant. Success is the share of episodes in which the named puck ends within 5 cm of its zone's centre within 12 s, on layouts from seeds the demonstrations never used (100 for training instructions, 60 for each held-out language split of Lesson 22.5).

| Vision | State input | Seed | Final loss | Success, training instructions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| patch | position and velocity | 0 | 0.0173 | 0.01 |
| keypoint | position and velocity | 0 | 0.0179 | 0.01 |
| oracle (true puck positions) | position and velocity | 0 | 0.0160 | 0.01 |
| patch | position | 0, 1, 2 | 0.0155, 0.0151, 0.0142 | 0.95, 0.92, 0.99 |
| keypoint | position | 0, 1, 2 | 0.0315, 0.0111, 0.0311 | 0.58, 0.96, 0.62 |
| oracle | position | 0, 1, 2 | 0.0068, 0.0047, 0.0067 | 0.99, 1.00, 1.00 |

Three findings, in the order they were obtained.

The velocity models fail with every encoder, including the oracle, which never sees an image. A failure that survives perfect perception is not a perception failure; this one test removed vision from the list of suspects.

The training loss does not order the models by success. The failed velocity models reach 0.016 to 0.018, lower than two keypoint runs (0.031) that succeed about 60% of the time.

Removing the velocity fixes all three encoders. The patch model, chosen for the rest of Level 22, reaches 0.92 to 0.99 over three seeds. The keypoint encoder is unstable: two seeds stall at a loss of 0.031 and one reaches 0.011; a single-seed result would have reported either 0.58 or 0.96.

### The shortcut

The pusher is driven by velocity actuators that track their command within a few milliseconds, and the demonstrator's commands change smoothly. The pusher's velocity at frame $t$ is therefore almost the demonstrator's action at frame $t-1$, and that action is almost the action at frame $t$: copying the previous action has an error of 0.071 (output 2), against actions of mean norm 0.58. A network given the velocity can fit the data to that accuracy without learning where any puck is. De Haan et al. (arXiv:1905.11979) call the general phenomenon causal confusion, in which "access to more information can yield worse performance"; Wen et al. (arXiv:2010.14876) describe this instance, an imitator that "learns to cheat by predicting the expert's previous action".

Output (2) confirms it offline, on 40 held-out demonstrations:

| Model | Error at each episode's first frame (pusher at rest) | Error at later frames |
|---|---|---|
| patch, position and velocity | 0.653 | 0.058 |
| patch, position only | 0.079 | 0.090 |
| copying the previous action | (none) | 0.071 |

On later frames the velocity model is the most accurate of the three, better even than copying. At the first frame, where the velocity is zero and carries no information, its error is larger than the actions themselves. Output (3) intervenes: setting the velocity input to zero changes its action by 0.75 on average, and its action lies closer to the velocity it is given (0.072) than the demonstrator's action does (0.092).

In closed loop, the velocity it is given is its own previous action. Output (4) runs both models from rest on 20 held-out tasks and asks the demonstrator what it would have done at each state the model visits:

| Model | Disagreement with the demonstrator, 0 to 1 s | 1 to 2 s | 2 to 3 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| patch, position and velocity | 0.739 | 1.109 | 1.300 |
| patch, position only | 0.129 | 0.163 | 0.184 |

An offline error of 0.058 becomes 0.74 in the first second and keeps growing: the model starts in the wrong direction, its velocity input then tells it to continue, and it does.

> [!recommendation] Before training any imitation policy
> List every input that is a consequence of the previous action (velocities, previous actions, filtered signals) and ask whether it predicts the next action without the task-relevant inputs. If it does, remove it, or make it less predictive (Wen et al. remove the previous-action information from the representation adversarially). Then measure the first-frame error and the on-policy disagreement, never only the held-out loss on demonstration frames.

```python file=examples/l22_4_train_minivla.py
import argparse
import json
import multiprocessing
import os
import time
from concurrent.futures import ProcessPoolExecutor
from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np
import torch

from mjcourse import tabletop as tt
from mjcourse.vla import data, language, train
from mjcourse.vla.model import VLAConfig

FAST = os.environ.get("MJC_FAST") == "1"
OUT = Path(__file__).resolve().parents[1] / "runs" / "vla"
EPISODES = {"train": 2, "paraphrase": 1, "synonym": 1, "composition": 1} if FAST else {"train": 100, "paraphrase": 60, "synonym": 60, "composition": 60}


def evaluate_one(args) -> dict:
    model_path, split, index = args
    torch.set_num_threads(1)
    model = torch.load(model_path, weights_only=False)
    rng = np.random.default_rng([2026, index])                        # layouts the demonstrations never used
    instructions = language.instructions(split)
    text, task = instructions[rng.integers(len(instructions))]
    layout = tt.sample_layout(rng, task)
    result = train.run_episode(train.VLAPolicy(model, language.default_tokenizer()), text, task, layout)
    return {"split": split, "index": index, "text": text, "task": list(task), "success": bool(result["success"]),
            "steps": result["steps"]}


def main() -> None:
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    parser.add_argument("--vision", default="keypoint", choices=["patch", "keypoint", "oracle"])
    parser.add_argument("--seed", type=int, default=0)
    parser.add_argument("--steps", type=int, default=100 if FAST else 30000)
    parser.add_argument("--demos", type=int, default=20 if FAST else 3000)
    parser.add_argument("--state", type=int, default=2, choices=[2, 4], help="2: pusher position (the course default); 4: position and velocity (Lesson 22.4, the copycat)")
    args = parser.parse_args()
    OUT.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)
    cpus = int(os.environ.get("SLURM_CPUS_PER_TASK", min(16, os.cpu_count() or 1)))
    torch.set_num_threads(cpus)
    name = (f"minivla_{args.vision}_s{args.seed}" + ("" if args.demos == 3000 else f"_d{args.demos}")
            + ("" if args.state == 4 else "_pos"))
    episodes = data.cached(0, 3000, "train")[:args.demos]
    tokenizer = language.default_tokenizer()
    arrays = data.to_arrays(episodes, tokenizer, 1)
    print(f"{name}: {sum(e['success'] for e in episodes)} successful demonstrations, {len(arrays['images'])} frames", flush=True)
    start = time.perf_counter()
    model, losses = train.fit(arrays, len(tokenizer.vocab), steps=args.steps, seed=args.seed,
                              cfg=VLAConfig(vocab=len(tokenizer.vocab), chunk=1, vision=args.vision, state_dim=args.state),
                              log=lambda m: print(m, flush=True))
    seconds = time.perf_counter() - start
    path = OUT / f"{name}.pt"
    torch.save(model, path)
    jobs = [(str(path), split, i) for split, n in EPISODES.items() for i in range(n)]
    with ProcessPoolExecutor(cpus, mp_context=multiprocessing.get_context("spawn")) as pool:
        episodes_out = list(pool.map(evaluate_one, jobs, chunksize=2))
    summary = {split: float(np.mean([e["success"] for e in episodes_out if e["split"] == split])) for split in EPISODES}
    record = {"vision": args.vision, "seed": args.seed, "steps": args.steps, "demos": args.demos, "state": args.state,
              "train_seconds": seconds,
              "final_loss": float(np.mean(losses[-1000:])), "summary": summary, "episodes": episodes_out}
    (OUT / f"{name}.json").write_text(json.dumps(record, indent=1))
    print(json.dumps({k: v for k, v in record.items() if k != "episodes"}), flush=True)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

```python file=src/mjcourse/vla/data.py
from __future__ import annotations

import multiprocessing
import os
from concurrent.futures import ProcessPoolExecutor

import numpy as np

from mjcourse import tabletop as tt
from mjcourse.vla import language


def episode(seed: int, index: int, split: str = "train", variation: tt.Variation | None = None, camera: str = "top"):
    rng = np.random.default_rng([seed, index])
    text, task = language.instructions(split)[rng.integers(len(language.instructions(split)))]
    scene = tt.Tabletop(variation)
    scene.reset(tt.sample_layout(rng, task))
    images, states, actions, pucks = [], [], [], []
    for _ in range(tt.EPISODE_STEPS):
        s = scene.state()
        a = tt.expert(s, task)
        images.append(scene.render(camera, 64))
        states.append(s[:4])
        pucks.append(np.r_[s[4:6], s[8:10], s[12:14]])
        actions.append(a)
        scene.step(a)
        if scene.success(task) and np.linalg.norm(scene.state()[4 + 4 * tt.OBJECTS.index(task[0]) + 2:][:2]) < 0.02:
            break
    ok = scene.success(task)
    scene.close()
    return {"text": text, "task": task, "images": np.array(images), "states": np.array(states, np.float32),
            "actions": np.array(actions, np.float32), "pucks": np.array(pucks, np.float32), "success": ok}


def generate(seed: int, n: int, split: str = "train", workers: int | None = None) -> list[dict]:
    workers = workers or min(32, os.cpu_count() or 1)
    with ProcessPoolExecutor(workers, mp_context=multiprocessing.get_context("spawn")) as pool:
        return list(pool.map(episode, [seed] * n, range(n), [split] * n, chunksize=4))


def cached(seed: int, n: int, split: str = "train") -> list[dict]:
    import pickle
    from pathlib import Path

    path = Path(__file__).resolve().parents[3] / "runs" / "vla" / f"demos_{split}_{seed}_{n}.pkl"
    if not path.exists():
        path.parent.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)
        path.write_bytes(pickle.dumps(generate(seed, n, split)))
    return pickle.loads(path.read_bytes())


def to_arrays(episodes: list[dict], tokenizer, chunk: int) -> dict:
    images, words, states, targets, masks, pucks, tasks = [], [], [], [], [], [], []
    for ep in episodes:
        if not ep["success"]:
            continue
        t_len = len(ep["actions"])
        tokens = tokenizer(ep["text"])
        padded = np.concatenate([ep["actions"], np.zeros((chunk, 2), np.float32)])
        task = [tt.OBJECTS.index(ep["task"][0]), list(tt.ZONES).index(ep["task"][1])]
        for t in range(t_len):
            images.append(ep["images"][t])
            words.append(tokens)
            states.append(ep["states"][t])
            targets.append(padded[t:t + chunk])
            masks.append((np.arange(chunk) + t < t_len).astype(np.float32))
            pucks.append(ep["pucks"][t])
            tasks.append(task)
    return {"images": np.array(images), "words": np.array(words), "states": np.array(states),
            "targets": np.array(targets), "masks": np.array(masks), "pucks": np.array(pucks), "tasks": np.array(tasks)}
```

```python file=src/mjcourse/vla/train.py
from __future__ import annotations

import time

import numpy as np
import torch
from torch.nn import functional as F

from mjcourse import tabletop as tt
from mjcourse.vla.model import MiniVLA, VLAConfig

STATE_SCALE = np.array([1 / 0.3, 1 / 0.3, 1 / 0.2, 1 / 0.2], np.float32)   # pusher x, y (m), vx, vy (m/s) to about +-1


def _batch(arrays, idx, shift: int, state_dim: int = 4):
    img = torch.as_tensor(arrays["images"][idx]).permute(0, 3, 1, 2).float() / 255.0
    if shift:
        img = F.pad(img, (shift,) * 4, mode="replicate")
        dx, dy = np.random.randint(0, 2 * shift + 1, 2)
        img = img[:, :, dy:dy + 64, dx:dx + 64]
    states = (arrays["states"][idx] * STATE_SCALE)[:, :state_dim]
    return (img, torch.as_tensor(arrays["words"][idx]), torch.as_tensor(states),
            torch.as_tensor(arrays["targets"][idx]), torch.as_tensor(arrays["masks"][idx]),
            torch.as_tensor(arrays["pucks"][idx]))


def fit(arrays: dict, vocab: int, steps: int = 6000, batch: int = 64, lr: float = 3e-4, seed: int = 0,
        shift: int = 4, cfg: VLAConfig | None = None, log=None) -> tuple[MiniVLA, list[float]]:
    torch.manual_seed(seed)
    np.random.seed(seed)
    model = MiniVLA(cfg or VLAConfig(vocab=vocab))
    opt = torch.optim.AdamW(model.parameters(), lr=lr, weight_decay=1e-4)
    sched = torch.optim.lr_scheduler.CosineAnnealingLR(opt, steps)
    n, losses, start = len(arrays["images"]), [], time.perf_counter()
    for step in range(steps):
        idx = np.random.randint(0, n, batch)
        img, words, state, target, mask, pucks = _batch(arrays, idx, shift, model.cfg.state_dim)
        pred = model(img, words, state, pucks)
        loss = (((pred - target) ** 2).sum(-1) * mask).sum() / mask.sum()
        opt.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), 1.0)
        opt.step()
        sched.step()
        losses.append(float(loss.detach()))
        if log and (step + 1) % 1000 == 0:
            log(f"step {step + 1}: loss {np.mean(losses[-1000:]):.4f} ({time.perf_counter() - start:.0f} s)")
    return model, losses


class VLAPolicy:
    def __init__(self, model: MiniVLA, tokenizer, ensemble: bool = False, decay: float = 0.5):
        self.model, self.tokenizer, self.ensemble, self.decay = model.eval(), tokenizer, ensemble, decay
        self.history: list[tuple[int, np.ndarray]] = []

    def reset(self) -> None:
        self.history = []

    @torch.no_grad()
    def chunk(self, image: np.ndarray, text: str, state: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
        img = torch.as_tensor(image).permute(2, 0, 1).float().unsqueeze(0) / 255.0
        words = torch.as_tensor(self.tokenizer(text)).unsqueeze(0)
        s = torch.as_tensor((state[:4] * STATE_SCALE)[:self.model.cfg.state_dim].astype(np.float32)).unsqueeze(0)
        pucks = None
        if self.model.cfg.vision == "oracle":                               # privileged: diagnosis only
            pucks = torch.as_tensor(state[[4, 5, 8, 9, 12, 13]].astype(np.float32)).unsqueeze(0)
        return self.model(img, words, s, pucks)[0].numpy()

    def act(self, t: int, image: np.ndarray, text: str, state: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
        chunk = self.chunk(image, text, state)
        if not self.ensemble:
            return chunk[0]
        self.history = [(t0, c) for t0, c in self.history if t - t0 < len(c)] + [(t, chunk)]
        preds = np.array([c[t - t0] for t0, c in self.history])
        weights = np.exp(-self.decay * np.arange(len(preds))[::-1])        # older chunks weigh more in ACT; see 22.3
        return (weights[:, None] * preds).sum(0) / weights.sum()


def run_episode(policy: VLAPolicy, text: str, task, layout: tt.Layout, variation: tt.Variation | None = None,
                camera: str = "top", record: bool = False) -> dict:
    scene = tt.Tabletop(variation)
    scene.reset(layout)
    policy.reset()
    log = {"states": [], "actions": [], "images": []}
    for t in range(tt.EPISODE_STEPS):
        s = scene.state()
        image = scene.render(camera, 64)
        a = policy.act(t, image, text, s)
        if record:
            log["states"].append(s)
            log["actions"].append(a)
            log["images"].append(image)
        scene.step(a)
        if scene.success(task) and np.linalg.norm(scene.state()[4 + 4 * tt.OBJECTS.index(task[0]) + 2:][:2]) < 0.02:
            break
    out = {"success": scene.success(task), "steps": t + 1, "final": scene.state(), **({"log": log} if record else {})}
    scene.close()
    return out
```

```python file=examples/l22_4_copycat.py
def predict(model, frames: list[tuple], tokenizer, zero_velocity: bool = False) -> np.ndarray:
    out = []
    with torch.no_grad():
        for image, text, state in frames:
            img = torch.as_tensor(image).permute(2, 0, 1).float().unsqueeze(0) / 255.0
            words = torch.as_tensor(tokenizer(text)).unsqueeze(0)
            s = (state[:4] * train.STATE_SCALE).astype(np.float32)
            if zero_velocity:
                s = s * np.array([1, 1, 0, 0], np.float32)
            out.append(model(img, words, torch.as_tensor(s[:model.cfg.state_dim]).unsqueeze(0))[0, 0].numpy())
    return np.array(out)

            policy = train.VLAPolicy(model, tokenizer)
            for t in range(10 * seconds):

                scene.step(a)
            scene.close()
        per_second = errors.reshape(tasks, seconds, 10).mean((0, 2))
        print(f"  {name:<34}" + "".join(f"{e:>10.3f}" for e in per_second))
```

## Debugging

**The demonstrator decides what can be imitated.** The tabletop's first demonstrator switched between an approach mode and a push mode at a distance threshold. Its actions jumped at the switch, and policies cloned from its state did poorly; collecting corrections with DAgger made them worse, because the corrections near the threshold contradicted each other. The demonstrator used here is one continuous velocity field: approach the point behind the puck, slide around it, push. Check a demonstrator's imitability before training a VLA on it: clone it from the true state first, where perception cannot be the problem.

**Low loss, no success.** Lower loss means a closer fit to the demonstration frames, which the policy will not visit once its own errors move it elsewhere. Evaluate in closed loop, and when closed loop fails, compare the first-frame error and the on-policy disagreement (outputs 2 and 4) with the held-out loss.

**One seed lied twice.** Keypoint seed 0 alone says the encoder reaches 0.58; seed 1 alone says 0.96. Three seeds are the minimum before saying anything about an encoder, and the spread across seeds belongs in the result.

## Scaling to the 7-DOF Franka manipulator in MuJoCo

The planar pusher isolates causal confusion in its purest form. Real robot deployments, however, operate multi-joint manipulators. The course companion code provides a full 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda simulation in MuJoCo (`franka_vla.xml` and `mjcourse.vla.franka.FrankaVLA`).

The 7-DOF Franka model incorporates:
1. Kinematic chain: seven revolute joints (shoulder yaw/pitch, arm roll, elbow pitch, forearm roll, wrist pitch, flange roll) matching Franka Panda limits and inertia.
2. End-effector: parallel-jaw gripper actuated with symmetric tendon coupling, tool center point (TCP) site, and in-hand wrist camera (`gripper/wrist`).
3. Dual-camera perception: global third-person context from the overhead camera (`top`) paired with egocentric visual feedback from the wrist camera.
4. Operational-space tracking: 7-D Cartesian delta actions $(\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z, \Delta \text{roll}, \Delta \text{pitch}, \Delta \text{yaw}, \text{grip})$ converted to joint commands through damped least-squares Jacobian inverse kinematics with nullspace posture regularization.

The causal confusion failure observed on the planar pusher translates directly to the Franka manipulator. If joint velocities $\dot{q} \in \mathbb{R}^7$ or TCP velocities are provided in the proprioceptive state token, the policy learns to integrate velocity commands rather than grounding the target objects in the image tokens. Restricting the Franka proprioceptive vector to position-only quantities (TCP coordinates $p \in \mathbb{R}^3$, orientation quaternion $q \in \mathbb{H}$, gripper width $g \in \mathbb{R}$, and joint angles $q_{\text{arm}} \in \mathbb{R}^7$, 15 dimensions total) forces the policy to ground its actions in the visual and language tokens.

```python file=examples/l22_4_franka_vla.py
from __future__ import annotations

import os
import numpy as np

from mjcourse.vla.franka import FrankaVLA, franka_expert_action


FAST = os.environ.get("MJC_FAST") == "1"
STEPS = 20 if FAST else 120


def main() -> None:
    print("Initializing 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda VLA simulator in MuJoCo...")
    env = FrankaVLA()

    # (1) Topology
    nq, nv, nu = env.model.nq, env.model.nv, env.model.nu
    print(f"Topology: nq = {nq}, nv = {nv}, nu = {nu} actuators (7 arm torques + 1 gripper)")

    # (2) Observations
    pos, quat = env.tcp_pose()
    state_no_vel = env.state(include_velocity=False)
    state_with_vel = env.state(include_velocity=True)
    print(f"TCP Pose: position = {np.round(pos, 3)}, quaternion = {np.round(quat, 3)}")
    print(f"Proprioception: {len(state_no_vel)} dims (position-only) vs {len(state_with_vel)} dims (with velocity)")

    # (3) Dual-camera rendering
    img_top = env.render("top", width=64, height=64)
    img_wrist = env.render("gripper/wrist", width=64, height=64)
    print(f"Rendered views: top = {img_top.shape}, wrist = {img_wrist.shape}")

    # (4) Rollout
    task = ("red_cube", "tray")
    env.reset(seed=42, task=task)
    print(f"Task: move {task[0]} to {task[1]}")

    success = False
    for t in range(STEPS):
        action = franka_expert_action(env)
        step_result = env.step(action)
        if step_result["success"]:
            success = True
            print(f"Goal achieved at step {t} (distance to tray = {step_result['dist_to_target'] * 1000:.1f} mm)")
            break

    if not success:
        print(f"Rollout finished: final distance to target = {step_result['dist_to_target'] * 1000:.1f} mm")

    env.close()
    print("7-DOF Franka VLA simulation interface verified.")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```
