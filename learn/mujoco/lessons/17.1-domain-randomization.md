## Core concept

Domain randomization tunes or trains a controller on a distribution of simulator parameters instead of one value, so that the real system, whose parameters are known only roughly, looks like one more draw from that distribution. Whether it helps depends on three things, and each needs a measurement rather than an assumption. The parameter change has to reach the simulation correctly. The comparison has to be against a controller tuned on nominal values, evaluated on held-out values that reach beyond the training range. And the outcome has to depend on the randomized parameter under the controller being tuned. This lesson tunes a pushing controller on nominal and on randomized puck mass and friction and evaluates both on a 5 × 5 grid of held-out values. It then repeats the comparison for an open-loop strike, whose outcome depends on friction directly, and adds a third option: measuring the friction before acting.

> [!established] What is randomized, and how
> `PushEnv.reset(options=...)` accepts `"puck_mass"` (kg; the inertia is scaled with the mass, `mj_setConst` recomputes the derived constants, and the state is reset) and `"friction"` (the sliding coefficient of both the puck and the floor). At equal priority MuJoCo gives a contact the larger of the two geoms' coefficients (measured here: puck 1.0 on floor 0.25 gives a contact friction of 1.0, and so does the reverse), so setting both sets the contact's. Values not given return to nominal: 0.2 kg and 0.5. Training draws mass from 0.1 to 0.4 kg and friction from 0.25 to 1.0 in every episode. The held-out grid is mass 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8 kg by friction 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, with the same 24 tasks in every cell for every controller.

## Mathematics

### (0) A mass change that does not reach the dynamics

Editing `model.body_mass` and `model.body_inertia` changes two fields, but MuJoCo also stores constants derived from them, computed by `mj_setConst` when the model is compiled. After doubling the puck's mass, `mj_setConst` changes six fields: `body_invweight0`, `dof_invweight0`, `dof_M0`, `body_subtreemass`, `cam_poscom0` and `light_poscom0` (the last two are reference positions for cameras and lights that track a centre of mass). Without that call, the push episodes diverge:

| Puck mass | Diverged, edited without `mj_setConst` | Diverged, through `reset(options={"puck_mass": m})` |
|---|---|---|
| 0.2 kg (nominal) | 0/10 | 0/10 |
| 0.4 kg | 10/10 | 0/10 |
| 0.8 kg | 10/10 | 0/10 |

Recomputing one field at a time at 0.4 kg isolates the cause: with only `body_subtreemass` recomputed no episode diverges (0/10), with only `dof_M0` 8/10 diverge, and with only either inverse weight all 10 do. Why a stale subtree mass destabilizes this step was not traced here; the rule does not depend on it. Call `mj_setConst` after editing any inertial parameter, then reset. MuJoCo reports the divergence as a warning (printed to stderr and counted in `data.warning`) and resets the state, time included, so a randomized training run with stale constants does not crash. It quietly trains on episodes that restart, which is why the script detects divergence by the clock running backwards.

### (1) to (3) A closed-loop pusher: randomization buys nothing measurable

The pusher has four parameters: push speed, a gain that slows it near the goal ($\min(v, k_\text{slow} d)$), push depth, and the lateral error it tolerates before repositioning behind the puck. The cross-entropy method tunes them on nominal dynamics and, separately, with mass and friction randomized in every episode (24 candidates, 8 episodes per candidate, 12 iterations). Nominal tuning ends at mean success 0.99 and randomized tuning at 0.88, where every candidate scored 7 of 8. The two parameter sets differ (push speed 0.107 against 0.181 m/s), and on the held-out grid both succeed almost everywhere:

| Mean success | Inside the randomized range | Outside it | Nominal cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| tuned on nominal | 1.00 | 0.99 | 1.00 |
| tuned with randomization | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

Over all 25 cells the nominal-tuned pusher fails 3 of 600 episodes (2 of them outside the range) and the randomized one fails 1 (inside it); single failures in cells of 24 tasks are too few to call a difference. The reason is physical. The pusher commands positions every 50 ms and the Cartesian impedance can apply up to about 40 N (2000 N/m times the 2 cm cap on how far the target may lead the tool). Sliding the heaviest, grippiest puck on the grid takes $\mu m g$ = 1.5 × 0.8 × 9.81 = 11.8 N, so the pusher is never force-limited, and the puck moves with it. Near the goal the speed is $k_\text{slow} d$, so the puck coasts a negligible distance when the pusher stops: from $d$ = 1 cm at the nominal gain, $v$ = 0.022 m/s and the coast at $\mu$ = 0.1 is $v^2 / 2\mu g \approx$ 0.24 mm. Feedback and a force margin make the outcome insensitive to mass and friction, so randomizing them has nothing to fix.

> [!recommendation] Measure sensitivity before randomizing
> Evaluate the nominal controller over held-out parameter values first. Where its success is flat, randomization has nothing to buy and costs training time (here the randomized tuning also scored lower during training, 0.88 against 0.99). Spend randomization on the parameters the outcome depends on, and check that every value in the range leaves the task feasible: here the largest sliding force on the grid, 11.8 N, is well below the 40 N the pusher can apply. A range that crosses a feasibility limit changes the task instead of the dynamics.

### (4) An open-loop strike: randomization moves the window

The strike launches the puck at $v = \sqrt{2\hat\mu g D}$ for a goal $D$ away and lets it slide. Under Coulomb friction the puck slides $v^2/(2\mu g) = D\,\hat\mu/\mu$, so it stops within the success radius $r$ = 3 cm only if

$$\left|\frac{\hat\mu}{\mu} - 1\right| \le \frac{r}{D}.$$

PushEnv's goals are 8 to 30 cm from the puck, so the tolerance on $\hat\mu/\mu$ is ±10% to ±37%, about ±20% at 15 cm. The simulator follows the formula: after a launch at 0.4 m/s the puck slides 81.2, 32.4, 16.1 and 8.2 mm at $\mu$ = 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 and 1.0, against 81.5, 32.6, 16.3 and 8.2 mm predicted.

$\hat\mu$ is chosen by grid search (0.05 to 1.5 in steps of 0.05) on 64 training tasks, once at the nominal friction and once with friction drawn from the randomized range. Nominal tuning picks 0.45 with a training success of 1.00 (the search keeps the first value that reaches the best score). Randomized tuning picks 0.7, the best any single value can do over 0.25 to 1.0, and that best is a training success of 0.44. On 24 held-out tasks per friction:

| True friction | 0.05 | 0.1 | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1.0 | 1.25 | 1.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\hat\mu$ 0.45 (chosen on nominal) | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 1.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| $\hat\mu$ 0.7 (chosen on randomized) | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 1.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| probe at 0.3 m/s, then strike | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.96 | 0.71 |
| oracle: $\hat\mu$ = true $\mu$ | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.54 |
| its largest tilt, median (degrees) | 0.1 | 0.2 | 0.2 | 0.6 | 1.3 | 1.1 | 3.9 | 116.1 |

Both single-value strikes succeed at one friction each; inside the randomized range their mean success is the same, 0.25. A controller that cannot observe the friction has one operating point, and training it on a distribution only chooses where that point sits. What extends the window is information about the parameter.

### Identification by a probe, and its operating range

The probe launches the puck at a known speed $v_0$, waits 1 s, and estimates $\hat\mu = v_0^2/(2gd)$ from the distance $d$ slid. Its accuracy depends on how far the puck slides:

| Probe speed | true 0.05 | 0.25 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.15 m/s: slide, estimate | 22.8 mm, 0.05 | 4.5 mm, 0.25 | 2.5 mm, 0.46 | 1.5 mm, 0.77 | 1.4 mm, 0.80 |
| 0.3 m/s: slide, estimate | 91.5 mm, 0.05 | 18.1 mm, 0.25 | 9.2 mm, 0.50 | 4.7 mm, 0.97 | 3.2 mm, 1.45 |

At 0.15 m/s and $\mu$ = 1.5 the formula predicts a 0.76 mm slide and the simulation gives 1.4 mm; at slides of a millimetre or two the extra distance dominates and the estimate saturates near 0.8. Doubling the probe speed quadruples the slide and keeps the estimate within 4% up to 1.5. Every measurement has an operating range like this, and it has to be measured, not assumed.

The last rows of the strike table separate estimation error from model error. Told the true friction, the strike succeeds at every friction up to 1.25 and at 0.54 at 1.5, below the probe's 0.71, so the failure at 1.5 does not come from the estimate. The puck stops sliding: the median of its largest tilt during a strike is 3.9° at 1.25 and 116° at 1.5. At the highest friction it tips over and tumbles, and $v^2/(2\mu g)$ no longer describes it. The physics check above stopped at 1.0; a model's range of validity needs the same check as an estimator's.

> [!recommendation] Randomize, adapt, or identify
> Randomization makes a controller insensitive to a parameter only if some single behaviour works across the range. Feedback can provide that (the pusher), an open-loop action cannot (the strike). When the outcome depends on a parameter the controller cannot observe, give it information: identify the parameter (the probe; Level 18), or let the policy infer it from its own history while it acts. Randomization is still needed for the second, because the policy has to see the range to learn how to infer within it.

"""Lesson 17.1: domain randomization for the push task, measured against held-out parameters (Level 17 checkpoint).

INPUT   mjcourse.envs.PushEnv (end-effector actions whose target leads the tool by at most 2 cm,
        episodes of 5 s, success judged where the puck ends)
PROCESS (0) a pitfall: the puck's mass changed in the model with and without mj_setConst, and
            with one derived field at a time recomputed;
        a parametric pushing controller (push speed, slowdown gain near the goal, push depth,
        lateral tolerance) tuned by the cross-entropy method twice:
        (1) on nominal dynamics (puck 0.2 kg, friction 0.5);
        (2) with puck mass drawn from 0.1-0.4 kg and friction from 0.25-1.0 in every episode;
        (3) both evaluated on a grid of mass and friction values reaching beyond the
            randomized range, 24 tasks per cell, the same tasks for both controllers;
        (4) an open-loop strike: the puck is launched toward the goal at v = sqrt(2 mu_hat g D)
            for its distance D and an assumed friction mu_hat, then slides; mu_hat chosen on
            nominal friction and on the randomized range; success against the true friction;
            first, the simulator's sliding distance against v^2 / (2 mu g); then a strike that
            first identifies the friction from a probe launch, after checking the probe's estimates;
            last, a strike told the true friction, which separates estimation error from model error
OUTPUT  printed tables

Run:  python examples/l17_1_domain_randomization.py          (about 3 minutes on 24 CPU cores)
"""

import math
import os
from multiprocessing import Pool

import mujoco
import numpy as np

from mjcourse.envs import PushEnv

FAST = os.environ.get("MJC_FAST") == "1"
R_PUCK, HALF, DT = 0.035, 0.008, 0.05
NOMINAL = {"puck_mass": 0.2, "friction": 0.5}
TRAIN_RANGE = {"puck_mass": (0.1, 0.4), "friction": (0.25, 1.0)}
GRID = {"puck_mass": (0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8), "friction": (0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5)}


def decode(theta: np.ndarray) -> dict:
    s = 1 / (1 + np.exp(-np.asarray(theta)))
    return {"speed": 0.05 + 0.25 * s[0], "slow_gain": 0.5 + 4.5 * s[1], "depth": 0.002 + 0.02 * s[2], "lateral": 0.005 + 0.03 * s[3]}


def pusher(p: dict, obs: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """Get behind the puck on its line to the goal, then push along that line, slowing near the goal."""
    tcp, puck, goal = obs[14:16], obs[17:19], obs[19:21]
    to_goal = goal - puck
    d = np.linalg.norm(to_goal)
    if d < 0.01:
        return np.zeros(2, np.float32)
    u = to_goal / d
    n = np.array([-u[1], u[0]])
    rel = tcp - puck
    along, lateral = rel @ u, rel @ n
    contact = -(R_PUCK + HALF)
    if along > contact + 0.015 or abs(lateral) > p["lateral"]:          # not behind the puck: reposition
        side = n * math.copysign(R_PUCK + HALF + 0.03, lateral if lateral != 0 else 1.0)
        target = puck + side if along > contact else puck - (R_PUCK + HALF + 0.03) * u
        speed = 0.3
    else:                                                                # push
        target = puck - (R_PUCK + HALF - p["depth"]) * u
        speed = min(p["speed"], p["slow_gain"] * d)
    step = target - tcp
    dist = np.linalg.norm(step)
    move = min(speed * DT, dist)
    return np.clip(step / max(dist, 1e-9) * move / 0.02, -1, 1).astype(np.float32)


_ENV = {}


def episode(job) -> float:
    """One episode: (theta, task seed, mass, friction) -> 1 success, 0 failure, nan if the simulation diverged.
    MuJoCo resets a diverging simulation to its initial state (time 0), so a clock that runs backwards, or a
    controller that fails at the reset pose, marks the episode."""
    theta, seed, mass, friction = job
    if "pusher" not in _ENV:
        _ENV["pusher"] = PushEnv(action_mode="ee_delta", terminate_on_success=False, max_lead=0.02)   # force at most about 40 N
    env, p = _ENV["pusher"], decode(theta)
    obs, _ = env.reset(seed=int(seed), options={"puck_mass": mass, "friction": friction})
    done, last_time = False, 0.0
    try:
        while not done:
            obs, _, terminated, truncated, info = env.step(pusher(p, obs))
            if env.data.time < last_time:
                return float("nan")
            last_time, done = env.data.time, terminated or truncated
    except np.linalg.LinAlgError:
        return float("nan")
    return float(info["distance"] < env.success_radius)


STALE = ("body_invweight0", "dof_invweight0", "dof_M0", "body_subtreemass")    # what mj_setConst changes after a mass edit


def stale_constants(mass: float, episodes: int, repair=None) -> int:
    """Diverged episodes when the puck's mass and inertia are edited directly. repair=None takes the
    environment's path (edit, mj_setConst, reset); otherwise only the listed derived fields are recomputed."""
    env = PushEnv(action_mode="ee_delta", terminate_on_success=False, max_lead=0.02)
    m, puck, diverged = env.model, env.puck, 0
    nominal_mass, nominal_inertia = m.body_mass[puck], m.body_inertia[puck].copy()
    m.body_mass[puck], m.body_inertia[puck] = mass, nominal_inertia * mass / nominal_mass
    mujoco.mj_setConst(m, env.data)
    correct = {k: getattr(m, k).copy() for k in STALE}                # the constants for the edited mass
    m.body_mass[puck], m.body_inertia[puck] = nominal_mass, nominal_inertia
    mujoco.mj_setConst(m, env.data)
    for seed in range(episodes):
        if repair is None:
            obs, _ = env.reset(seed=seed, options={"puck_mass": mass})
        else:                                                      # the same edit made directly
            obs, _ = env.reset(seed=seed)
            m.body_mass[puck], m.body_inertia[puck] = mass, nominal_inertia * mass / nominal_mass
            for k in repair:
                getattr(m, k)[:] = correct[k]
        try:
            last, done = 0.0, False
            while not done:
                obs, _, terminated, truncated, _ = env.step(pusher(decode(np.zeros(4)), obs))
                if env.data.time < last:
                    diverged += 1
                    break
                last, done = env.data.time, terminated or truncated
        except np.linalg.LinAlgError:
            diverged += 1
        m.body_mass[puck], m.body_inertia[puck] = nominal_mass, nominal_inertia
        mujoco.mj_setConst(m, env.data)                            # leave the model as it was
    env.close()
    return diverged


def cem(pool, randomize: bool, seed: int, iterations: int, population: int, episodes: int) -> np.ndarray:
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    mean, std = np.zeros(4), np.full(4, 1.5)
    for it in range(iterations):
        thetas = rng.normal(mean, std, (population, 4))
        tasks = rng.integers(0, 10**6, episodes)                          # shared tasks within an iteration
        if randomize:
            params = [(rng.uniform(*TRAIN_RANGE["puck_mass"]), rng.uniform(*TRAIN_RANGE["friction"])) for _ in range(episodes)]
        else:
            params = [(NOMINAL["puck_mass"], NOMINAL["friction"])] * episodes
        jobs = [(th, t, m, f) for th in thetas for t, (m, f) in zip(tasks, params)]
        scores = np.nan_to_num(np.array(pool.map(episode, jobs))).reshape(population, episodes).mean(axis=1)
        elite = thetas[np.argsort(scores)[-max(2, population // 5):]]
        mean, std = elite.mean(axis=0), elite.std(axis=0) + 0.05
        if it in (0, iterations - 1):
            print(f"    iteration {it + 1:>2}: mean success {scores.mean():.2f}, best {scores.max():.2f}")
    return mean


def strike(job) -> tuple[float, float, float]:
    """Launch the puck toward the goal at sqrt(2 mu_hat g D) and let it slide for 3 s with the arm holding
    its start pose: (final distance to the goal, distance the puck travelled, largest tilt of its axis
    from vertical in degrees, sampled every 50 ms)."""
    mu_hat, seed, friction = job
    if "strike" not in _ENV:
        _ENV["strike"] = PushEnv(action_mode="ee_delta", terminate_on_success=False, max_episode_steps=60)
    env = _ENV["strike"]
    env.reset(seed=int(seed), options={"friction": friction})
    start, goal = env.puck_xy(), env.goal_xy()
    distance = np.linalg.norm(goal - start)
    dof = env.model.jnt_dofadr[env.model.joint("puck").id]
    env.data.qvel[dof:dof + 2] = math.sqrt(2 * mu_hat * 9.81 * distance) * (goal - start) / distance
    tilt = 0.0
    for _ in range(env.max_episode_steps):
        env.step(np.zeros(2, np.float32))
        tilt = max(tilt, math.degrees(math.acos(np.clip(env.data.xmat[env.puck][8], -1.0, 1.0))))
    return float(np.linalg.norm(env.puck_xy() - goal)), float(np.linalg.norm(env.puck_xy() - start)), tilt


PROBE = 0.3                                          # probe launch speed (m/s)


def probe_estimate(speed: float, friction: float, seed: int = 3) -> tuple[float, float]:
    """Launch toward the goal at `speed`, wait 1 s: (distance slid, friction estimated as v^2 / (2 g d))."""
    if "strike" not in _ENV:
        _ENV["strike"] = PushEnv(action_mode="ee_delta", terminate_on_success=False, max_episode_steps=60)
    env = _ENV["strike"]
    env.reset(seed=seed, options={"friction": friction})
    dof = env.model.jnt_dofadr[env.model.joint("puck").id]
    start, goal = env.puck_xy(), env.goal_xy()
    env.data.qvel[dof:dof + 2] = speed * (goal - start) / np.linalg.norm(goal - start)
    for _ in range(20):
        env.step(np.zeros(2, np.float32))
    slid = float(np.linalg.norm(env.puck_xy() - start))
    return slid, speed**2 / (2 * 9.81 * max(slid, 1e-4))


def probe_then_strike(job) -> float:
    """Launch at PROBE m/s, wait 1 s, estimate mu from the slide (v0^2 / (2 g d)), then strike from where
    the puck stopped. Returns the final distance to the goal."""
    _, seed, friction = job
    if "strike" not in _ENV:
        _ENV["strike"] = PushEnv(action_mode="ee_delta", terminate_on_success=False, max_episode_steps=60)
    env = _ENV["strike"]
    env.reset(seed=int(seed), options={"friction": friction})
    dof = env.model.jnt_dofadr[env.model.joint("puck").id]

    def launch(speed: float, steps: int) -> float:
        start, goal = env.puck_xy(), env.goal_xy()
        env.data.qvel[dof:dof + 2] = speed * (goal - start) / np.linalg.norm(goal - start)
        for _ in range(steps):
            env.step(np.zeros(2, np.float32))
        return float(np.linalg.norm(env.puck_xy() - start))

    slid = launch(PROBE, 20)
    mu_hat = PROBE**2 / (2 * 9.81 * max(slid, 1e-4))
    launch(math.sqrt(2 * mu_hat * 9.81 * np.linalg.norm(env.goal_xy() - env.puck_xy())), 40)
    return float(np.linalg.norm(env.puck_xy() - env.goal_xy()))


MU_GRID = (0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.25, 1.5)


def strike_success(pool, mu_hat: float, frictions, tasks) -> float:
    jobs = [(mu_hat, t, f) for f, t in zip(frictions, tasks)]
    return float(np.mean([d < 0.03 for d, _, _ in pool.map(strike, jobs)]))


def part4(pool, per_cell: int) -> dict:
    """The strike: physics check, then mu_hat chosen by grid search on nominal and on randomized friction."""
    print("  sliding distance after a launch at 0.4 m/s: simulated against v^2 / (2 mu g)")
    for mu in (0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0):
        # mu_hat chosen so that the launch speed is 0.4 m/s for this task's distance
        env = PushEnv(action_mode="ee_delta", max_episode_steps=60)
        env.reset(seed=3)
        d = np.linalg.norm(env.goal_xy() - env.puck_xy())
        env.close()
        _, travelled, _ = strike((0.16 / (2 * 9.81 * d), 3, mu))
        print(f"    mu {mu:<5} simulated {1000 * travelled:6.1f} mm, predicted {1000 * 0.16 / (2 * mu * 9.81):6.1f} mm")
    print("  friction estimated by a probe launch (one task), at two probe speeds:")
    for speed in (0.15, PROBE):
        cells = []
        for mu in (0.05, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5):
            slid, mu_hat = probe_estimate(speed, mu)
            cells.append(f"{mu}: {1000 * slid:.1f} mm -> {mu_hat:.2f}")
        print(f"    {speed} m/s   " + ";  ".join(cells))
    rng = np.random.default_rng(17)
    train_tasks = rng.integers(0, 10**6, 64)
    candidates = np.round(np.arange(0.05, 1.51, 0.05), 2)
    chosen = {}
    for name, frictions in (("nominal", np.full(64, NOMINAL["friction"])), ("randomized", rng.uniform(*TRAIN_RANGE["friction"], 64))):
        scores = [strike_success(pool, c, frictions, train_tasks) for c in candidates]
        chosen[name] = float(candidates[int(np.argmax(scores))])
        print(f"  chosen on {name} friction: mu_hat {chosen[name]} (training success {max(scores):.2f})")
    tasks = np.random.default_rng(1718).integers(0, 10**6, per_cell)
    print(f"  success against the true friction, {per_cell} held-out tasks each:")
    print(f"    {'true friction':<34}" + "".join(f"{m:>7}" for m in MU_GRID))
    table = {}
    for name, mu_hat in chosen.items():
        table[name] = [strike_success(pool, mu_hat, np.full(per_cell, m), tasks) for m in MU_GRID]
        print(f"    {f'mu_hat {mu_hat} (chosen on {name})':<34}" + "".join(f"{v:>7.2f}" for v in table[name]))
    table["probe"] = [float(np.mean([d < 0.03 for d in pool.map(probe_then_strike, [(0, t, m) for t in tasks])])) for m in MU_GRID]

    print(f"    {'  its largest tilt (deg, median)':<34}" + "".join(f"{np.median([a for _, _, a in o]):>7.1f}" for o in oracle))

def grid(pool, theta, tasks) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
    """Success per cell (diverged episodes count as failures) and the number of diverged episodes per cell."""
    jobs = [(theta, t, m, f) for m in GRID["puck_mass"] for f in GRID["friction"] for t in tasks]
    out = np.array(pool.map(episode, jobs)).reshape(len(GRID["puck_mass"]), len(GRID["friction"]), len(tasks))
    return np.nan_to_num(out).mean(axis=2), np.isnan(out).sum(axis=2)

## Debugging

**Randomized mass makes the simulation diverge, or makes episodes restart.** Derived constants are stale. Call `mj_setConst` after editing mass or inertia, then reset; check `data.warning` or the clock for silent resets.

**Randomized training scores lower than nominal training, and held-out performance is the same.** The outcome may not depend on what is randomized. Evaluate the nominal controller across the held-out values before concluding anything.

**The randomized controller is best on average and fails at every individual value.** It has no information about the parameter; a single action is a compromise. Add an observation that carries the parameter, or identify it.

**A measurement of a parameter works in one range and not another.** Check its operating range on simulated data where the truth is known, as the probe table does.

**A rest check ends a rollout early and changes the results.** In the lab, stopping a slide when the puck's $x$ velocity first fell below $10^{-6}$ m/s changed the probe's success at friction 1.5 from 0.45 to 0.90: a puck whose sliding velocity has reached zero can still be rocking. Test every degree of freedom.

## Research connection

> [!research] Randomization and adaptation
> Tobin et al. (arXiv:1703.06907) randomized rendering and trained an object detector only on simulated images with non-realistic random textures; on real images it located objects to 1.5 cm. Peng et al. (arXiv:1710.06537) randomized dynamics for an object-pushing task with a robot arm and report policies "capable of adapting to very different dynamics"; read their design-decision study for what the policy must observe to adapt, the question this lesson's strike raises. Kumar et al.'s RMA (arXiv:2107.04034) makes the adaptation explicit, with a base policy and an adaptation module, both trained in simulation. When reading such results, check whether the evaluation reaches beyond the training range and whether a nominal-tuned baseline was evaluated on the same values (inference from this lesson): without those two, a randomization gain cannot be told apart from a controller that was insensitive all along. The reading list's sim-to-real section has more.
